# paper

A paper article reports on one research paper: what it claims, how far the
evidence supports that claim, and where the claim stops. Its subject is the
paper, never the experience of reading about it.

Open with the source. The `abstract` section carries the paper card (title,
authors, venue, link) and the abstract verbatim, so the original's own words are
on record before any commentary.

Rebuild the argument in your own words, your own examples, and the order that
teaches best. Do not assume the original's order does that. Define each concept
at the point the argument first uses it. Anchor the moments the argument turns
on in the citations themselves, with an honest `data-nb-locator` and
`data-nb-note` on the cite, and quote the original's exact sentence in a note
where the exact wording is the evidence.

Weigh the evidence as a reviewer would: what was measured, on what, and what
would count as the result failing to generalize. Say plainly where an evaluation
is weak.

The focal paper's claims are the subject. Bring in another source only where it
changes the interpretation, and never to replace the paper or pad the list.
Place the paper among the work it builds on and the work that disagrees with it,
and state a verdict on its claims before the sources.
