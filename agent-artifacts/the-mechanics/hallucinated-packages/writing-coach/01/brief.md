# writing-coach brief: the-mechanics/hallucinated-packages (01)

Assignment: a lesson for The Mechanics desk on why AI coding assistants import
libraries that do not exist, why the same fake name recurs, and the
slopsquatting supply-chain risk that follows. The reader is smart and widely read
but not living in a codebase; they have probably seen an assistant invent an
import. The piece works backward from the behavior to its cause and lands on
where the weakness lives today. Register: plain, concrete, explains a mechanism
without code dumps, in the spirit of press/editorial's "write like Matt Yglesias
explaining something he understands well."

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (house standard, slop and headline rules, press voice,
  lesson identity, The Mechanics prompt)

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Study writers who explain how software systems behave and how security failures
work, clearly, to a serious general reader (the field rates them for making a
mechanism legible, not for hot takes). Pull passages that show how to trace a
surprising behavior back to an ordinary cause, and how to explain a security
risk soberly without either alarm or shrug. Quote for voice, not substance;
avoid passages that would hand the writer this lesson's own findings.
