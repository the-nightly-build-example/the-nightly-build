Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md  (house standard, paper voice, the-mechanics series prompt)

Output: agent-artifacts/the-mechanics/glitch-tokens/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: an explanatory lesson that answers "how does it actually do
that" for one strange, well-documented behavior — a language model breaking when
asked to handle a few odd tokens like " SolidGoldMagikarp" — by working backward to
its cause in how the tokenizer's vocabulary is built separately from the model's
training. The lesson names real parts of the system in plain words and marks what
is settled and what is open. No code. Register: a serious daily course in how AI
works, written like Matt Yglesias explaining something he understands well — plain
claims, concrete stakes, no fuss. The reader is smart, widely read, and has no
time in a codebase.

Study writers who take a small, concrete, slightly uncanny technical glitch and
walk a general reader down to the real mechanism a step at a time, without
mystifying it or overselling the weirdness. Show how such writers use a single
running example to carry an explanation and stay precise about what is known
versus guessed.
