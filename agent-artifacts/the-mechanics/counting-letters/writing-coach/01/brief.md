# writing-coach brief: the-mechanics/counting-letters (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md  (house standard, paper voice, the-mechanics series prompt)

Output: agent-artifacts/the-mechanics/counting-letters/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: an explanatory lesson that answers "how does it actually do
that" for one behavior everyone has seen — a chatbot miscounting the letters in a
word (the "how many r's in strawberry" miss) — by working backward to its cause
in how the model reads text. The lesson names real parts of the system (the
tokenizer, byte-pair encoding) in plain words and marks what is settled and what
is open. No code. Register: a serious daily course in how AI works, written like
Matt Yglesias explaining something he understands well — plain claims, concrete
stakes, no fuss. The reader is smart, widely read, and has no time in a codebase.

Study writers who take a small, concrete, slightly comic technical failure and
walk a general reader down to the real mechanism a step at a time, without either
mystifying it or drowning it in jargon. Show how such writers use a single
running example to carry an explanation, and how they stay precise about what is
known versus guessed.
