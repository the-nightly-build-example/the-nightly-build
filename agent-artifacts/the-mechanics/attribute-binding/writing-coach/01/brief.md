# writing-coach brief: the-mechanics/attribute-binding (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (artifact root) — house standard, paper voice, The Mechanics series prompt.

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: a teaching lesson that starts from a behavior anyone who uses
an image generator has seen (adjectives and positions landing on the wrong
objects) and works backward, step by step, to the mechanism inside a
text-to-image system. The domain is how neural image generators work, explained
for a smart non-programmer. No code. Register: a serious daily course, plain and
concrete, patient with a mechanism, never breezy.

Study writers the field rates who explain how a machine-learning system works to
non-specialists by reasoning backward from a visible behavior to its cause, one
step at a time, and who are honest about where the explanation runs out into open
research. Show how they introduce one technical part at a time in plain words, how
they use a small concrete example to make a step land, and how they mark the line
between settled engineering and open question. Prefer writers who explain vision
or language models well; avoid hype-driven or SEO explainers. Do not quote
passages that carry this lesson's own findings about attribute binding; quote for
the writing.

This piece has to make the reader able to say why the red goes on the wrong
object, not just that it does. Passages that build a causal chain the reader can
follow, and passages that admit an open question cleanly, are the most useful.
