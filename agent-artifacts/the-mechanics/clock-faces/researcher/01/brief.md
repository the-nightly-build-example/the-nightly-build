# researcher brief: the-mechanics/clock-faces (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — subject, angle, and source obligations

Output: evidence.md

Assemble the evidence for four mechanism steps (see commission). Establish
firsthand, with locators:
- The behavior, documented: image generators default clocks/watches to ~10:10 and
  fail on a requested time. Find the strongest documented evidence — a study, a
  systematic test, or a reputable demonstration. Record the exact prompt/model/
  date where a source gives it. Reproducible anecdote is secondary; a measured
  test is primary to its own finding.
- The reading side: vision-language models reading analog clocks. Find at least
  one research paper that tests this and reports accuracy; record the numbers and
  models with locators.
- The diffusion mechanism: a primary statement that a diffusion generator models
  the training data distribution / produces samples matching training statistics
  (cite the objective as the source states it). Keep it to what the reader needs
  to see that "renders the most likely appearance" is a real description, not a
  metaphor.
- The 10:10 convention in training imagery: a primary establishing that watches
  and clocks are conventionally photographed at ~10:10 (a watch brand's own
  statement, a horology/marketing primary, or a documented analysis of watch
  photos). If a dataset-level analysis of clock images exists, record it.
Contradictions to search for: cases where a newer model DOES render a requested
time (tool use, planning, or targeted training), and any claim that the cause is
something other than the dataset prior. Record them in full, with what would
settle the settled-vs-open line.
Meet counts (min 8; primary >=4; secondary >=1) with sources that carry the
argument, never padding. If four genuine primaries are not available, say so
plainly and record exactly which chain step is thin. Confirm every URL resolves
to the source's own page.
