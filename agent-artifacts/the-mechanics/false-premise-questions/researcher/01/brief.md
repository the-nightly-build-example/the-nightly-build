# researcher brief: the-mechanics/false-premise-questions (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — the subject, angle, required contribution, and source floor
Output: ./evidence.md

This round's focus, from the commission: source the causal chain and, above all,
the open part. Find the primary research that (1) defines and measures how models
answer questions with false premises or questionable assumptions, with rates tied
to named models and datasets; (2) supports the mechanism claims — that
premise-corrections are rare in training data and that a next-token predictor
conditions on presupposed content; and (3) shows where current models and any
fixes (prompting, fine-tuning, refusal-aware training) land, so the settled-versus-
open line is sourced, not asserted. Record contradictory findings: where a study
shows models *can* catch premises, that belongs in the record for the open
section. A concrete, real false-premise example the writer can use is useful, but
verify it reproduces or attribute it to the study that reported it.

Run-environment note: outbound HTTPS is proxied; arXiv and ACL Anthology are
reachable. A 403 or paywall is gated, not dead — record the document's canonical
page as the URL.
