# writer draft-handoff: the-mechanics/thinking-out-loud (01)

## Original work
This lesson assembles the scattered evidence into one backward causal chain
(observed behavior -> bounded compute per token / TC0 -> serial computation
across generated tokens -> conditioning on its own output) and stages it on a
worked 837 x 649 multiplication the reader watches resolve one written line at a
time, while holding the proven compute result and the unresolved faithfulness
question at two audibly different confidence levels.

## Notes for the editor
- Precision requirement 1 (filler / free compute) is handled in
  "Where writing the steps stops helping": the content-independent compute
  result is attributed to theory and to Pfau's purpose-trained models, and set
  against Lanham's deployed-model finding of no gain from filler, so the piece
  never implies real models get computation from arbitrary padding.
- Precision requirement 2 (faithfulness) is handled in "The printed steps might
  not be the computation": marked open, tied to Turpin and Lanham, and
  explicitly scoped to older/smaller models and planted-bias setups rather than
  today's frontier reasoning traces, with a stated condition that would settle
  it. No lean toward an answer.
- Furniture: a stat strip carries the two behavior numbers in the opener and a
  table performs the multiplication. I deliberately captured no source asset:
  the argument spends the multiplication's step-by-step structure, which the
  table performs directly, more than it spends any benchmark figure the evidence
  offered.
