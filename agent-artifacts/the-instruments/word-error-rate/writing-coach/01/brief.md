# writing-coach brief: the-instruments/word-error-rate (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md  (house standard, paper voice, the-instruments series prompt)

Output: agent-artifacts/the-instruments/word-error-rate/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: an explanatory lesson that teaches how one AI measurement is
made and what it can and cannot support. The measurement is Word Error Rate, the
speech-recognition score behind "human parity" claims. The lesson explains where
the number comes from (aligning a transcript to a reference and counting edits)
and shows a real case where it misled people. Register: a serious daily course in
how AI works, written like Matt Yglesias explaining something he understands well
— plain claims, concrete stakes, no fuss. The reader is smart, widely read, keeps
meeting AI performance claims they cannot check, and has no time in a codebase.

Study writers who explain how a metric or a measurement is actually constructed to
a general but serious reader, who are precise with numbers, and who can show why a
figure that looks authoritative rests on choices the reader never sees. Show how
such writers keep a quantitative point concrete without turning the prose into a
spec sheet.
