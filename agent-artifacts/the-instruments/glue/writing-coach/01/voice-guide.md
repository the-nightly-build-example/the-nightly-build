# Voice guide: the-instruments/glue (01)

## Directive

Write in the register of someone who has taken the number apart on the bench and
is walking a smart, non-technical reader through the parts. The authority comes
from exactness about procedure, so stay exact: name each step's actor, its input,
and the metric it produces in ordinary words, and never blur a procedure to
smooth a sentence. When a figure is really a construct, set out its ingredients
before you quote its size, so the reader meets the number already knowing what it
is made of.

The reader is being handed a tool to audit a public claim, not a verdict to
repeat. Write as a colleague thinking alongside them. Grant the tempting reading
its due before you complicate it: state plainly what the score seems to show,
then show what it actually rests on. The correction lands harder for being fair.

Hold a skeptical stance that never curdles into cynicism or smugness. The people
who built the benchmark did careful work, and the people who over-read it were
not conned. Credit the achievement and the honest intent, then show that the
failure is structural. A sneer at either party is a sentence to cut.

The move that carries this piece is showing rather than asserting the gap between
the score and the thing it is taken to measure. Put a concrete instance in front
of the reader and let them watch the two come apart, the way you would run a
control and let the result speak. Do not tell the reader that a number can hide a
shortcut; build the small case where they see the shortcut pass the test.

## Licenses

form: the checkpoint question
move: Rogers and Mitchell each halt at the moment the obvious reading looks
  settled and put it to the reader as a short question ("Or do they?", "Pretty
  good, right?"), then spend the next lines showing the reading was incomplete.
bar: it marks a real turn where the sentences that follow overturn the reading it
  names, and it earns its answer inside the piece. A question that only softens a
  claim the writer will not stand behind (Betteridge) is cut.

form: the staged reveal
move: let the reader reach the tempting conclusion from the evidence first, then
  expose the trick that produced it, so understanding arrives as something they
  watched happen rather than were told. Mitchell walks the reader into a result
  before showing the control that dissolves it.
bar: the withholding is short and in service of exposing a mechanism, and the
  payoff changes what the reader understands. A hold kept for suspense, or a
  reveal that only restates what preceded it, is an unearned punchline and is cut.

form: dry understatement drawn from the material
move: Rogers lets the subject's own facts carry the wit, deflating a grand claim
  by stating plainly what sits under it. The humor is in the accuracy, not in a
  joke laid over it.
bar: the wit rises from a fact already on the page and a lay reader gets it with
  no inside knowledge. At most a light touch, never relief imported from outside
  the subject, and never at a named person's expense.

## Anna Rogers, "How the Transformers broke NLP leaderboards"
Source: https://hackingsemantics.xyz/2019/leaderboards/
Craft:
- cadence: short emphatic turns set against longer explanatory runs; fragments
  like "Or do they?" reset the rhythm and pull the reader back to attention.
- argument: a problem-solution arc that loads tension before releasing it; she
  states the seemingly neutral format, then uncovers its hidden costs one layer
  at a time, and withholds the remedy until the reader feels why it is needed.
- evidence: specific costs deployed sparingly for weight (a compute bill, a
  marginal point of gain over the prior model), each framed as a barrier to
  participation rather than a mere expense, and placed late so it lands.
- stance: generous, not cynical; she credits the underlying work as a real
  achievement while faulting the incentive structure, reserving frustration for
  systems rather than people.
- notice: the category error others miss, that more data and compute producing a
  new high is not itself a research finding, and that the leaderboard rewards a
  blend of factors no reader can disentangle.
- diction: plain and slightly wry, technical terms sitting beside conversational
  phrasing so both insider and outsider are admitted without condescension.
- reader: cast as co-investigator; "how many of us actually look for those
  details?" makes the reader complicit and curious rather than lectured.
- the important move: she embeds the counterargument inside her own argument,
  conceding the reasonable case ("even inevitable") and then showing its
  incompleteness from within, so the piece reads as thinking in progress rather
  than prosecution.

## Melanie Mitchell, "On Evaluating Cognitive Capabilities in Machines (and Other 'Alien' Intelligences)"
Source: https://aiguide.substack.com/p/on-evaluating-cognitive-capabilities
Craft:
- cadence: declaratives alternate with short conversational checkpoints ("Pretty
  good, right?") that invite the reader to hold a provisional belief before it is
  tested; parallel structure carries the longer procedural sentences.
- argument: nested evidence, moving abstract principle to concrete case to
  mechanism to implication, with the hard concepts delayed until an earlier plain
  example has made them feel earned.
- evidence: she demonstrates rather than asserts, walking the reader through the
  actual failure data and its human comparison so the reader sees the result come
  apart; a control experiment (the permuted-alphabet variant, the Clever Hans
  case) does the puncturing.
- stance: rigorous sympathy; over-readings are treated as overgeneralizations
  driven by structural incentives, not as deception, and that fairness sharpens
  the critique.
- notice: that a headline average can be driven by tasks that reward a surface
  cue, and that the honest test is whether the result survives a change that
  should not matter if the model understood.
- diction: metaphor kept tangible and precise; jargon such as construct validity
  is introduced only after a story has made the idea concrete.
- reader: addressed as an intellectual peer; a parenthetical that flags where the
  logic will bend ("it doesn't quite turn out that way") builds a quiet intimacy
  without narrating the piece.
- the important move: the performative demonstration; instead of claiming a
  benchmark obscures a mechanism, she stages the moment it happens, so the reader
  watches the failure occur and does not have to take her word for it.

## Raji, Bender, Paullada, Denton, Hanna, "AI and the Everything in the Whole Wide World Benchmark"
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15366
Craft:
- cadence: measured academic prose, but it earns clarity by translating each
  technical claim into a plain question the reader can hold ("what does the score
  really measure?").
- argument: disaggregation as structure; take the single aggregate score, break
  it into its parts, and let the internal contradictions among the parts show
  that the one number was standing in for many different things.
- evidence: task-level analysis and competing definitions laid side by side, then
  tested against the data, so the claim of generality is dismantled with the
  benchmark's own results rather than with assertion.
- stance: skeptical of specific claims while keeping faith in benchmarking's
  purpose; the aggregation is framed as a solvable measurement problem, never as
  fraud.
- notice: the gap between what the score's builders intended and what the score
  actually captures, and that reasonable people using the same number mean
  different things by it.
- diction: construct validity rendered without jargon, as "we agree on the score,
  not on what it means"; precision offered to the reader rather than withheld
  behind terminology.
- reader: trusted to handle exactness; the paper explains the machinery plainly
  and expects the reader to follow the disaggregation.
- the important move: proving that disagreement was hiding inside the aggregate
  all along, so the number's claimed universality collapses on inspection rather
  than under attack.
