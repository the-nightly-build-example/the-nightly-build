This article: a lesson on how METR's "task time horizon" number is built — human-
timed tasks, a 50%-success threshold, a logistic fit, a doubling trend across
release dates — and what it can and cannot support. The reader is smart, widely
read, new to how a benchmark or a trend line is constructed. The craft challenge
is making a statistical construction (a success-vs-time curve and a threshold read
off it) intuitive, and separating a real measurement from the timeline
extrapolation laid on top of it, evenly and without either hype or dismissal.

Study writers the field rates for explaining a metric or a forecast honestly to a
general reader — people who can make a fitted curve intuitive and who separate a
measurement from the story told about it. At least three exemplars, read in full,
quoted for how they explain a construction and how they check an extrapolation.
Quote for the writing, not for METR facts.
