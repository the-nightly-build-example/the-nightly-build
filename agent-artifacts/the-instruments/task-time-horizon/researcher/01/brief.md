This lesson teaches how METR's task time horizon is produced and where it
misleads. Build the evidence record from primary documents. Primary anchor:
Kwa et al. 2025, "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks" (METR).

Source obligations: minimum 8 sources, primary >= 4, secondary >= 1. Every URL
resolves; cite only what you read.

Establish, each with a primary citation and exact figures:
  - The construction step by step: how human completion times are measured, the
    task suite(s) and their size/domains, how model success is scored, the
    logistic fit of success vs human-time, and how the 50% horizon is read off it.
  - The headline figures: the reported horizon length(s), the doubling period and
    its stated uncertainty range, and the set of models/dates in the trend.
  - What the paper says it can and cannot support, in its own words (the 50%
    threshold vs reliability, task-suite dependence, external validity limits).
  - A documented instance of the number being over-read: a public forecast or
    claim that treats the 50% horizon as reliable capability or extrapolates the
    doubling to a dated milestone. Prefer a primary statement of that claim.
  - Any primary critique, replication, or methodological caveat (e.g. on task-suite
    representativeness, human-baseline measurement, or the extrapolation).

Keep primary distinct from secondary reporting. Flag any figure you could not
verify (note if the live results page is client-rendered and unreadable) and mark
what is settled vs contested.
