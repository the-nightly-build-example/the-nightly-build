This round's focus: document, from the sources that own them, exactly how each
"Codeforces rating" claim was produced and under what conditions.
- Codeforces' own account of its rating: that it is Elo-style, contest-driven,
  relative to a live human field; the percentile bands and title thresholds;
  update rules in plain terms.
- AlphaCode (Li et al., Science 2022, and the DeepMind write-up): the exact
  percentile/rating claim, the number of samples/attempts used to reach it, and
  the conditions (simulated participation in past contests). Record the precise
  figure and its denominator.
- OpenAI o-series (o1 and/or o3 system cards or announcements): the reported
  Codeforces Elo/rating, and every stated condition — estimated vs live, number of
  attempts, whether ratings came from a simulated field. Record the exact numbers.
- At least one documented case where such a rating/percentile misled: overstatement
  of AlphaCode's standing, a contamination finding (contest problems present in
  training data inflating a result), or an "estimated rating" quoted without its
  qualifier. Establish the misleading and, where possible, its cost or distortion
  from a source in a position to know.
- Search for what breaks the angle: a lab that graded the model through
  Codeforces' real judge under contest-like limits, or evidence the estimates
  track live performance well. Record under Contradictions.
Classify each source primary/secondary by authorship and stake. Confirm every URL
resolves to the document's own page. Preserve any full rating/percentile series
that a chart could use.
