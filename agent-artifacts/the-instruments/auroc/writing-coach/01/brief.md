# writing-coach brief: the-instruments/auroc (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (artifact root) — house standard, paper voice, The Instruments series prompt.

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: a teaching lesson that explains one measurement, AUROC (area
under the ROC curve), to a smart non-programmer, and shows one real case where a
high AUROC misled the people relying on it. The domain is applied statistics and
medical-AI evaluation, explained for a lay-but-literate reader. Register: a
serious daily course, plain and concrete, unhurried with a definition, never
breezy.

Study writers the field rates who explain a statistical measure to non-specialists
and then show it failing in the real world without either mystifying the math or
sliding into innumeracy. Show how they build an intuition for a probability or a
rate from a small concrete case, how they keep a ratio honest about its
denominator, and how they narrate a measurement that looked fine and a system that
did not work. Statistics and medicine both have first-rate explanatory writers;
prefer them to generic data-science content. Do not quote passages that carry
this lesson's own findings about AUROC or the Epic sepsis model; quote for the
writing.

This piece has to make a reader who fears math unafraid of one number, then make
them skeptical of it. Passages that teach a formula through a worked example, and
passages that hold "the score was high" against "the tool failed," are the most
useful.
