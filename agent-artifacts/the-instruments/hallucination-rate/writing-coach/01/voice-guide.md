# Voice guide: the-instruments/hallucination-rate (01)

Write as an investigator reconstructing a machine, not a critic warning a
buyer. The reader is a competent peer being walked through how a number is
built, then shown where its meaning changes. Hold two attitudes at once: full
respect for the people who built the metric, and exact precision about the seam
where what it measures stops matching what it is quoted to mean. That double
posture, not the plain correct explanation, is what earns this piece past the
house default.

Three moves should change sentences here. First, do not state the gap between
measure and claim in the abstract when you can run one concrete instance through
the pipeline until the reader sees the gap open. Second, before you name any
limit of a design choice, give the genuine reason a builder made it, in terms
that builder would own. Third, when you credit what the number does support,
mark in the same breath the exact edge where support ends, both halves true.
Keep every term of the pipeline named once and reused; a synonym reads as a new
part of the machine.

## Licenses

form: a worked trace — one real instance run step by step through the pipeline
move: Ritchie runs two named populations through the averaging so the headline's
  loss becomes visible arithmetic rather than a claimed defect. Deploy once, at
  the hinge where the measure and the everyday reading of it diverge.
bar:  the trace must show a specific place a reasonable reader would have guessed
  wrong. If the general statement of the gap survives deleting the trace with no
  loss, the trace is decoration and goes.

form: the builder's reason before the limit
move: Levine establishes why a structure is genuinely sound before "not totally
  safe," so the flaw lands on something the reader already respects. Deploy
  wherever the piece is about to fault a construction choice.
bar:  the reason given must be one the metric's designers would actually endorse,
  not a weak version set up to fall. One sentence passes only if a builder would
  nod at it before reaching the "but."

form: grant-and-bound — a sentence that credits the number's real reach, then
  marks the precise edge where that reach ends
move: Levine's "That is a reasonably safe stream of cash flows. Not totally
  safe—" grants the true thing, then bounds it with a real, specific limit.
bar:  both halves must be independently true and load-bearing, and the boundary
  must be one a supporter of the number would concede. If the first half states a
  reading nobody holds, it is the banned hedged contrast wearing a grant, and it
  is cut.

## Hannah Ritchie, "The Living Planet Index: what does it really mean?"
Source: https://ourworldindata.org/living-planet-index-decline
Craft:
- cadence: short declaratives that puncture ("But they are all wrong.")
  alternating with measured elaboration; the correction arrives in a handful of
  words, then the explanation follows it.
- argument: three moves — show what the headline claims, run a concrete case the
  claim cannot survive, then state what the metric actually measures. The worked
  case is the hinge and comes before the general principle.
- evidence: one worked numeric example over any abstract statement; the reader
  computes the distortion and feels it before it is named.
- stance: professional respect for the metric's makers, whose choices she
  explains rather than mocks; gentleness toward the misreaders ("I sympathize
  with the journalists"). The target is careless communication, not the number.
- notice: the exact definitional word carrying the load ("average relative
  decline," not "lost"); which ordinary word a reader will wrongly substitute.
- diction: precise nouns held constant (populations, not animals); honest
  signposting of difficulty ("things get slightly more complex") without talking
  down.
- reader: inclusive partnership ("let's take a look"), reader positioned as a
  fellow checker rather than an audience.
- the important move: she makes the reader compute one case before she explains
  it, so the critique is experienced rather than asserted.

## Tim Harford, "Tim Harford's guide to statistics in a misleading age"
Source: https://medium.com/financial-times/tim-harfords-guide-to-statistics-in-a-misleading-age-652a597c1d88
Craft:
- cadence: short declarative openers that build authority, then longer parallel
  constructions to explore the implication.
- argument: sets up a simple scaffold or test, then asks whether the suspect
  number survives it; common sense is carried a long way before any technical
  step.
- evidence: a familiar yardstick (the index-card simplicity) against which the
  quoted figure is measured, so the reader has something plain to hold.
- stance: skeptical but measured — numbers are treated as neutral and their
  deployment as the problem; the incentive to mislead is named without cynicism.
- notice: the distance between what a figure counts and what it is quoted to
  mean; he asks what would have to be true for the claim to hold.
- diction: plain and conversational; jargon is named, then dissolved in the same
  breath.
- reader: the rhetorical question as invitation, pulling the reader into the
  check rather than lecturing; verification treated as a conversational act.
- the important move: he hands the reader a test they can run themselves, so
  judgment feels available rather than reserved to experts.

## Matt Levine, "Money Stuff: Financial Engineering for Green Power"
Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/banking-law/matt-levines-money-stuff-financial-engineering-for-green-power
Craft:
- cadence: simple conditional, then a specific detail, then a conversational
  translation ("call it 0.3 cents"); an affirmation followed at once by escalating
  complication.
- argument: build the mechanism patiently until it feels sensible, then pivot to
  the point where its logic cracks; generous before critical, which makes the
  critique land harder.
- evidence: the mechanism's own internal logic is the evidence, carried in
  concrete specifics rather than adjectives.
- stance: the designers built something coherent and are treated as such; fair,
  even warm, before the seam is shown.
- notice: where a locally rational structure produces a perverse edge — the "not
  totally safe" boundary that the sunny version omits.
- diction: technical material handled conversationally ("call it"), collapsing
  distance; asides stay controlled.
- reader: a trusted peer walked through the build; any lightness keeps company,
  never scores a point.
- the important move: affirm-then-complicate — grant the real truth of a thing,
  then mark its exact limit in the next beat, so fairness and critique occupy the
  same sentence.
