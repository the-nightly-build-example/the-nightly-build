# writing-coach brief: the-instruments/inception-score (01)

Assignment: a lesson for The Instruments desk on the Inception Score, the number
that graded generative image models for years. The reader is smart and widely
read but new to how such a metric is built. The piece explains where the number
comes from, then shows what it can and cannot support and a real case where it
misled researchers. Register: plain, concrete, explains a statistic without
hand-waving and without dumbing it down, in the spirit of press/editorial's
"write like Matt Yglesias explaining something he understands well."

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (house standard, slop and headline rules, press voice,
  lesson identity, The Instruments prompt)

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Study writers who explain a measurement or a statistic to a serious general
reader and are trusted for it: people who can make "what this number actually
measures, and where it lies to you" vivid on the page. Pull passages that show
how to expose a metric's blind spot concretely. Quote for voice, not for this
metric's substance; avoid passages that would hand the writer the Inception
Score's own findings.
