# Voice guide: the-instruments / needle-in-a-haystack (01)

## Directive

Write as a fair-minded examiner walking the reader through how a test is built,
then showing exactly what a high score on it does and does not require. The
register is the house plain teaching voice. The one thing this article must add
to the default is a controlled *deflation*: the near-perfect number is real, and
the writer's job is to keep the reader from reading more into it than the test
can hold.

Deflate through mechanism, never through attitude. When a score looks more
impressive than it is, the correcting sentence explains how the test works, not
how the writer feels about it. The reader should come away thinking the number
was honestly measured and honestly bounded, not that it was a trick or that the
system is a fraud. No sneer, no reveal, no gotcha. The limit is a property of
what the test asks for, and the prose states it as flatly as it states the
score.

Handle the impressive number the way a careful grader does. The first time a
strong score appears, the sentence that carries it also carries the comparison
that fixes its meaning: the floor a guess would reach, or the identical score on
a task that needs only lookup. The reader never holds the headline figure alone
long enough to over-read it. This is stricter than the house rule that every
number gets a familiar comparison. Here the comparison is chosen to reveal the
ceiling, and it travels in the same breath as the number.

Move at the pace of building one thing at a time. Lay a part of the test down,
make it work, then lay the next part on it, in the order the reader needs to
watch the score get produced. Let the concrete apparatus carry the abstraction:
the planted sentence, the surrounding filler, the question, the grader's yes-or-
no. Reach for the abstract word (retrieval, reasoning, comprehension) only once
the concrete machine has earned it, and once named, hold the name.

Keep the two capabilities the article turns on cleanly separated every time they
appear. Whatever the body calls "finding the planted fact" and "using the whole
document," those are two names for two different things, and a sentence that lets
them blur has lost the lesson. The reader's fluency is the whole deliverable: by
the end they can look at any long-context score and ask what the test actually
required.

## Licenses

form: direct address (second person) in the body
move: Mitchell states the reader's own natural assumption in plain words, then
      turns the evidence on it, so the correction feels like a shared discovery
      rather than a lecture. The default reserves direct address to the bookends;
      this licenses it inside the body for staging-and-testing an intuition.
bar:  each use names a specific assumption the declared reader actually holds
      about the score, and the sentences that follow overturn or confirm it with
      the test's mechanism. Cut any "you" that only decorates or that gestures at
      a hypothetical reader's reaction.

form: a question the lesson answers in the same beat
move: Narayanan and Kapoor pose the reframing question that splits what a number
      measures from what it is taken to prove ("or did it just memorize?"), then
      answer it on the spot. The house is wary of questions; this admits one when
      it marks the exact pivot from what the test scores to what people read into
      it.
bar:  the article answers the question within the same paragraph, from evidence
      already on the page. One such pivot at most. A question left hanging, or
      one the piece never resolves, is cut.

form: the plain undercut
move: Willison retires an apparent win with a quiet qualifying clause instead of
      a flourish, so the deflation reads as fact rather than triumph. This
      licenses ending a strong-sounding result on a flat clause that bounds it.
bar:  the undercut rests on a stated reason from the test's construction, not on
      tone or a knowing aside. If the bounding clause carries no mechanism, it is
      attitude, and it goes.

## Melanie Mitchell, "Evaluating Large Language Models Using 'Counterfactual Tasks'"
Source: https://aiguide.substack.com/p/evaluating-large-language-models
Craft:
- cadence: short arresting openers give way to one long qualifying sentence that
  complicates the claim just made; the rhythm itself enacts "not so fast."
- argument: pairs a task the system does well with a matched task it cannot have
  memorized, and lets the gap between the two scores carry the whole point.
- evidence: every headline accuracy figure arrives beside its control, most
  often the random-guess floor, so the reader reads the two numbers as one fact.
- stance: skeptical without cynicism; treats a strong result as real but
  unproven, and insists on the test that would tell the difference.
- notice: fixes on the slip between surface performance and the mechanism under
  it, and names that mechanism plainly (patterns from training vs general
  reasoning).
- diction: technical terms sit next to plain ones in the same sentence, each
  abstraction cashed out in a concrete failure the reader can picture.
- reader: voices the reader's own reasonable expectation first, then tests it, so
  the correction lands as collaboration.
- the important move: she never lets an impressive number stand alone for even a
  sentence; the deflating comparison is built into the clause that reports it.

## Simon Willison, "AbsenceBench: Language Models Can't Tell What's Missing"
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/20/absencebench/
Craft:
- cadence: short declaratives alternate with longer explanatory lines; reads as
  someone thinking aloud and landing each step before the next.
- argument: inductive and discovery-shaped: pose the puzzle, show the data, then
  offer the mechanism, in that order, so the reader reaches the conclusion with
  him.
- evidence: numbers sit in a table and are left to speak; the prose points at the
  contrast rather than reciting the figures.
- stance: observer-reporter; notices the counterintuitive result and declines to
  editorialize past what the data shows.
- notice: seizes on the case that runs opposite to the famous easy result, using
  the near-perfect score as the setup for where the same system fails.
- diction: plain and concrete, tasks named by their real particulars rather than
  by abstract categories.
- reader: trusts the reader to draw the inference; supplies the pieces and the
  turn, not the moral.
- the important move: he frames a limitation as a property of the machinery
  (what can and cannot be attended to), so the deflation reads as mechanism, not
  complaint.

## Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, "Evaluating LLMs is a minefield"
Source: https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~arvindn/talks/evaluating_llms_minefield/
Craft:
- cadence: unadorned claim, then an immediate clause that qualifies or undercuts
  it; the sentence trains the reader to distrust the surface reading.
- argument: separates what a test scores from what the score is claimed to prove,
  and drives the wedge with a single reframing question.
- evidence: an impressive result is set down whole, then reopened by asking what
  else could produce it (memorization, prompt artifact), with the alternative
  made concrete.
- stance: plainspoken and mildly exasperated at hype, while crediting the reader
  with the appetite for an honest accounting.
- notice: fixes on the methodological gap others skip: whether the number
  measures the system or an artifact of how it was asked.
- diction: accessible throughout; the technical point is carried in ordinary
  words and a worked example.
- reader: addresses a capable reader directly and expects them to follow a
  reversal without hand-holding.
- the important move: converts a strong benchmark result into an open question by
  naming the cheaper explanation the number cannot rule out.

## Note for the writer

The house default already gives plain teaching prose, concrete diction, and a
familiar comparison for every number. What this article needs beyond it: the
deflating comparison rides in the same clause as the impressive score, the two
capabilities the piece turns on stay unblurred at every mention, and the bound
on the number is always a stated mechanism rather than a tone. Direct address
and a single reframing question are licensed above to run the intuition-then-
test move; use them where the pivot from what-the-test-scores to what-people-
read-into-it actually happens, and nowhere else.
