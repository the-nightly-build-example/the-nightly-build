# researcher brief: what-could-go-wrong/open-weights-release (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (../../editorial-direction.md) — citation standard,
  series territory, declared reader
- commission.md (../../commission.md) — the argument, angle, source floor

Output: evidence.md (this directory)

Build the evidence record for the open-weights proliferation argument, from the
original documents, not commentary. Answer:

- The careful statement of the risk and its irreversibility (Seger et al. 2023):
  the exact claims, and who the authors are.
- The demonstrated safety-removal result (Qi et al. 2023): exactly what was shown
  — how cheaply/quickly safety fine-tuning was stripped, on which models, with the
  numbers.
- The demonstrated/speculative line on catastrophic uplift: the RAND study
  (Mouton et al. 2024) TOP-LINE published conclusion on whether LLM access gave a
  measurable operational uplift for biological-weapon attack planning, and its
  study design/scope (number of red teams/cells, that it compared LLM-assisted vs
  internet-only), taken from the report's summary/abstract and findings section.
  Record only the conclusion and its scope — that the study found no statistically
  significant uplift. Do NOT extract, quote, paraphrase, or record any operational
  detail, step, agent, or method from the threat scenarios; the article's point is
  precisely that the catastrophic-uplift claim is not demonstrated, and it needs
  only the finding, never the hazardous specifics. If a source pushes into
  operational CBRN detail, skip that passage. Also record whether open models trail
  the frontier at the relevant capabilities. Record contradictions honestly — this
  demonstrated/speculative gap is the crux.
- The marginal-risk rebuttal (Kapoor, Narayanan et al. 2024): the framework and
  what it concludes, and the defensive/research value of open weights.
- What today's proponents want done (evaluations, staged release, licensing, KYC)
  and current policy touchpoints, from primary or reliable secondary sources.
- Meta's open-weight release position as a primary document/event (release note
  or license), reported as fact.

Source floor: at least 8 sources, at least 4 primary, at least 1 secondary.
Classify each and say why. The Contradictions section is central here: record the
strongest evidence against both the doom and the dismissal. Confirm every URL
resolves to the document's own page.
