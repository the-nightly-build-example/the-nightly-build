# Editorial review: what-could-go-wrong/situational-awareness (editor/02)

This is a focused confirmation read scoped to the one editor/01 required item and
whether its fix introduced anything new. Settled objections are not reopened.

## Skeptic

The editor/01 break was a false label in the SAD table's display text: the ceiling
column was headed "Human" and the caption called 90.7% "a human ceiling" that
"matches an informed person." The figure was correct; the label was false, because
SAD's 90.7% is its Upper Baseline — a composite that uses human roleplay on the
task categories a person can do and assumes an oracle 100% on those none can
(token-counting in INTROSPECT, ANTI-IMITATION). No informed person scores 90.7% on
all of SAD.

The fix holds against the evidence (SAD §3.1 / Table 1). In the article now:

- The column header reads "Upper baseline," not "Human."
- The caption describes it honestly: "SAD's upper baseline — human roleplay on the
  tasks a person can do, an assumed perfect score on those none can." That is the
  accurate composition of the Upper Baseline in the primary.
- The 90.7% (all of SAD) and 37.5% / 27.4% floors are unchanged and correct.
- The STAGES row's ~70% now sits under "Upper baseline" and remains accurate:
  both STAGES tasks are human-doable, so the STAGES upper baseline is the human
  roleplay ceiling (~70.3%). The column label is true for that row too.

The label that a reader keeps now matches what SAD measured. The costly, most
visible error is resolved.

## Cut

The changed caption clause carries no slop. "Human roleplay on the tasks a person
can do, an assumed perfect score on those none can" is a factual gloss on the
baseline, tied to this subject, doing real explanatory work; it makes no claim
beyond the evidence and grades nothing about the article's own method. The added
"A model at chance has no ability" is a plain factual clarifier on the floor
column, not filler. Both are grammatical. No new negative-parallelism reflex, no
signpost, no prompt leakage entered with the change.

Nothing else moved. The editor/01-approved body is intact, including the two
already-correct framings the brief named: "far below the reachable ceiling" (the
49.5% overall) and "well short of a human" (the 50.7% STAGES). Grammar and syntax
across the changed display text are clean.
