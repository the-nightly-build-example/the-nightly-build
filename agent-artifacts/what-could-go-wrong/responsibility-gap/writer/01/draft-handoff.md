harder to resolve.

## How the corrected angle was honoured

- The deployed cases are presented as supervised by design (Uber developmental
  with a safety driver; Tesla Autopilot SAE Level 2 with the driver as the
  responsible operator), so they refute the strong "no one can be held" claim
  only for supervised systems. The fully-autonomous, no-human case is stated
  plainly as untested (the-line section and the body's close).
- Legal and moral responsibility are kept separate. The legal outcomes (Vasquez
  convicted of endangerment, three years' probation, reduced from negligent
  homicide; Uber not charged; the 2,031,220-vehicle Tesla recall) are reported
  as the actual "who was held," distinct from NTSB probable cause. Tigard's
  concession that the moral gap is "far less clear, and thereby less easily
  resolved" lands the split.
- EU law is not presented as having closed the gap: the AI Liability Directive
  was withdrawn in 2025; the Product Liability Directive applies only from
  December 2026 and only to "products."
- autonomous-weapons, automation-bias and normal-accidents are linked in prose,
  not re-taught. No company is named as an authority.
