Genre: a careful risk-argument explainer that states an environmental worry at
full strength, then separates measured aggregates from viral per-unit figures,
without joining the alarm or dismissing it. Register: Matt Yglesias explaining
something he understands — plain, concrete, numerate, even-handed; trusted not
to be selling either panic or complacency.

energy question with numbers, fairly, for a smart general reader: candidates
include Hannah Ritchie / Our World in Data, writers at The Economist, Brian
Potter (Construction Physics) on energy/infrastructure, or a data journalist who
debunks viral figures carefully. Prefer writing that respects a real concern
while puncturing a bad number. If you quote a piece on AI energy specifically,
quote only for the writing.

Two or three passages per exemplar, each from a different part, quoted exactly,
with checked URL and date. Under each, two or three plain sentences on what is
good and where the person shows. Report the path and the single strongest
takeaway for the writer (especially: taking a real concern seriously while
refusing a badly-sourced number).
