# writing-coach brief: what-could-go-wrong/lethal-trifecta (01)

Assignment: a lesson for the What Could Go Wrong desk on the "lethal trifecta"
risk argument: an AI agent that can read private data, reads untrusted content,
and can talk to the outside world can be hijacked by prompt injection to leak
that data. The reader is smart and widely read but not a security engineer. The
piece states the argument at full strength, tests it against the real 2025
exploits, and separates what is demonstrated from what is speculated. Register:
plain, sober, judges a risk argument on its merits without alarm or dismissal,
in the spirit of press/editorial's "no hype and no doom."

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (house standard, slop and headline rules, press voice,
  lesson identity, the What Could Go Wrong prompt)

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Study writers who explain computer-security threat models to a serious general
audience and are trusted for calibration: people who can make an attack legible
and say exactly how worried to be. Pull passages that show how to lay out an
adversary's reasoning fairly and then draw a clean line between a demonstrated
exploit and a hypothetical one. Quote for voice, not substance; avoid passages
that hand the writer this lesson's own exploits or conclusions.
