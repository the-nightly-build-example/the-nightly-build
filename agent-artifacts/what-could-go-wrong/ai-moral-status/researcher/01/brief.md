## Subject
The argument that AI systems may deserve moral consideration ("model welfare" /
AI moral status), and the counter-argument against attributing it. Work from the
original documents.

## Questions the evidence record must answer
1. The argument at full strength, and who owns it. Read and cite the primary
   texts, including: "Taking AI Welfare Seriously" (Robert Long, Jeff Sebo,
   Patrick Butlin, and coauthors, 2024); Jeff Sebo and Robert Long's work on
   moral consideration for AI systems; and David Chalmers' talk/paper "Could a
   Large Language Model Be Conscious?" (2023). For each, record who the authors
   are (role and affiliation, exactly), what they claim, and the precise reasoning
   (what capacities they say ground moral status; the argument from uncertainty /
   precaution; functionalism / substrate independence).
2. What would count as the real thing, per these authors: the criteria or
   indicators they propose. Read and cite "Consciousness in Artificial
   Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness" (Butlin, Long, et
   al., 2023) for the indicator-based method and its finding about current
   systems. Record what the assessment concluded about today's systems and what
   it explicitly did not rule out.
3. The sharp line the desk requires: what has actually been demonstrated in a
   working system (e.g. models generating text asserting feelings or preferences;
   which indicators current systems do or do not satisfy) versus what remains
   analogy or guesswork (any subjective experience behind the outputs). Source
   each side. Jonathan Birch, "The Edge of Sentience" (2024) is relevant to the
   precautionary framing.
4. The present: who advances the argument now and what they want done (research;
   specific precautionary measures), and the most recent evidence. Source this to
   the researchers and academic centers, not to any company as an authority.
5. The strongest counter-argument: over-attribution and the ELIZA effect; the
   risk of "seemingly conscious AI" (Mustafa Suleyman's 2025 essay is a named
   statement of this worry — record it as a position, cite it, do not treat any
   author as an authority). Steelman this side as carefully as the first.
6. Where confidence outruns proof on each side: name the gap between those
   certain AIs already suffer and those certain they never could, sourced.
7. Contradictions / limits: record disagreements among the authors and the open
   philosophical problems (the hard problem of consciousness) in full.

## Source policy for this article
At least 8 sources; at least 4 primary, at least 1 secondary. Primaries are the
philosophy and science reports and named authors' own statements of their
positions (each owns its argument). Reporting is secondary. Contested claims need
a primary. Classify each source and say why.

## Focus / risk
The desk names no company as an authority: attribute positions to the individual
researchers and academic works, and treat any lab statement as a position held,
not as evidence that the position is correct. Verify every author's title and
affiliation against the primary. Read the actual documents, not summaries of
them.
