## The lesson holds
The demonstrated-vs-analogy line is stated plainly and never blurred. The
"rule-based" section says the 2010 crash carried no AI: the sell program was one
fixed instruction, the high-frequency traders ran fixed strategies, and Sarao's
layering was rule-based too, all "years before a language model could hold a
conversation." The "simulations" section says the AI worry is a simulation and a
set of regulator arguments, closing on "What none of them can point to is a
crash on record caused by AI trading agents. That event has not happened." The
two are kept apart. The distinction from algorithmic-collusion (pricing) and
algorithmic-monoculture (correlated decisions) is named in Orientation with
plain links and listed again in Background, not re-argued.

## Substantive edits (all deletions, per slop rule)
1. Cut "A five-second automatic pause on the futures exchange broke the slide."
   The five-second pause is a specific figure evidence.md does not carry; the
   recovery itself ("rebounding almost as quickly," reverted by ~3:00 p.m.) is
   supported, so I kept "Buyers returned, and by about 3:00 p.m. most stocks
   were trading at sane prices again." Hedged by removing the unverifiable
   number rather than inventing a source.
2. Cut the stakes-announcing opener "Here is the part that matters most for any
   argument about AI." It grades the argument instead of continuing it; the
   section now opens on the concrete claim, "Nothing in that account was
   artificial intelligence."
3. Reworked the "what would settle" opener from a rhetorical question ("Who
   presses the argument today, and what do they want?") to a declarative,
   because the prior section already opens on a question ("So why worry about AI
   at all?") and two in a row read as a mold.
4. Trimmed the body's final couplet ("The flash crash on the record has
   happened. The flash crash people fear from AI has not.") and its trailing
   [6] citation. It duplicated the takeaway's parallel construction, closed the
   body on a verdict-style restatement the press rule forbids, and the [6]
   (simulation paper) did not support a "has not happened" claim. The body now
   closes on the evidence gap: "Neither exists yet." s6 is still cited twice in
   the simulations section, so no source was orphaned.

## Furniture, headline, structure
- Headline commits to the argument's own claim and avoids the desk's "X, and
  none/nothing has yet" and "X did Y until Z" molds.
- Dek adds the mechanism and the simulation point without a semicolon reversal,
  suspended question, or comma triad.
- Section headings are varied and concrete; a skimmer reconstructs the argument.
  No "How far the evidence actually reaches" heading; none is a comma-and clause.
- Bookends address the reader (allowed); the body does not. Takeaway lands the
  judgment; the body no longer closes on a restate block.
- Zero em-dashes, no banned terms. Stat strip and holds-up grid are the only
  furniture; both carry only verified figures.
