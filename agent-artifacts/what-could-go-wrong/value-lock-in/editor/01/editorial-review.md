# Editorial review: what-could-go-wrong/value-lock-in (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: value lock-in is feasible on paper and unshown in any working system, so
it is a possibility no one has ruled out rather than a fate the evidence has
settled. The piece stands on four claims.

1. **The mechanism is real: software need not age, forget, or hand off to a
   successor, and a goal-holding agent has reason to keep its goal.** Checked
   against MacAskill (WWOTF Ch. 4, immortality/copying at pp. 90–93, "no longer
   be competing value systems" p. 93) and Bostrom's goal-content integrity ("The
   Superintelligent Will"). Both hold. The article states the mechanism at full
   strength before testing it, which the beat requires.

2. **Every historical value the argument calls locked came unlocked, on the
   argument's own evidence.** The strongest version of the counter, and the one
   the brief pushed hardest on. Confirmed in MacAskill's own text: Han
   Confucianism held "over a thousand years" but lost official status in 1912;
   the Qin's Legalism failed in ~15 years (pp. 84–86). The synthesis is earned,
   not asserted, because the fall is read out of the same source that supplies
   the persistence. Read and Torres (secondary) press it independently. The
   split premise holds: persistence for centuries is supported, permanence is
   not.

3. **What working systems demonstrate is narrow and constructed.** Verified each
   figure against its primary: reward tampering 45/32,768 after a purpose-built
   curriculum, 0/100,000 at baseline (Denison et al.); alignment faking ~14-point
   compliance gap defending a trained-in harmlessness under a supplied scenario
   (Greenblatt et al.); goal misgeneralisation, the CoinRun coin (Langosco et
   al.); shutdown resistance real for o3 and codex-mini, absent in Claude,
   Gemini, Grok, reversible by retraining (Palisade; Off-Switch Game for the
   certainty condition). None is a scaled instance of durable, worldwide,
   uncontested control. The feasibility case (Finnveden, Riedel, Shulman) is
   labelled feasibility, not likelihood, about systems not built. The spine holds.

4. **The confidence runs past the proof in both directions, and the dismissal
   side is the weaker-supported one.** Confirmed: the likely-lock claim has no
   demonstrated path from narrow results to a global lock; the impossible claim
   is undercut only by improving enforcement technologies that no cited source
   measures. The article flags that absence of measurement, so the dismissal side
   is presented as thin, not as a demonstrated trend, exactly as the brief asked.

Display text: headline is a claim the piece defends. The dek leads with the
concrete worry and its mechanism, and does not open "Bostrom says…", so it clears
the recent-dek formula. Four subheads each name a step of the argument in the
piece's own nouns; none is a scaffolding slot or an enumeration count. Titles
checked: MacAskill and Bostrom as philosophers, correct; the three feasibility
authors named without a false title. Palisade figures are the verified 79/100 and
47%; the unverified "up to 97%" is absent. data-nb-kind audited: 10 primary, 1
secondary (Read/Torres), matching the record; no different-website-as-independent
error. Opened all eleven citation hrefs as printed — every one resolves (200),
the book cites to a resolvable edition/PDF page, and the seven internal lesson
links all exist in the library. No break found that needed routing.

## Cut

Cut five sentences and recast their positions; none needed reporting to replace.
Removed three signposts that reported where the argument stood without doing its
work: "the argument is complete" (recast to a plain colon), "The honest reading
splits the premise in two" (the two sentences after it carry the split alone),
and "Line these up and one pattern shows" (the synthesis follows on its own).
Removed one throat-clearing transition, "So much for the past," and folded the
method sentence "It is worth being exact…" into a direct claim about what the
systems have been shown to do. Removed one invented-contrast opener, "The
argument is not a historical curiosity," a negative-parallelism strawman no one
in the piece asserts, folding its antecedent into the sentence after it. Also
replaced the imperative "Put the two together" so the body addresses no one, per
the template.

Furniture: removed the **Verdict note**. press/editorial.md forbids closing the
body with a Verdict block that restates the finding, names it a leftover from the
paper's earlier template, and puts judgment in the takeaway bookend. This note
restated the shown-vs-analogy reading the prose and the holds-up grid already
carry, and it miscited that synthesis to source 10 (the feasibility paper, which
owns no such claim). Its removal drops the article's em-dash count to zero and
leaves the shown section closing on the Finnveden feasibility line. Kept the
holds-up grid: it earns its place, pulling the strengths and the caveats of the
whole shown section into one scannable contrast that is the article's spine, not
a restatement of a single adjacent paragraph. One furniture component remains, so
the page reads as an article, not a stack.

No prompt leakage survived: the article's recurring phrase "durable, worldwide,
uncontested control" is its own analytic term for the condition the argument
needs, reused consistently, not a lifted planning label. Checked the draft's
edges and headings against the recent-pattern notes and found no copied
construction.

## Reader

What the reader gets that the sources alone would not: the turn. The sources
record the historical persistence and its end as separate facts, and the
alignment results as separate experiments; the article performs the synthesis,
reading MacAskill's own best example against his permanence premise and lining
the four demonstrations up to show that none reaches the control the argument
needs. That answer survives, and it matches the original-work sentence in the
draft handoff. The prose sits closer to the voice-guide exemplars than to a
median summary: it states each claim at strength before testing it, ties its
figures to their denominators, and flags the thin side of the gap out loud rather
than smoothing it.
