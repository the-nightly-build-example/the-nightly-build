# writing-coach brief: what-could-go-wrong/emergent-misalignment (01)

Inputs:

- `commission.md` (at the artifact root) — the argument, angle (open at strength,
  draw the shown-versus-inferred line, bring to the present), neighbors, and
  habits not to inherit.
- `editorial-direction.md` (at the artifact root) — house standard, slop, headline
  standard, press voice, lesson identity, and the series prompt.

Output: `writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md` (under this article's artifact root).

This round: the genre is an even-handed teaching piece on a surprising, contested
AI-safety result, one that states the argument at full strength and then draws a
hard line between what a real experiment showed and what stays inference. Study
writers who handle a disputed empirical claim fairly: who can steelman a worry,
hold it to the evidence without alarm or dismissal, and keep reported fact,
estimate, and speculation visibly separate. Prefer exemplars who reason carefully
about surprising findings over advocates on either side.

Break the paper-wide opener formula ("By the end you will know X. You will also
see Y") and do not model the recent what-could-go-wrong "famous number, then its
deflation" opener.

Run environment: outbound web is available through a proxy. Retrieve the raw text
of every exemplar page and verify each quotation word for word against it before
reporting, per your skill.
