Subject and angle: the takeoff-speeds argument, fast/discontinuous versus
slow/continuous AI progress, and the warning time each implies. The article
steelmans the fast case, tests it against real-system evidence, and separates
what is shown from what is analogy or projection. Work from the original
documents, never the commentary about them.

Read first, primary, in full where relevant:
- I. J. Good, 1965, "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine,"
  for the original intelligence-explosion statement and its exact wording.
- The Hanson-Yudkowsky "AI-Foom Debate" (2008, later collected), for the fast-
  takeoff case Yudkowsky makes and Hanson's continuous counter. Get each side's
  actual claims.
- Nick Bostrom, Superintelligence (2014), the sections on speed of takeoff (slow,
  moderate, fast) and decisive strategic advantage. Cite the specific passages.
- Paul Christiano, "Takeoff speeds" (2018), for the continuous case: the specific
  arguments about incremental progress, forecastability, and economic effects
  preceding any runaway.

Then, for the present state of the argument:
- The scaling-laws evidence that capability has been a smooth function of compute
  and data (Kaplan et al.; Hoffmann et al. / Chinchilla) — only what supports the
  continuity claim.
- The best recent measured evidence on the trend, for example METR's work on the
  length of tasks models can complete and any measured AI R&D uplift. Give exact
  figures, dates, and what was measured.
- A current fast-takeoff scenario or statement (for example the "AI 2027"
  scenario or named researchers arguing short, sharp timelines) and a current
  continuous-progress position, with who holds each and what they want done.

Questions the evidence must answer:
- The strongest version of the fast-takeoff case, in its authors' own terms.
- The strongest version of the continuous case, in its authors' own terms.
- Which claims are supported by observed system behavior (continuity of scaling;
  measured R&D assistance) and which rest on analogy (evolution, AlphaGo) or on
  systems that do not yet exist.
- Where present-day confidence, alarm or dismissal, outruns the evidence.

Record contradictions in full; the disagreement is the subject, so both sides get
their strongest form. Steelman each before any weighing. Preserve any verified
quantitative series suitable for a chart (for example a measured capability trend
over time), only from primary numbers. Note source assets only if an exact figure
from a primary document would carry the argument better than prose.
