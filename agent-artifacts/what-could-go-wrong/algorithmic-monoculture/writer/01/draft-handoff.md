## Original work

The article converts the evidence record's inventory of ten sources into a
single argument-and-test arc that keeps each paper's stated scope attached to
its own claim, so a reader holds Kleinberg-Raghavan's noise-family conditions,
Bommasani et al.'s partial-not-strict monoculture qualification, and Peng and
Garg's applicant-welfare reversal in one frame rather than choosing among
them. That synthesis is visible section by section: orientation states the
theorem in its authors' own quantities and quote; mechanism teaches the
preference-for-independence intuition and pins the effect to Mallows,
Gaussian/Laplacian RUMs at n=3, and the Plackett-Luce vanishing case;
deployed-measurements introduces the outcome-homogenization ratio (Bommasani
2022), tabulates FER+ and DIGIT joint-failure rates against per-model rates
(Toups 2023), and adds Kim et al.'s LLM agreement numbers; pymetrics reports
the FAccT 2026 chi-square and adverse-impact results together with the paper's
own "only 42 shared models, 142 employer pairs" qualification and its Kline et
al. cross-check that fits the null; the extensions section carries Peng and
Garg's Theorem 2(i) applicant-welfare reversal and Jo, Garg, and Raghavan's
IRT-null critique as constraints on current claims; the takeaway keeps the
three parts in one register without collapsing the finding into either
overclaim or dismissal. Amazon 2018 and CrowdStrike 2024 appear only as
correlated-failure analogies with an explicit label to that effect.
