This round's focus — the skeptic read matters most here, because the record
materially complicates the argument and the piece must not overclaim:

- Kleinberg-Raghavan 2021 holds only under stated conditions (Mallows always;
  Gaussian/Laplacian RUMs with three candidates; vanishes under Plackett-Luce;
  worked example is ~4% welfare loss; extension beyond n=3 is open). Confirm the
  article states these limits, not a universal claim.
- Peng and Garg 2024 is the opposing finding (monoculture matches applicants to
  preferred firms on average, more robust to differential access, does not
  increase systemic exclusion overall in the many-firm limit). Confirm it is
  steelmanned from its own document, not strawmanned.
- The deployed evidence is *partial* correlation via shared vendor and shared
  features (pymetrics: 42 shared models, 142 employer pairs), not strict
  monoculture. Confirm the article does not present it as strict monoculture.
- Amazon 2018 and CrowdStrike are labeled as correlated-failure analogies, not
  monoculture evidence. Confirm the labels are accurate.
- The "price of anarchy at most 2" figure must NOT appear (the underlying
  Kleinberg 2025 primary was not opened; the writer correctly dropped it and did
  not cite Hedden & Raghavan). Confirm it is absent; if it crept in, cut it.
- Two secondaries (MIT Tech Review, Wikipedia/CrowdStrike): audit their
  data-nb-kind and that they carry only context, not a load-bearing claim.
