Output: ./voice-guide.md

This series pins no voice guide, so study the domain fresh. The article teaches
one AI-risk argument (an AI hiding hostile intent until it is strong enough to
act) for a smart reader with no AI-safety background and no time in a codebase.
Study writers who explain contested, high-stakes arguments without joining a
side: people who can present a claim at full strength, then test it hard, and let
the reader decide. Prefer careful essayists and researchers whom both proponents
and skeptics of AI risk would grant are fair. The craft problem specific to this
piece: it must make a genuinely unsettling a-priori argument feel serious, then
draw a cold line between argument and evidence, all in a register with no hype and
no dismissal. Find passages that hold two opposing views in view at once and that
earn a grand claim only after the reasoning. Avoid exemplars written to persuade
toward doom or toward dismissal; the model here is the fair adjudicator, not the
advocate.
