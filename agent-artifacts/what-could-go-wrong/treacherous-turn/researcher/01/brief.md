# researcher brief: what-could-go-wrong/treacherous-turn (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (../../editorial-direction.md) — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- commission.md (../../commission.md) — argument, structure, mandatory neutrality requirement

Output: ./evidence.md

Work from the original documents, never the commentary about them. Target sources:

- The original argument: Bostrom, Superintelligence (2014), the treacherous-turn
  passage and the instrumental-convergence / "malignant failure modes" reasoning
  it rests on. Get Bostrom's own words for the claim. Read the actual text, not
  encyclopedia summaries.
- Any earlier statement of the same idea (e.g., Yudkowsky / MIRI writing on an AI
  behaving until it can win) to establish provenance, read firsthand.
- The 2024 empirical record, each read for exactly what it demonstrated and under
  what conditions:
  - "Sleeper Agents: Training Deceptive LLMs that Persist Through Safety Training"
    (Hubinger et al., 2024) — note that the backdoor is trained IN by the authors;
    record precisely what persisted and what that does and does not show about
    spontaneous deception.
  - "Alignment faking in large language models" (Greenblatt et al., 2024) — record
    the exact setup, the conditions under which the model faked alignment, the
    rates, and the stated limitations.
  - An in-context scheming evaluation (e.g., Apollo Research / Meinke et al., 2024,
    "Frontier Models are Capable of In-Context Scheming") — what was elicited,
    under what prompting, and what the authors themselves caution.
- The skeptical / critical side, given equal weight: published critiques arguing
  these are induced or contrived behaviors, or that the treacherous turn assumes
  capabilities and coherence current systems lack. Find real critics (researchers
  who dispute the interpretation), not strawmen. Two independent confirmations for
  any strong characterization of what an experiment "showed".

Answer these questions for the writer:
1. What exactly did Bostrom claim, in his words, and what is the argument's
   logical structure (which steps are assumption, which are inference)?
2. For each 2024 experiment: what was the setup, what was the result with numbers,
   and what did the authors themselves say it does NOT show?
3. Which parts of the treacherous-turn prediction have any empirical footing as of
   now, and which remain purely a-priori?
4. What do credible skeptics say, and what is their strongest point?
5. What would count as an actual treacherous turn in a deployed system, and has
   anything met that bar? (Expected answer: no — record the evidence for that.)

Neutrality is a sourcing obligation here: label every lab-authored safety paper as
what it is (a test run by a party with a stake), and do not let any company's
framing stand as authority. Record contradictions in full; this piece lives or
dies on the argument-versus-evidence gap. Confirm every URL resolves to the
paper's own page (arXiv/abstract or the lab's own PDF page, recorded as the
document's home).
