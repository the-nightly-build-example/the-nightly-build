This is a lesson for a serious educational paper explaining a public policy and
AI-safety debate to a general reader. All sources are published policy documents,
UN reports, and advocacy/research statements; report them factually, without alarm
or dismissal.

The spine (series requires it): open with the argument at FULL STRENGTH,
steelmanned — the accountability gap, difficulty complying with distinction/
proportionality under the laws of war, the lowered threshold for war, and
scalable/anonymous killing — so the reader sees why serious people are alarmed
before a word against it. Define "meaningful human control" plainly. Then TEST the
argument against what real systems do, drawing the sharp line. Then bring it to the
present and name where confidence outruns proof in BOTH directions.

Hold these findings from the record exactly (they are the article's real
contribution — weigh them, do not smooth them):
- The line, deflationary side: there is "no publicly available evidence" (Belfer
  Center, Feb 2025) that any country has used a FULLY autonomous weapon in combat.
  The 2021 UN Libya report's own wording ("or") does not resolve whether the
  Kargu-2 or a human-piloted aircraft did the killing; the closest expert claim
  (Kallenborn) is doubly hedged ("if... would likely represent"). "Slaughterbots"
  is an advocacy DRAMATIZATION commissioned by campaigners, not a documented case —
  say so when you use it.
- The line, against-dismissal side: narrow autonomy is already fielded and
  consequential. IAI markets the Harpy loitering munition as "fully autonomous"
  (detect-identify-strike via its own radar seeker, no described per-strike human
  confirmation), fielded since the 1990s — before the 2015 open letter framed this
  as a future risk. Article 36, the NGO that coined "meaningful human control,"
  concedes in its founding paper that "there are already limitations to levels of
  human control exerted in existing military systems." So some erosion of per-shot
  control is already normal, not purely hypothetical.
- The honest present verdict lives in that gap: fully autonomous kill-decisions at
  scale with no human in the loop remain unfielded (the alarm's runway), while
  narrow autonomy that blurs "meaningful human control" is already deployed (the
  dismissal's blind spot).

Sourcing caveat: the Campaign to Stop Killer Robots' own site was unreachable, so
its argument is reconstructed via Human Rights Watch's 2025 report (HRW is a
coordinating member). Attribute claims to HRW where that is the source you have;
do not quote the campaign's own words as if read.

Continuity (link, do not overlap): what-could-go-wrong/cyber-uplift and
bioweapon-uplift are about AI helping humans BUILD/find weapons — different from
autonomy in the WEAPON'S kill decision. Make the distinction explicit and link.
Name no company or state as an authority; report positions. Leave the reader to
decide. No verdict block in the body beyond a documented Verdict note on a
holds-up grid if used — the takeaway bookend lands the judgment.
