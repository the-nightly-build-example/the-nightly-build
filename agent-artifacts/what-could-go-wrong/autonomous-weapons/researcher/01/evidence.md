This record supports the argument at full strength from the documents that own it (the FLI open letter, Article 36's definition of meaningful human control, the ICRC's position, and Human Rights Watch's 2025 report standing in for the Campaign to Stop Killer Robots), and it supports a firm answer to what is actually deployed: no fully autonomous system has killed at scale, but the Harpy loitering munition has autonomously executed the full detect-identify-attack sequence for decades, and the one widely cited battlefield case, the Kargu-2 in Libya, rests on a UN report that is hedged exactly where the "first autonomous kill" claim needs it to be firm. The record is thin in one place the brief asked for directly: the Campaign to Stop Killer Robots' own site (stopkillerrobots.org) refused every fetch attempt behind a bot-detection wall, so its position is reconstructed here through Human Rights Watch, its founding and coordinating member, and through search-engine-surfaced fragments of the campaign's own language, not through a document opened and read in full. That gap is recorded honestly in Discarded rather than papered over with an unread citation.

### Contradictions

Evidence that the alarm outruns anything fielded:
- The Belfer Center (Feb 2025), independently of the UN report dispute, states flatly that "there is no publicly available evidence indicating that countries have used fully autonomous weapon systems in combat," naming the Kargu-2 itself as a system where "human operators are still required to select targets and issue commands."
- The Lieber Institute and the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists independently reach the same reading of the one incident most often cited as a real-world case: the UN report's own language ("or") does not resolve which system did the killing, and the closest thing to an on-record expert claim (Kallenborn) is doubly hedged ("if... would likely represent").
- The single most vivid piece of evidence for the "scalable, anonymous killing" scenario in this record, Slaughterbots, is explicitly a dramatization commissioned by advocates, not a report of an existing system — a fact FLI's own page does not hide, but does not foreground either.
- The CCW's own 2023 mandate to its expert group is written to "not prejudge" whether any eventual instrument will be legally binding, which sits in tension with Guterres and Spoljaric's 2026 language of an imminent "moral red line."

Evidence that deployed autonomy is more consequential than a "just sci-fi" dismissal allows:
- The Harpy loitering munition is not a future scenario. IAI's own page markets it as "Fully Autonomous" and "Fire and Forget," capable of the complete detect-identify-strike sequence via its own radar seeker with no described requirement for per-strike operator confirmation, and it has been manufactured and exported since the 1990s — decades before the 2015 open letter that treats full autonomy as a near-future risk.
- Article 36, the NGO that coined "meaningful human control," concedes in its own founding paper that "there are already limitations to levels of human control exerted in existing military systems during the application of force" — meaning the erosion of per-shot human control is not a hypothetical the debate is trying to prevent, but a description of some systems already in service.
- Even the hedged Libya case shows loitering munitions and UCAVs operating together to inflict, in the Panel's own words, "significant casualties" on a retreating, disorganized force with "no real protection from remote air attacks" — a real operational effect on a real 2020 battlefield, whatever the precise autonomy level of any single kill.
