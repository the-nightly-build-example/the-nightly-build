This is a lesson for a serious educational paper explaining a public policy and
AI-safety debate to a general reader. All sources are published policy documents,
UN reports, and advocacy/research statements; summarize them factually.

The argument under examination: that lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS) are
a distinct danger. Work from original documents, not commentary.

Research questions the evidence record must answer, each traced to an owning
primary (give locators):
- The argument at full strength from the sources that own it: the 2015 Future of
  Life Institute open letter on autonomous weapons (what it claims, who signed),
  Stuart Russell's stated case (and the "Slaughterbots" scenario — what it depicts
  and who made it), the International Committee of the Red Cross position, and the
  Campaign to Stop Killer Robots. Establish the specific claims: accountability
  gap, difficulty complying with distinction/proportionality under international
  humanitarian law, lowered threshold for war, scalable/anonymous killing.
- A plain, sourced definition of "meaningful human control" and of what counts as
  a LAWS versus an automated/defensive system.
- What is already deployed, with exact provenance and how autonomous each really
  is: defensive systems that engage without a per-shot human decision (e.g.,
  close-in weapon systems, some air-defense systems) and loitering munitions with
  autonomous modes (e.g., the Harpy; the STM Kargu-2). For the Kargu-2, read the
  2021 UN Panel of Experts on Libya report (S/2021/229) and quote exactly what it
  says and how hedged it is; note any later disputes about that account.
- What remains analogy: fully autonomous systems selecting and killing at scale
  with no human in the loop, and cheap swarms performing selective assassination.
  Confirm no such system has been fielded, from a credible primary/expert source.
- The present state of the debate: the UN CCW Group of Governmental Experts
  process and why it has stalled, the split among states over a binding treaty,
  and recent calls for regulation (e.g., the UN Secretary-General). Cite primary
  outcome documents/statements.

Search for what breaks the angle in BOTH directions and record in Contradictions:
evidence that deployed autonomy is more consequential than the "just sci-fi"
dismissal allows, and evidence that the alarm outruns anything fielded.

Source policy: at least 8 sources, at least 4 primary, at least 1 secondary. A UN
report or an organization's own position paper is primary for what it states.
Classify each by authorship and stake. Confirm every URL resolves to the
document's own page.
