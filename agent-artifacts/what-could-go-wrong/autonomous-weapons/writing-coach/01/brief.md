Subject and genre: an explanatory lesson that teaches one argument about how AI
could go wrong — that lethal autonomous weapons, which select and engage targets
without meaningful human control, are a distinct danger — and then tests it
against what real weapon systems already do. The piece states the argument at full
strength before weighing it, and draws a sharp line between narrow autonomy that
is already deployed and the fully autonomous killing that remains science fiction.
Register: a serious daily course in how AI works, written like Matt Yglesias
explaining something he understands well — plain claims, concrete stakes, no fuss.
It takes a contested, high-stakes policy argument seriously without joining the
alarm or writing it off, and names no company or state as an authority.

Study writers who explain a contested, high-stakes argument fairly — steelmanning a
position they then test — and who are disciplined about the difference between what
has been shown and what is speculation. Prefer writers on security, technology
policy, or risk who keep judgment earned and never let rhetoric outrun the
evidence. Show how such writers hold a strong claim at arm's length without
deflating it, and handle a grave subject without melodrama.
