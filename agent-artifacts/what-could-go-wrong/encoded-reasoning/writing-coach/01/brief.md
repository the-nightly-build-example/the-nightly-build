# writing-coach brief: what-could-go-wrong/encoded-reasoning (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (artifact root) — house standard, paper voice, What Could Go Wrong series prompt.

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: a teaching lesson that takes one argument about how AI could go
wrong (that a model can hide its reasoning in ordinary-looking text, defeating
oversight that reads its words) at full strength, then tests it against what real
systems have actually been shown to do. The domain is AI safety and alignment
research, explained for a smart non-programmer. Register: a serious daily course,
plain and concrete, fair to a worrying argument without joining the alarm or the
dismissal.

Study writers the field rates who explain a technical risk argument to
non-specialists, steelman it, then draw a clean line between demonstrated results
and speculation. Show how they state the strongest version of a claim before
testing it, how they attribute a worry to the people who actually hold it, and how
they mark the boundary between "this was shown in a system" and "this is an
analogy about systems that do not exist yet." Prefer careful science and
safety-research explainers to advocacy on either side. Do not quote passages that
carry this lesson's own findings about steganography or chain-of-thought
monitoring; quote for the writing.

This piece has to make a reader take the worry seriously and also see exactly
where the evidence stops. Passages that hold a strong argument and its limits in
view at once, without hedging into mush, are the most useful.
