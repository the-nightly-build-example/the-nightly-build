# Commission: what-could-go-wrong/automated-escalation

## The argument

That putting AI into military decision-making could cause an inadvertent or
accelerated escalation to war, up to nuclear use, faster than humans can step in:
a "flash war" by analogy to a flash crash. One lesson, one argument, judged on its
merits.

## Why this closes a gap tonight (and why it is not a duplicate)

The desk has taught lethal autonomous weapons (autonomous-weapons, which is about
who chooses a target) and market cascades (flash-crash-risk, which is about
trading). It has not taught the distinct argument about decision speed and crisis
instability: that AI in early-warning, wargaming, and command support could
compress the time and human judgment that has kept crises from tipping into war.
There is a canonical original document (RAND, Geist and Lohn, 2018) and fresh
working-system evidence (2024 experiments running language models in wargames).

## What the lesson must do (follow the series prompt exactly)

1. Open with the argument at full strength. Name who made it (Geist and Lohn's
   RAND analysis; the "flash war" writers) and what worried them: automation bias
   in the loop, use-it-or-lose-it pressure, the speed at which coupled automated
   systems interact, and the erosion of the human deliberation that has defused
   past crises. Give the reasoning its most careful defender would.
2. Test it against what real systems actually do. Draw a sharp line between what
   has been shown in a working system (AI already in ISR, early warning, and
   decision support; the 2024 result that LLM agents escalate, sometimes to
   nuclear use, in wargame settings) and what is still analogy or guesswork
   (fully automated nuclear command; no state operates one, and several
   explicitly keep humans in the decision). The case turns on that line.
3. Bring it to the present: who advances it now and what they want (keeping humans
   in nuclear decisions; limits on AI in nuclear command, control, and
   communications; the 2023 US political declaration; the November 2024 US-China
   statement on human control of nuclear-weapons decisions), checked against the
   most recent evidence. Where confidence outruns proof, name the gap, whether the
   confidence is alarm or dismissal.

Work from the original documents, not commentary. Name no company as an authority.
Leave the reader to decide how worried to be.
