This article: a lesson that teaches the algorithmic-collusion risk argument on its
merits — states it at full strength (agents learning to coordinate without being
told to), draws a sharp line between what simulated and constrained-LLM systems
have demonstrably done and what is still extrapolation about real markets and
oversight, then brings it to the present without alarm or dismissal. The reader is
smart, widely read, new to both the economics and the AI-safety framing. The craft
challenge is holding a contested argument fairly: steelman before testing, keep
demonstrated results and conjecture visibly distinct, stay level.

Study writers the field rates for reasoning carefully about a contested technical/
economic argument for a general reader — people who steelman a position and then
qualify it, and who separate evidence from conjecture on the page. At least three
exemplars, read in full, quoted for how they present an argument at its strongest
and then test it. Quote for the writing, not for collusion facts. Do not model a
polemical voice.
