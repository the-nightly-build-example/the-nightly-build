This lesson teaches the algorithmic-collusion risk argument. Work from the original
documents, never the commentary about them.

Source obligations: minimum 8 sources, primary >= 4, secondary >= 1. Every URL
resolves; cite only what you read. Contested claims need a primary source.

Establish, each with a primary citation and exact quotes/figures:
  - The argument at full strength and its origins: Ezrachi & Stucke ("Virtual
    Competition," 2016) and the Calvano et al. 2020 AER paper. Quote the strongest
    statement of the worry and the mechanism (tacit collusion with no agreement).
  - Demonstrated results, with specifics and constraints: Calvano et al. 2020 —
    what the Q-learning agents did, the prices reached vs the competitive
    benchmark, the reward-punishment dynamics, and that it is a simulation; Fish,
    Gonczarowski & Shorrer 2024 ("Algorithmic Collusion by Large Language Models")
    — what the LLM pricing agents did and under what prompt/setup constraints.
    State exactly what was shown.
  - The real-world adjacent case kept distinct: the RealPage rent-pricing
    litigation (DOJ 2024) — establish that it is shared-data/common-algorithm
    coordination, not autonomously learned collusion, so the writer does not blur
    it into the demonstrated autonomous cases.
  - The speculative extrapolation, kept separate: coordination in real open markets
    at scale, and coordination against human oversight in safety-relevant settings
    — who argues it and on what basis, and what evidence is and is not offered.
  - The present debate: regulator positions (DOJ/FTC/EU/CMA) and economists, what
    remedies they propose, and a credible skeptical/deflationary primary (e.g.
    work questioning whether simulation results transfer to real markets) so the
    piece can steelman both sides.

Keep reported fact, demonstrated-in-simulation result, and conjecture distinct.
Name no company as an authority. Flag any disputed or unverifiable claim.
