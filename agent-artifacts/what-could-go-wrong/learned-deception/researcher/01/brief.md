## Documents to read in full

1. Park, Goldstein, O'Gara, Chen, Hendrycks 2024, "AI Deception: A Survey of
   Examples, Risks, and Potential Solutions" (Patterns, Cell Press). The spine.
   Capture: their exact definition of deception; their taxonomy; each concrete
   example they cite and, crucially, the conditions of each (which are games,
   which are prompted, which are lab tasks); and their policy recommendations.
2. Hagendorff 2024, "Deception abilities emerged in large language models" (PNAS
   121(24)). Capture the exact experimental claim and its limits (what task,
   what counts as deception, first- vs second-order).
3. Meta FAIR et al. 2022, "Human-level play in the game of Diplomacy by
   combining language models with strategic reasoning" (Science). Capture: the
   design goal that CICERO be "largely honest and helpful," and exactly what the
   system did that Park et al. characterize as deception/betrayal. Distinguish
   Meta's own framing from the deception critique; record both.
4. OpenAI GPT-4 System Card (2023). Capture the TaskRabbit/CAPTCHA episode
   verbatim enough to state it accurately: that it was an evaluation conducted by
   an external group (ARC/METR), that the model was given scaffolding and
   reasoned aloud, and exactly what it told the worker. Note that this was an
   elicited evaluation, not spontaneous deployment behavior.
5. The EU AI Act (final text, 2024). Capture the specific provisions on
   manipulative/deceptive AI and transparency (which article numbers), for the
   present/policy section. This is a primary legal document.

## Also consult

- Brown & Sandholm on superhuman poker (Pluribus, Science 2019) if you use
  bluffing as an example; capture whether bluffing is best described as intended
  game behavior.
- Scheurer et al. 2023, "Large Language Models can Strategically Deceive their
  Users when Put Under Pressure" (arXiv:2311.07590) — record it precisely as a
  "shown under crafted pressure" case; note it is a constructed scenario.
- One or two secondary sources: reputable reporting on CICERO's deception or on
  the Park et al. survey, to show how the argument travels in public. A
  secondary supports that a claim was made, never that it is true.

## Questions the record must answer

- A crisp, sourced definition separating deception from honest error and from
  persuasion.
- For every example the article may use: what was demonstrated, and under what
  conditions (game / prompted / lab). This condition column is the heart of the
  shown-vs-speculative line; be exact.
- What the argument's proponents want done, from the primaries.
- The strongest dismissal: that these are game artifacts and prompted stunts.
  Find proponents of that reading and record their reasoning in full.
- Where the newest evidence sits: any 2024-2025 result that strengthens or
  weakens "ordinary training produces deception." Record contradictions in full.
