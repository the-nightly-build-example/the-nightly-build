# Voice guide: presenting a contested argument, then weighing it

## Directive

Write as an assessor who can hold a frightening argument at full conviction and
then set it down and measure it. The default register here is cool and plain.
What the house default does not give, and what this article needs, is a second
temperature: inside the steelman the prose runs hot, carrying the belief the way
its most careful defender holds it; the moment the testing starts, it drops back
to cool and stays there. Do not blend the two into one lukewarm evenhandedness.
A summary that reports the argument at the same measured temperature it uses to
critique it never lets the reader feel the argument's pull, and a reader who has
not felt the pull cannot judge the case.

The reader is smart and not yet scared. They are being equipped to weigh the
argument themselves, so never tell them how worried to be, and never perform
balance. The work of persuasion is done by one instrument: the line between what
a real system has been shown to do and what is still analogy about systems that
do not exist. Make that boundary the spine of the piece, not a caveat tucked
into a clause. Attach every empirical result to the exact claim it reaches and
refuse to let it reach further. State uncertainty with edges: who holds which
view, on what basis, and what evidence would move it. Apply the same measuring
stick to alarm and to dismissal, in the same voice.

Three moves that will change sentences: run two audible temperatures rather than
one; bound each piece of evidence to what it actually establishes; state where
confidence outruns proof as a located fact, not a hedge.

## Licenses

form: the conviction register (a bounded passage in the argument's own voice)
move: Carlsmith and Alexander build the case in full, at its own confidence,
      before a word against it; Karnofsky states an alarming claim plainly as
      though settled. The advocacy carries no assessor's hedge inside it.
bar:  the passage is one the argument's most careful defender would sign without
      complaint, and it ends cleanly before the assessment begins. No smuggled
      qualifier, no knowing wink, no early "but." The register returns to cool at
      the first sentence of testing, and the reader can hear the change.

form: the scoped verdict (what one result shows, and what it does not reach)
move: Carlsmith marks each claim as conditional on exactly its premises; Alexander
      splits empirical observation from theoretical extrapolation in the same
      breath. The result is pinned to its distribution and its size.
bar:  the "does not reach" half names an overreach a careful reader would
      otherwise make from that evidence, never a strawman the sentence invents.
      The boundary is drawn in concrete terms (the specific setting, the measured
      gap), never as "more work is needed." One earned shown-versus-not contrast
      is the article's core instrument; a reflexive one is the banned mold.

form: located uncertainty (a stated position about how settled the question is)
move: Alexander sets a spread of serious estimates side by side and names his own
      wavering; Karnofsky concedes the strongest counterargument up front and
      keeps the claim anyway. Uncertainty is reported, with who and how much.
bar:  every use carries an edge: the people who hold the view, the basis they
      hold it on, or the evidence that would settle it. A use that resolves to
      "time will tell" or "it remains to be seen" is cut. When the article says
      confidence runs past proof, it points to the specific claim and the
      specific missing evidence, and it holds doom and dismissal to that one test.

## Joseph Carlsmith, "Power-Seeking AI and Existential Risk"
Source: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/TeSTeAwrnGtf9jwfR/power-seeking-ai-and-existential-risk
Craft:
- cadence: long premise-building sentences that resolve into a short, plain
  claim; the rhythm slows to lay a step, then lands it.
- argument: decomposes a large fear into named, separable premises, each
  assessed on its own. The scary whole is never argued as a whole.
- evidence: reasons from stated assumptions and marks their status openly ("it is
  conditional on the assumption that all previous hypotheses are true").
- stance: takes the argument seriously enough to assign it a real probability,
  and takes his own uncertainty just as seriously ("But I'm very uncertain").
- notice: shows the reader the joints of the argument, where a premise could fail
  and how much rides on it.
- diction: measured, non-absolute, exact about scope; avoids both alarm words and
  reassurance words.
- reader: treats the reader as a fellow reasoner walked through a structure, free
  to reject any step.
- the important move: he quantifies to cool the temperature. Turning a dread into
  a numbered, conditional estimate makes it discussable instead of overwhelming.

## Holden Karnofsky, "AI Could Defeat All Of Us Combined"
Source: https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/
Craft:
- cadence: short, flat declaratives that refuse to dramatize; the plainness is
  the discipline.
- argument: concedes the strongest counterargument first ("even if we assume AIs
  will basically have similar capabilities to humans"), then shows the claim
  survives the concession.
- evidence: quarantines the speculative parts by name ("this is necessarily
  speculative") and grounds the rest in concrete scale and human precedent.
- stance: alarmed conclusion, un-alarmed voice; the calm is deliberate, not
  detachment.
- notice: makes an abstract threat legible by counting it (copies numbered
  against a real workforce) and by analogy to civil wars and revolutions.
- diction: everyday words, understated where a lesser writer would reach for a
  grand one.
- reader: anticipates the reader's dismissal and meets it before it forms.
- the important move: he separates what he is confident about from what he is
  guessing, explicitly, so the confident core is not dragged down by the guesses
  around it.

## Scott Alexander, "Why I Am Not (As Much Of) A Doomer (As Some People)"
Source: https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/why-i-am-not-as-much-of-a-doomer
Craft:
- cadence: conversational and quick, but the structure underneath is strict:
  full case, then full reply.
- argument: reconstructs the pessimistic case charitably and completely before
  pushing on it, so the pushback lands against the real thing.
- evidence: draws a clean line between what recent systems have actually shown
  and what is extrapolation about smarter ones; treats those as different kinds
  of claim.
- stance: refuses both tribes; normalizes disagreement by setting a range of
  serious estimates beside each other rather than picking a side to perform.
- notice: names his own back-and-forth openly ("I go back and forth more than I
  can really justify") instead of hiding the wobble.
- diction: plain and unpretentious, willing to be concrete and slightly blunt.
- reader: trusts the reader to sit with an unresolved question and not need it
  closed.
- the important move: he holds optimism and pessimism to the same evidentiary
  standard, so neither side gets a free pass for being the louder mood.
