# Draft handoff: what-could-go-wrong/technological-unemployment (01)

## Original-work statement

The article takes the single 47% figure the debate travels on and threads it
through five studies to pull apart four measurements the public argument
routinely fuses into one — occupation-level automation risk (Frey-Osborne 47%),
task-level automatability (OECD 9%), task exposure (Eloundou), and the measured
real-system effects (Brynjolfsson's +14% productivity, Hui's -2%/-5.2% demand) —
making visible that none of them is a count of jobs actually lost and that both
the alarm (Amodei) and the dismissal (ITIF) run past that gap.
