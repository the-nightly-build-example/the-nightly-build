# Editorial review: what-could-go-wrong/liars-dividend (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: the deepfake alarm holds two separate worries that the evidence rates
differently. The "flood of fakes swings elections" worry did not measurably
arrive in 2024; the "liar's dividend" worry is turning up in real disputes, yet
the one controlled test of whether it works found it pays off against text
scandals and largely fails against video, the very material its authors feared
most. The piece states this clearly and I could recover it from the draft alone.

The claims it stands on, and how each held:

- Chesney and Citron named the liar's dividend in 2019 and predicted it bites
  hardest on genuine audio and video. Opened s4 (California Law Review); the
  page loads as the named article by both authors and carries the "flows,
  perversely, in proportion to success in educating the public" sentence
  verbatim, in the section the locator names. The "genuine video or audio
  evidence" framing is in the evidence record's quotes. Holds.
- The 2024 record undercuts the flood-of-fakes fear. Opened s5 (Knight); it
  loads as Kapoor and Narayanan's "We Looked at 78 Election Deepfakes," and
  confirms 78 instances, 39 with no deceptive intent, 19 of 22 candidate uses
  aimed at improving materials, the undercount caveat verbatim, and that they
  name the liar's dividend as the more real risk. Holds. I pushed on the
  article's universal "No study of the year, theirs included, found a single AI
  fake that measurably moved a result." The researcher read only Knight fully
  (CETaS gated), but the evidence record frames the field as finding no measured
  effect and no measured swing has surfaced; the claim sits within the record.
  Holds, not routed.
- The move has reached a courtroom (Huang v. Tesla). Opened s6 (Fortune); it
  loads as the named report, quotes Judge Pennypacker's "deeply troubling" and
  the "because Mr. Musk is famous" line, and confirms the three-hour
  authentication deposition over the 2016 Autopilot statements. Holds.
- The Trump/Harris photo case is a looser fit. Opened s7 (PolitiFact); loads as
  the fact-check, confirms the ~15,000 campaign figure, Hany Farid finding no AI
  generation, and the "Pants on Fire" rating. The article correctly ranks it
  below Tesla because the image was a rival's crowd, not the speaker's own
  recorded conduct. Holds.
- The move predates deepfakes. Opened s8 (Brennan Center); loads as Goldstein
  and Lohn's report, confirms the Dastis "fake photos" (2017) and Access
  Hollywood "not authentic" instances and the provenance/norms recommendations.
  The article attributes these to the Brennan Center's review rather than to the
  underlying records, which is the honest move the evidence record asked for
  (those older cases were read only inside this report). Holds.
- The sharpest complication: the only controlled test found the tactic weakest
  on video. Opened s9 (APSR); loads as Schiff, Schiff & Bueno's study (five
  pre-registered experiments, 15,000+ participants), and carries the "primarily
  (if not exclusively) when used against scandals reported in text format"
  quote and the text-scandal support shift. Holds, and the article foregrounds
  it in its own section and table rather than burying it.

Display text, descriptor by descriptor: headline verified against s6 (Tesla's
lawyers did argue the recordings could be deepfakes). Every named person's role
checks (Ovadya a technologist; Chesney and Citron law professors; Kapoor and
Narayanan at Princeton; Schiff, Schiff and Bueno the study's authors; Judge
Evette Pennypacker; Hany Farid a forensics researcher). One display-text label
was wrong and I fixed it: the section heading called the 78 WIRED-catalogued
items "election fakes," but 39 of them carried no intent to deceive, so
"fakes" mislabels the set the section then dismantles. Changed to "78 catalogued
AI uses."

data-nb-kind audit: all nine labels are correct against the primary/secondary
test. Ovadya's own HBR and MIT TR pieces and Chesney/Citron, Kapoor/Narayanan,
and the APSR study are primary; the BuzzFeed profile (carries Ovadya's quotes),
Fortune (retells the court order), PolitiFact (reports Trump's own act), and the
Brennan Center (reports the concept and older cases) are secondary. Five primary,
four secondary, nine total, meeting series policy. No source the evidence marked
inaccessible (Ovadya WaPo/Medium, the live WIRED tracker, CETaS/Turing) is cited
as read; WIRED appears only as the dataset behind Knight. No company is named as
an authority.

Every citation href opened as printed and landed on the source itself, not an
endpoint. Two source-list titles differ from the publisher's current on-page
headline but match the stable URL slug and the correct document: s3 (MIT TR now
displays "Making deepfake tools doesn't have to be irresponsible. Here's how.";
slug and cite read "Ethical Deepfake Tools: A Manifesto") and s7 (PolitiFact's
H1 reads "Donald Trump is wrong. These images ... are corroborated, not AI.";
cite tracks the slug). Both resolve to the right article and support the claim
exactly; I left the titles as the slug-backed forms rather than guess at a
re-headline. Noted, not blocking.

The 7x cheap-fakes figure is attributed twice over (to the News Literacy Project,
and "I am reading it secondhand"), never asserted as primary, as the brief
required.

## Cut

Slop pass, every sentence including display text and furniture prose: the draft
is clean. No empty conclusions, no vague attribution, no decorative-analysis
copulas, no self-reference outside the two bookends the template allows, and the
bookends say something specific to this lesson. No em-dashes, no semicolons, no
comma splices; punctuation is plain throughout. Negative-parallelism
constructions ("not warning about a particular fake ... warning that the tools";
"not AI at all but cheap fakes"; "a change of degree rather than a new move";
"the move is being tried is not the move working") each correct a real, named
misconception and carry a reasoning step, so they stay.

Edge and delete pass turned up four weak sentences; I cut or recast all four:

- Section-2 opener "2024 was the year to watch" was a signpost that graded the
  year without adding a fact; the concrete "Dozens of countries held national
  elections in 2024" now opens, with the year folded in.
- "their conclusion deserves to be met at full strength" described the article's
  own method (that it will steelman) rather than stating anything; cut. The
  steelman still stands in the following sentences and the position card.
- "The result that matters here is where the denial paid off" self-graded which
  result matters; recast to "The payoff turned on the scandal's format," which
  states the finding the next two sentences substantiate.
- "Read the table against the theory and the contradiction is plain" was an
  instruction-plus-self-grade; deleted, letting the concrete "Chesney and Citron
  feared the dividend most for genuine audio and video" open the paragraph.

Formula check against the recent-pattern notes: the dek reproduced the exact mold
the brief and commission flagged, "The [worry] that [people] named in [YEAR] ...
even as [counter] never arrived." I rewrote it in the piece's own terms, dropping
the "named in YEAR" construction and the reversal, and foregrounding the article's
sharpest and most distinctive finding (the text/video asymmetry) rather than the
flood-of-fakes contrast the mold leaned on. New dek: "The denial that a real
recording is a deepfake is already being tried in court and campaigns, and the
only experiment to test it found it works on text scandals but largely fails on
video, the very evidence it was meant to discredit." Updated in both the dekline
and nb-meta so they match. Section headings are varied in construction, none is a
bare reversal or a scaffolding slot, and none copies a prior article's shape. No
house catchphrase.

Leakage check against the commission, briefs, and voice guide: none. The two-worry
split and the "attempted vs works" distinction are the article's own analytical
content, grounded in the evidence, not lifted planning labels. No exemplar
phrasing from the Cegłowski, Alexander, or Luu quotations appears in the draft.

Furniture: the note (defining quote), stat strip (the three carrying numbers),
position card (the skeptics' stance), and table (the text/video split) each earn
their place; the piece still reads as continuous prose, not a stack of blocks. No
component is decorative and none is a missed opportunity, the table being exactly
the visual the crux needs.

## Reader

Read straight through as the paper's declared reader, I leave able to state both
worries in the form their holders hold them and to say what the best evidence
shows about each, which is what the commission asked. What I have that the sources
alone would not give me: the collision the article stages between Chesney and
Citron's prediction that the dividend bites hardest on video and the one
experiment that found it works there least, with the Tesla and Trump episodes
reclassified as the move attempted rather than the move proven. No single source
does that assembly. The draft-handoff's original-work sentence claims exactly
this, and the article delivers it, so the piece is not a restatement of its
sources. The prose sits with the voice-guide exemplars, not a median summary:
claims stated flatly, the two readings set side by side at real strength before
either is weighed, and the conclusion hedged to the size of the evidence. The
headline, read as the largest claim, is true and defended in the body.
