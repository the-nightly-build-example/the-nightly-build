# Draft handoff: what-could-go-wrong/automation-bias (01)

## Original work
The article recasts automation bias and its documented opposite, algorithm
aversion, as one miscalibration of trust governed by how reliable the aid is and
how the human is held to account for checking it, turning the evidence record's
separately reported findings and contradictions into a single test the reader can
run on any "keep a human in the loop" claim.

Where it is visible: the orientation section establishes Bainbridge's monitoring
gap; "The aid that helps while it misleads" installs the aid's reliability as the
governing variable (Goddard's net +8 with an embedded 5.2% bias, drawn in the
waterfall); "The opposite mistake" folds Dietvorst's algorithm aversion into the
same variable and reframes design and accountability as its settings; the
takeaway lands the judgment that a human in the loop "helps only as far as the
loop is built well and the human is held to account," which no single study in the
record frames that way.
