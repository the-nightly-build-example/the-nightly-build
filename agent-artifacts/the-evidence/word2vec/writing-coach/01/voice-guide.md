# Voice guide: the-evidence/word2vec (01)

## Directive

Write for a reader who arrived believing the legend. They have seen
`king − man + woman = queen` presented as proof that the vectors "understand"
meaning, and they trusted it because smart people repeated it. The article owes
two things to every sentence at once: full credit to what the 2013 work actually
measured, and an honest account of what the folklore added on top. Neither
adoring nor debunking. The register is a patient teacher who respects both the
paper and the reader too much to let a good story stand in for the mechanism.

Three moves change sentences here, beyond the house default:

**Deflate by showing the mechanism, not by announcing a verdict.** Where the
default voice would write "this is a myth," put the operation in front of the
reader and let the legend shrink in their own head: the arithmetic that returns
the input word, the step that quietly excludes it, the nearest-neighbor lookup
underneath the "reasoning." Name the deflation only after the reader has watched
it happen. A sentence that tells the reader the result is overstated, before it
has shown them why, is doing the writer's job for them.

**Credit lands before the correction, and it is specific.** Say plainly what the
method achieved and why it stunned people in 2013, in its own terms, before
pressing on what got oversold. The correction reads as fair only when the praise
was real. Vague generosity ("an important contribution") is not credit; it is a
throat-clear before the "but."

**Keep the temperature low.** The claim is wrong in places; the people who
repeated it were not fools. Correct the claim, never the believer. No "actually,"
no gotcha, no reveal staged as a punchline. The strongest version of this article
would let a reader who once loved the legend finish it feeling smarter, not
scolded.

Cadence: state the finding flat, then excavate. After a dense mechanical
passage, a short sentence to reset. Vary the shape so the corrections do not all
arrive in the same mold.

## Licenses

form: second-person direct address ("you")
move: Evans and Speer hand the reader the operation and let them run it, so the
  conclusion is something the reader reached, not something they were told.
bar:  the sentence puts the reader through a specific step or observation that
  advances the mechanism ("subtract the vector and you land back on king"). Cut
  it where it only builds rapport or addresses the reader for warmth.

form: the diagnostic question opening a paragraph
move: Evans poses the exact question the next few sentences answer ("So what's
  going on?"), marking a real turn in the investigation.
bar:  the same paragraph delivers the answer, and the question names a genuine
  pivot in the argument. A question used as a section-topic label, or one the
  paragraph does not resolve, fails.

form: naming the legend's genuine pull before deflating it
move: Luu concedes real failures and Speer credits "the cool part" before
  pressing, so the correction cannot be read as a takedown.
bar:  the concession states one specific, true thing the work earned, and the
  deflation that follows rests on cited measurement, not on the concession's
  tone. A concession that praises in generalities, or that exists only to set up
  the reversal, is not admitted.

## Julia Evans, "Why is DNS still hard to learn?"
Source: https://jvns.ca/blog/2023/07/28/why-is-dns-still-hard-to-learn/
Craft:
- cadence: short sentences puncture dense technical passages and reset attention;
  a hard question or a four-word line follows a long explanatory chain.
- argument: diagnosis before prescription. She names why the thing confuses
  people, layer by layer, before proposing anything.
- evidence: shows rather than asserts. She displays the actual confusing tool
  output instead of describing it as confusing, and grounds claims in lived
  specifics ("it took me probably 5 years to realize").
- stance: fellow sufferer, not expert on a podium. She refuses to dumb things
  down while admitting the difficulty is real.
- notice: interface friction and hard-won folk knowledge, the small frictions an
  expert has stopped seeing.
- diction: casual but precise; technical terms enter without fanfare, assuming
  competence.
- reader: highly dialogic, direct questions and shared experience, reader treated
  as a peer working the problem alongside her.
- the important move: she legitimizes the reader's confusion as the subject's
  fault, not the reader's, which buys permission to go slow without condescending.

## Dan Luu, "Steve Ballmer was an underrated CEO"
Source: https://danluu.com/ballmer/
Craft:
- cadence: flat declarative claim, then a long excavation of the evidence.
  Claim-then-dig mirrors folklore-contradicted-by-record.
- argument: establish the received wisdom, show where it came from, then lay the
  ledger. He concedes the real failures before turning the numbers.
- evidence: specific figures used to reframe, not decorate. A "failure" becomes a
  business most companies would celebrate once the actual numbers are on the page.
- stance: scrupulously fair. He grants the criticisms their due and presses only
  as far as the evidence carries, admitting his own wrong bet against the subject.
- notice: the asymmetry in how the same behavior gets judged depending on who
  does it; the gap between reputation and record.
- diction: vernacular dismissal and financial precision in the same paragraph,
  fluent in both the folklore's language and the evidence's.
- reader: assumes sophistication without arrogance; teaches the portfolio logic
  rather than preaching the conclusion.
- the important move: he undercuts his own authority (the losing bet) so the
  reassessment reads as shared correction, not the writer knowing better.

## Simon Willison, "Catching up on the weird world of LLMs"
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2023/Aug/3/weird-world-of-llms/
Craft:
- cadence: alternates a bare assertion ("It's a file") with the longer
  justification that follows, breaking complex ideas into separate thoughts.
- argument: calibration over advocacy. He separates what excites him from what he
  is certain of, and states limitations head-on rather than burying them.
- evidence: lived, concrete, checkable. Real output, real failure, the specific
  supply chain, never an abstract claim where a demonstration is available.
- stance: curious but wary, the anthropologist's voice; genuinely strange things
  get careful observation, neither hype nor dismissal.
- notice: where transparency fails and where a system's behavior is stranger than
  its marketing admits.
- diction: vernacular ("the dirty little secret") that lowers the barrier while
  the rigor stays.
- reader: fellow explorer invited to test and to doubt, not a pupil receiving
  conclusions.
- the important move: he lets an impressive result and its named failure sit in
  the same breath, so wonder and skepticism arrive together instead of competing.

## Rob Speer, "Cramming for the test set: We need better ways to evaluate analogies"
Source: https://blog.conceptnet.io/posts/2016/cramming-for-the-test-set-we-need-better-ways-to-evaluate-analogies/
Craft:
- cadence: conversational rhythm, short declaratives alternating with longer
  explanation; punchy transitions mark each move in the dismantling.
- argument: methodical. Credit the genuine achievement, then raise expectations
  of it, then use the evaluation's own numbers to show where the story thins.
- evidence: performance figures deployed to reveal a flaw rather than to
  celebrate a score; the numbers turn against the legend that cited them.
- stance: fair-handed. He credits the original work and applies pressure
  selectively, hardest on the evaluation, gentlest on the authors, deflating
  without attacking.
- notice: what the test actually measures versus what people assume it measures;
  the questions that "involve nothing about the meanings of the words."
- diction: sophisticated but plain; a light metaphor ("teaching to the test")
  anchored in the subject's own terms, never a pun on the abstraction.
- reader: collegial, addressed as a fellow skeptic ("you might wonder"), never
  condescended to.
- the important move: he separates the method's real capability from the
  benchmark's inflated reputation, so the reader learns to distrust the folklore
  without distrusting the underlying work. This is the exact stance this article
  needs.
