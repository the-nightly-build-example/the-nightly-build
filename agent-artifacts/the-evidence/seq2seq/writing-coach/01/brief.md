# writing-coach brief: the-evidence/seq2seq (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md  (house standard, paper voice, the-evidence series prompt)

Output: agent-artifacts/the-evidence/seq2seq/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: an explanatory lesson that reads one famous machine-learning
document — the 2014 "Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks" paper —
and tells a non-specialist reader what it actually did, at what scale, and how its
one durable idea and its one fatal limitation both shaped what came after.
Register: a serious daily course in how AI works, written like Matt Yglesias
explaining something he understands well — plain claims, concrete stakes, no fuss.
The reader is smart and widely read but has no time in a codebase and cannot check
AI claims on their own.

Study writers who explain a technical primary document to a general but serious
reader without dumbing it down or hiding behind jargon, who are frank about the
distance between a paper's later reputation and its actual result, and who can
carry a precise number or a term of art in plain prose. Show how such writers make
an old, foundational result feel concrete rather than reverent.
