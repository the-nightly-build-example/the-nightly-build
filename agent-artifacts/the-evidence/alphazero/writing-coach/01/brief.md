# writing-coach brief: the-evidence/alphazero (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (at the artifact root) — house standard, paper voice, and The Evidence series prompt.

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: a teaching lesson that reads one famous AI document, the
AlphaZero paper (DeepMind, Science 2018), and explains to a smart non-programmer
what it actually reported versus how it is cited. The domain is machine-learning
research explained for a lay-but-literate reader. The register is a serious daily
course, plain and concrete, in the mould of a good explanatory technologist or
science writer, never breezy or hyped.

Study writers the field rates who explain hard machine-learning or
quantitative-science results to non-specialists without dumbing them down or
inflating them. Show how they state a strong result and its caveats in the same
breath, how they handle a contested benchmark or a match with fine print, and how
they keep numbers honest. Do not use passages that carry AlphaZero's own findings;
if an exemplar covers AlphaZero or AlphaGo, quote only passages that show the
writing, not the subject.

This piece must land a gap between a real result and its loose citation without
sneering and without hedging into mush. Passages that show a writer holding "this
is genuinely impressive" and "here is exactly what it does not prove" together are
the most useful.
