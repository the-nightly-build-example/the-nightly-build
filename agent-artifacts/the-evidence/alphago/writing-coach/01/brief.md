## Domain and genre
Explaining a landmark machine-learning result (deep reinforcement learning +
tree search that mastered the game of Go) to a smart, widely-read reader who
does not build systems. Genre: the technical explainer that teaches a mechanism
honestly without hype and without talking down. Study writers the field itself
rates for exactly this: clear expository writers on deep learning / RL / search
(for example the tradition of Karpathy-style walkthroughs, Distill/Chris-Olah
visual explanation, Michael Nielsen, or strong long-form science explainers who
handle games and search). Pick your own ≥3 exemplars; prefer the primary piece
over commentary; skip influencer/SEO content and skip company blogs used as
authorities.

## What the voice guide must solve for this article
- Teaching an abstraction (Monte Carlo Tree Search; policy vs value network)
  through a small concrete worked example without a diagram or code.
- Holding a plain, committed register while correcting a popular
  overstatement (AlphaGo "beat the world champion with no human input"): the
  correction must land through fact, not through a "not X but Y" reflex.
- Handling numbers (games, positions, win rates, dates, hardware) so each gets
  a comparison the reader already holds.
- Keeping "no hype, no doom": grand words only after the argument earns them.

## Recently used, do not reuse
- Opener mold "The number X published about itself" (used twice this week).
- Comma-triad headings and deks; semicolon-reversal and suspended-question deks.
- Any reusable closer formula or house catchphrase.

Extract transferable craft only — cadence, how to stage a worked example, how
to correct a myth cleanly — never a named writer's persona or lines. Calibration
passages stay in the guide.
