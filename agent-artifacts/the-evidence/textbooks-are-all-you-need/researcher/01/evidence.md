# Evidence: the-evidence/textbooks-are-all-you-need (01)

The evidence strongly supports the commissioned angle. The phi-1 paper is a real,
narrow result that has been carried into a broad slogan. Verified from the paper
itself: phi-1 is a 1.3B-parameter Python-only code model trained on ~7B tokens
(4 days, 8 A100s) that scores 50.6% pass@1 on HumanEval (164 hand-written
problems) and 55.5% on MBPP, matching or beating code models 10-12x its size such
as StarCoder (15.5B, ~1T tokens). What the paper actually demonstrates is
narrower than "data quality substitutes for scale," and the paper's own Section 5
is where this shows: at the strictest similarity threshold, 71 of 164 HumanEval
problems have a close match in phi-1's synthetic training exercises, and once
phi-1 is retrained with those matches removed and evaluated only on the
non-similar HumanEval problems, its edge over the 12x-larger StarCoder collapses
to a few points and reverses at the most aggressive pruning. Two things stay
firm and support the angle: the training data was generated by GPT-3.5 and
filtered by GPT-4, so the "small model" is distilling a much larger one (Microsoft
concedes this in the phi-4 report), and the whole result lives inside a single
language on two small benchmarks. Where the evidence is thin: the "quality beats
scale" contamination concern is best documented for later phi models (phi-1.5,
phi-2, phi-3 on math benchmarks via GSM1k), not phi-1's HumanEval directly, so the
strongest independent overfitting evidence is adjacent to phi-1 rather than aimed
at it. Nothing found undermines the angle; the paper's own numbers carry it.

## Contradictions

- **The authors' decontamination claim vs. their own Table 3.** The phi-1 paper
  concludes that pruning proves the score is "not due to dataset contamination."
  But Table 3 shows the honest comparison is much weaker than the conclusion
  implies. At the strictest threshold (τ=0.95), 71 of 164 HumanEval problems are
  flagged as similar to a training exercise. On the *non-similar* HumanEval
  problems — the fair test — the retrained phi-1 scores 32.3% versus StarCoder-
  Prompted's 29.0% (a ~3-point edge, not the ~17-point headline gap of 50.6 vs
  33.6). At the most aggressive pruning (τ=0.8), the retrained phi-1 scores 27.1%
  on the non-similar set and StarCoder scores 31.2% — StarCoder *wins*. So the
  paper's own strongest-scrutiny numbers show phi-1 roughly matching, not
  dominating, a model 12x its size once the benchmark-resembling exercises are
  removed. The authors' "by a large margin" language rests on overall retrained
  scores, not the non-similar subset.

- **"Quality substitutes for scale" vs. distillation.** phi-1's textbooks and
  exercises were generated by GPT-3.5 and filtered by GPT-4 labels. Microsoft's
  own phi-4 report later states the earlier phi models "largely distill the
  capabilities of a teacher model (specifically GPT-4)." So the small model's
  competence partly reflects a much larger model's scale relocated into the
  training data, which cuts against reading phi-1 as scale-free.

- **Scope of the overfitting evidence.** The independent overfitting signal
  (GSM1k, Maini) is measured on phi-1.5 / phi-2 / phi-3 math and language tasks,
  not on phi-1's HumanEval. It confirms the *family's* benchmark numbers deserve
  caution but does not directly measure phi-1 code contamination beyond the
  paper's own Table 3. The article should not present GSM1k as a verdict on phi-1
  specifically.

- **Magnitude of the phi-family drop is modest.** GSM1k shows phi models drop
  4-6 points, and the worst offenders are other models (Yi, math-shepherd), not
  phi. So the honest reading is "systematic but moderate overfitting," not fraud.
