## The document

"Textbooks Are All You Need," Suriya Gunasekar and colleagues at Microsoft
Research, arXiv:2306.11644, June 2023. It introduced phi-1, a 1.3-billion-
parameter Transformer trained for Python code on roughly 7 billion tokens: web
code filtered for "textbook quality" by a classifier the authors trained on
GPT-4 labels, plus synthetic textbook-style data generated by GPT-3.5, then a
small synthetic exercise set for finetuning. phi-1 reached a reported 50.6%
pass@1 on HumanEval, competitive with far larger models trained on far more
data. The paper is cited to argue data quality can stand in for scale.

## Angle

Teach what "textbook-quality data" actually was as a procedure, show the real
numbers and the honest scale of the result, and separate the demonstrated
finding from the slogan the title became. The finding the paper earned: a small
model on curated and synthetic data matched much larger models on two Python
coding benchmarks. The claim it is used for: data is all you need, quality beats
scale in general. The gap between those is the lesson. Give the reader the
decontamination check the authors ran against test-set leakage, and where later
readers doubted it, because a benchmark result built partly on data distilled
from GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 invites exactly that doubt.

The desk's discipline applies: show the size of the foundation. One narrow domain
(Python), two benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP), a 1.3B model, about 7B training
tokens. State plainly where today's "quality over scale" usage runs past what
the paper measured.
