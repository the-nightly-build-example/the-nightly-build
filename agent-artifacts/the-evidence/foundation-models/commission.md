# Commission: the-evidence/foundation-models

## The document

"On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models," Rishi Bommasani, Percy
Liang, and a large group of Stanford Center for Research on Foundation Models
(CRFM) authors, 2021 (arXiv:2108.07258). The report that coined the term
"foundation model" and pushed it into universal use. The reader meets the phrase
constantly and does not know it was named, deliberately, by one 2021 document
with a stake in the naming.

## Angle

Read the document for what it actually is: not an experiment with a result, but a
sweeping ~200-page position paper by a large author list that proposed a new name
for large pretrained models and argued they are a paradigm. Show the scale
honestly the way this desk does: how many authors and pages, what the report
actually contains (a taxonomy and a research agenda, not a measured finding), and
that its central act was an act of naming and framing, not of measurement. Then
bring it to the present: the term won and is now everywhere, and the naming itself
drew a sharp, well-argued objection at the time (that "foundation" smuggles in a
claim these models are a settled base to build on, from the very institution
proposing to study them). State plainly where the document's influence rests on
evidence and where it rests on having named the thing first.

The reader should leave able to separate a research finding from a successful act
of framing, and to ask of any influential AI document what it actually
established versus what it merely named.

## What it teaches (short, complete)

1. What the report is and did: named "foundation models," from a large CRFM
   author list, as a taxonomy-and-agenda position paper rather than an
   experiment. Give the concrete scale (authors, length, what is and isn't in it).
2. Why naming is power: the coinage shaped the field's language and funding, and
   the choice of the word "foundation" carried an argument. Work one concrete
   example of the framing doing work.
3. The contemporaneous objection to the name and framing, at full strength, and
   what has and hasn't held up as the term became standard.

## Boundaries

- Not a takedown of the researchers or of large models. Report what the document
  did; let the gap between "named a paradigm" and "proved a paradigm" carry the
  judgment. No hype, no doom.
- Claims about the report come from the report itself, read directly, and its
