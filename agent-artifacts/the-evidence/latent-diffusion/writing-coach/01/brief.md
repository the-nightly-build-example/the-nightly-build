# writing-coach brief: the-evidence/latent-diffusion (01)

Assignment: a lesson (explanatory teaching, not reportage) for The Evidence desk
on the Latent Diffusion Models paper (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) that became
Stable Diffusion. The reader is smart and widely read but new to how diffusion
image models work. The piece walks through what the paper did (moved diffusion
into a compressed latent space to make high-resolution image generation cheap),
its scale, and how its findings hold up today. Register: plain, concrete,
explains machine-learning ideas without hype, in the spirit of press/editorial's
"write like Matt Yglesias explaining something he understands well."

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (house standard, slop and headline rules, press voice,
  lesson identity, The Evidence prompt)

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Study writers who explain machine learning and image generation clearly to a
general-but-serious reader (the field rates them for clarity, not reach). Pull
passages that show how to make a compression-and-reconstruction idea concrete
without a diagram and without code. Avoid exemplars whose passages would hand the
writer this paper's own findings; quote for voice, not for substance.
