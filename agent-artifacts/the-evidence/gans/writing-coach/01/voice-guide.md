# Voice guide: the-evidence/gans

## Directive

Write as a teacher who has the actual 1406.2661 PDF open and is reading it
aloud with the reader, sizing each claim the moment it appears. The house
voice is already plain, concrete, and unhyped; this lesson adds one demand on
top of that. It has to hold two sizes of the same thing in view at once, the
small object the paper actually produced and the large reputation the object
now carries, and let the reader feel the distance between them without the
prose ever announcing the distance. The register that does this is exact, not
knowing. Report what is literally on the page, in the page's own terms
translated to plain words, and trust the plainness to do the sizing.

The reader relationship is a specific trust: this reader has met the GAN
reputation everywhere and has never seen what the paper contains. They are
trusting you to have opened it. Reward that by paying out the concrete facts a
person who only read coverage could not have, the resolution, the metric's
actual name, the paper's own admissions, and by naming them where the pull is
strongest to reach instead for the grandeur the reputation supplies.

Three moves will change sentences here.

Grant the achievement in full before you size it. The exemplars who deflate a
claim fairly first state, generously and without reservation, what the work
genuinely did. Do that before any sentence measures the gap, so the measuring
reads as fairness and not as a takedown. A lesson that sizes fame against
evidence has to be visibly on the paper's side first.

When the clean result and the messy reality diverge, state each at full
strength in turn. Give the idealized claim its whole force, on its own terms,
in one place. Then give the practical failure its whole force in the next.
Never soften either to reconcile them; the divergence is the thing being
taught, and a sentence that blurs the two halves has taught nothing.

Size every magnitude against something the reader already holds in their body,
a screen size, a thumbnail, a distance they can see. This is the house rule on
numbers pressed harder than usual, because here the numbers are the whole point
and a bare figure that the reader cannot feel will let the reputation refill
the space.

## Licenses

form: deadpan understatement that sizes a claim
move: Mitchell and Hartnett let a flat, factual sentence land the gap between a
  claim and its evidence, with no adjective and no signalling "but" carrying the
  irony; the fact's own plainness deflates.
bar:  the sentence states a verifiable fact whose flatness alone does the work.
  If it needs a wry adjective, a scare quote, or a contrast clause to signal
  that it is being dry, it is a punchline and gets cut.

form: direct address that seats the reader in front of a concrete artifact
move: Hartnett writes "Imagine you're given a very brief glimpse" to stand the
  reader inside the experiment before any mechanism is named, looking at the
  actual object.
bar:  the address points at one specific, described thing the reader could have
  seen on a 2014 screen or in the paper, described in words. It never narrates
  the reader's reaction ("you'll be surprised") and never gestures at a generic
  hypothetical reader; cut it if you could not replace "you" with a named object
  and keep the sentence true.

## Ferenc Huszár, "How to Train your Generative Models? And why does Adversarial Training work so well?"
Source: https://www.inference.vc/how-to-train-your-generative-models-why-generative-adversarial-networks-work-so-well-2/
Craft:
- cadence: alternates a formal statement of a result with a colloquial line
  that unpacks it; lets the math settle before translating it, so each
  technical claim gets a plain-English echo in the next breath.
- argument: principle, then the failure of the obvious approach, then the
  mechanism that fixes it. Builds the reader's footing one step at a time and
  never uses a step before laying it.
- evidence: separates what holds in the limit from what holds under finite data
  and a wrong model, and says which regime every real use lives in. Grants a
  guarantee its full theoretical strength, then names exactly where it stops
  applying.
- stance: tempered toward its own subject; closes admitting the method "is still
  in its infancy and has many issues and limitations" while genuinely excited by
  the idea. Interest and skepticism sit in the same sentence.
- notice: the distinction between how a model will be used and how it is trained,
  a difference most writing on the topic elides, becomes the whole organizing
  spine.
- diction: names the exact object ("KL[P|Q] is liberal, KL[Q|P] is
  conservative") rather than a vague magnitude; reaches for the plain adjective
  attached to the precise quantity.
- reader: treats the reader as capable of the real idea but owed the plain
  version of it; explains without condescending and without hiding the math.
- the important move the axes missed: he earns the right to be skeptical of a
  method by first demonstrating he understands why it works better than the
  alternatives. Credibility to deflate comes from visible fairness first.

## Melanie Mitchell, "Magical Thinking on AI"
Source: https://aiguide.substack.com/p/magical-thinking-on-ai
Craft:
- cadence: forensic and even, alternating conversational accessibility with
  technical specificity; builds trust by never condescending and never inflating.
- argument: quotes the inflated claim generously and in full, then dismantles it
  by asking what the evidence for it actually is and going to the source to
  check. The question invites the reader to demand better rather than asserting
  a verdict at them.
- evidence: fact-checks a specific public claim against the primary technical
  document it distorts, linking the original. Trusts evidence over authority and
  shows the reader how to do the same.
- stance: skeptical of the narrative, not of the reality; names hype as hype
  without dismissing the genuine stakes underneath it.
- notice: where a confident public claim quietly lacks a foundation, and makes
  the absence of evidence the subject.
- diction: precise without pedantry; the specific, checkable detail carries the
  paragraph rather than a strong adjective.
- reader: respectful skeptics who deserve better information than either hype or
  dismissal; agency stays with the reader to weigh the evidence.
- the important move the axes missed: she quotes the overreaching claim at its
  strongest before touching it, so the correction reads as fairness to the
  claim, not hostility to the claimant.

## Kevin Hartnett, "Machine Learning Confronts the Elephant in the Room" (Quanta Magazine)
Source: https://www.quantamagazine.org/machine-learning-confronts-the-elephant-in-the-room-20180920/
Craft:
- cadence: short declaratives that build momentum, then a longer expository
  passage that decelerates and lets complexity settle; punchiness and patience
  in alternation, tuned for a reader new to neural networks.
- argument: concrete failure first, then the mechanism that explains it, then
  why it matters. Delays every technical term until the reader already holds the
  stakes it belongs to.
- evidence: reports the actual experiment in vivid specifics, the described image,
  the successive misidentifications, without burying the reader in data; shows
  the finding rather than characterizing it.
- stance: measured; frames a limitation as a vulnerability to understand, not as
  a disproof, and quotes the deflating line ("'deep learning' isn't really that
  deep") rather than editorializing it.
- notice: the single relatable failure point becomes a narrative anchor the piece
  returns to, so an abstract mechanism stays attached to one image the reader can
  picture.
- diction: plain, physical words; the concrete scene ("a man seated on the edge
  of a shabby chair") does the work an abstraction would have asked the reader to
  carry.
- reader: a smart non-specialist met at eye level; stakes are made legible before
  jargon, never after.
- the important move the axes missed: an ordinary human experience opens the
  piece and grounds the whole technical account, so the reader measures the
  machine against something they already know how to do.

## Self-test

Following only the house default, a writer would produce plain, cited, unhyped
prose about the GAN paper. This guide asks for something the default does not
supply: a lesson built to hold the paper's small 2014 object and its large 2026
reputation in the same frame and let flat, exact facts carry the distance, while
staying visibly fair to the paper before it sizes it. The deadpan-understatement
license and the concrete-artifact address are the two forms that let the writer
land that distance without reaching for the emphasis the house register bans.
