This series pins no voice guide, so study the domain fresh. The article tells one
real AI failure (an automated hiring-screening system at the center of a
discrimination lawsuit) and teaches why such systems fail, for a smart reader with
no background in employment law or machine learning and no time in a codebase.
Study writers who report a legal or institutional failure precisely and fairly:
people who narrate what happened in order, keep the difference between an
allegation and a finding scrupulously clear, and explain a technical or legal
mechanism to a lay reader without dumbing it down. Prefer accountability
journalists and legal explainers the field respects. The craft problem specific to
this piece: the core discrimination is still unproven and being litigated, so the
voice must carry real stakes without convicting anyone, and must make a dry legal
holding (a software vendor counting as an employer's "agent") land as the genuine
surprise it is. Find passages that stay cold and exact about who did what while
still holding the reader.
