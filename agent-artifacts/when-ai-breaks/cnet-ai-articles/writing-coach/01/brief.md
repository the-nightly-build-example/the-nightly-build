# writing-coach brief: when-ai-breaks/cnet-ai-articles (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md  (house standard, paper voice, the when-ai-breaks series prompt)

Output: agent-artifacts/when-ai-breaks/cnet-ai-articles/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: an explanatory lesson that tells one real AI failure in order
and then explains why that kind of system fails that way. The incident: CNET
quietly published dozens of AI-generated personal-finance articles in 2022-2023,
many of which turned out to carry factual errors, and got caught. The lesson names
people, outlets, and dates, tells the story straight, then teaches why fluent
AI-written prose can be confidently wrong and why fluency hides the errors, and
where the same weakness lives today. Register: a serious daily course in how AI
works, written like Matt Yglesias explaining something he understands well — plain
claims, concrete stakes, no fuss. The reader is smart, widely read, and has no
time in a codebase.

Study writers who narrate a real institutional or media failure with precision and
restraint — accountability reporting and media/press post-mortems — carrying the
facts and dates cleanly, then stepping back to explain the underlying mechanism.
Show how such writers keep a story moving without melodrama and hand a reader a
general lesson without moralizing.
