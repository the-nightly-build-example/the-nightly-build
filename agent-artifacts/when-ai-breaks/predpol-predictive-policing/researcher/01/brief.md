# researcher brief: when-ai-breaks/predpol-predictive-policing (01)

Inputs:
- ../../commission.md — the assignment, order of telling, mechanism, boundaries,
  and the three neighbours this must stay distinct from.
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader.

Output: ./evidence.md

Sourcing floor (nb source-policy): at least 8 sources, at least 4 primary, at
least 1 secondary. Work from the record; get the accuracy figure from the
investigation that owns it.

Primary documents to read and record with locators:
- The Markup (with Gizmodo), 2023 investigation of Geolitica/PredPol predictions.
  Verify the exact accuracy finding (the fraction of predictions that matched a
  later reported crime — record the precise number and how they defined a "hit"),
  the departments and time period analysed, and how they obtained the predictions.
  Read their methodology write-up, not just the headline.
- The founding model paper(s): Mohler et al. (self-exciting point-process / crime
  "aftershock" model; and the LAPD field-trial paper). Verify the origin
  (earthquake-aftershock math repurposed) and what the field trials claimed.
- The feedback-loop papers, read firsthand: Lum & Isaac, "To predict and serve?"
  (Significance, 2016) — verify their demonstration that training on recorded
  (drug) crime would send police back to the same neighbourhoods; and Ensign et
  al., "Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing" (2018) — verify the formal
  result.
- A government/independent audit: e.g. the LAPD Office of the Inspector General
  review of predictive policing (2019). Verify its findings on data quality and
  whether the tool's value could be shown.
- A company/founder statement responding to the accuracy criticism (PredPol/
  Geolitica). Verify what they argued the accuracy number missed.

Secondary (at least one): reporting that held up on adoptions, drops, and bans
(e.g. Santa Cruz's 2020 ban, departments discontinuing use).

Mechanism facts to pin, kept distinct: recorded crime vs. actual crime; why
rare-event base rates make most box-shift predictions wrong; how the patrol
response feeds back into the training data. Classify each source primary/secondary
honestly.

Numbers section: the accuracy/hit-rate figure (with definition, departments,
period), adoption counts, and any audit figures, each with its owning primary.

Contradictions: the accuracy exposé and feedback-loop findings vs. the vendor's/
defenders' rebuttal and any study claiming crime-reduction benefit. Record the
strongest version of each and what would settle it.

Source assets: note any figure/table (e.g. The Markup's hit-rate chart, a
feedback-loop diagram from Lum & Isaac) that could carry the argument. Do not
prescribe crops.

Do not browse the repository for background. Confirm every URL resolves. Flag any
commission claim the record cannot support, and keep the distinction from
chicago-heat-list (person-based), shotspotter (acoustic), and compas (sentencing)
clear in the record.
