# Commission: when-ai-breaks/tessa-eating-disorder-chatbot

## The incident

Tessa, a chatbot the National Eating Disorders Association (NEDA) offered in place
of parts of its human helpline, which in mid-2023 was found giving weight-loss and
dieting advice to people seeking help for eating disorders, and was pulled within
days. One lesson, one incident: a deployed system in a high-stakes support role, a
public failure with a record, and the reason that kind of system fails that way.

## The angle

Tell it in order, explain the mechanism, then bring the weakness to the present.
Handle a sensitive subject strictly from the record; report harm without
sensationalizing and without reproducing specific harmful instructions.

- What the system was built to do and the context: NEDA had a long-running human
  helpline; it moved to wind down the helpline and pointed users to Tessa, a bot
  originally developed by researchers (Ellen Fitzsimmons-Craft and colleagues) and
  operated by a vendor (Cass, formerly X2AI) as a scripted body-image prevention
  tool. Name the people, organizations, and dates precisely, and the labor context
  (helpline staff had recently moved to unionize).
- What it actually did and who it affected: activist Sharon Maxwell and others
  documented Tessa giving advice contraindicated for eating-disorder recovery
  (calorie deficits, measuring body fat, and the like). Establish what was
  documented and by whom. NEDA first disputed, then took Tessa down. Give the
  dates and the sequence.
- Why that kind of system fails that way. Draw the transferable mechanism: a tool
  validated for one narrow purpose (structured prevention exercises) was placed in
  a broader, higher-stakes role (a helpline replacement for acutely vulnerable
  users), where generic wellness advice that is harmless to most is dangerous to
  this population; and automating a role that turned on human judgment removed the
  check that would have caught it. Say plainly what the record establishes about
  whether Tessa's harmful output came from a rules/script change, an added
  generative component, or scope misuse, and where accounts differ, present the
  strongest version of each and what evidence would settle it.
- Where the same weakness lives now: other support, health, and wellness chatbots
  placed in front of vulnerable users, and the general lesson that fitness for one
  narrow task does not transfer to a higher-stakes deployment. Name the closing
  section for its content.

## Required contribution

the same advice that is harmless to most users can be dangerous to the ones a
