## Round focus

Verify the incident is told in order and the mechanism is taught honestly, and that
these evidence-record boundaries survived. This is the read to push hardest on:
- Every FTC-sourced claim is an allegation resolved by a consent order in which
  Rite Aid neither admitted nor denied it. Check every such sentence is framed that
  way, including the 11-year-old (FTC-only). Rite Aid's own position must be present.
- The base-rate arithmetic uses illustrative numbers, because Rite Aid never
  measured its own per-scan false-match rate. Check that any worked figure is
  marked illustrative in the prose (not only a caption), and that NIST thresholds
  (FMR 0.00001, 0.00003) are used as illustrative anchors, not as Rite Aid's
  measured rate.
- The demographic-skew mechanism must be at honest strength: NIST measured large
  false-match differentials in many but not all algorithms, on good-quality photos;
  Rite Aid used low-quality CCTV and unknown algorithms and never measured its own
  by-race error. The honest line is "demographic skew is real and often large, and
  Rite Aid never checked which kind of system it had." Flag any sentence that reads
  as "NIST proved Rite Aid misread [groups] by 10-to-100x."
- If a NIST figure is used as a source asset, presenting Figure 26 (skewed
  algorithms) without Figure 27 (uniform ones) misrepresents the report; check the
  crop and the caption.
Keep reported fact, allegation, and analysis distinct throughout. Check claims about
named people and the company most deeply. Audit every data-nb-kind (FTC is primary
for its charges and order; NIST for its measurements; Rite Aid for its position;
Reuters is the independent secondary). Confirm the three ordered reads, edit
directly what is yours, route only what needs reporting, and record every change.
