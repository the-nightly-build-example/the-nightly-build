Genre and domain: an explanatory lesson that tells one real algorithmic-harm
incident in order (Rite Aid's facial-recognition surveillance and the FTC's 2023
action) and teaches why that kind of system fails that way, for a smart, widely
read reader with no time in a codebase. The register is sober narrative reporting
that carries a statistical explanation without turning lurid and without going
academic: name people and dates, keep allegation and fact distinct, and make the
base-rate and error-skew reasoning land in plain numbers. Study exemplars who
report a technical or institutional failure with a human cost accurately and
fairly, who can tell a story in order and teach the mechanism underneath it. Pick
passages that show that reporting-plus-explanation craft, especially the handling
of a contested account without editorializing. Avoid passages that carry a
verdict; the lesson reaches its own.

Run environment: web tools work through a configured proxy. Read each exemplar in
full from its own page and verify every quotation word for word before reporting.
