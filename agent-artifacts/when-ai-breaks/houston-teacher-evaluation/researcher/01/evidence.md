# Evidence record: when-ai-breaks/houston-teacher-evaluation (01)

The evidence firmly supports the commission's core account. The federal court's own
opinion in *Houston Federation of Teachers, Local 2415 v. Houston Independent School
District*, entered May 4, 2017, holds that HISD teachers "have no meaningful way to
ensure correct calculation of their EVAAS scores" and are therefore "unfairly subject
to mistaken deprivation" of a protected property interest in their jobs. The opinion
records the mechanism the commission asks for firsthand: HISD did not calculate the
scores (its vendor SAS did), SAS treated the equations and source code as trade secrets
"unavailable to plaintiffs or HISD," HISD admitted it did not itself verify or audit the
scores, and HISD conceded that any teacher's attempt to replicate a score "will
necessarily fail." The district's own FAQ (a litigation exhibit, recovered here in full)
supplies the admission the court quoted about scores being unrecomputable. SAS's own fact
sheet supplies the strongest version of the vendor's defense ("precise, reliable and
unbiased results," an "externally validated methodology"). The ASA statement and the
scholarly record supply the peer-reviewed context on volatility and the 1%-14% figure.

Two limits the writer must respect. First, the court did **not** rule EVAAS
statistically invalid or find that its scores are volatile. It granted HISD summary
judgment on the substantive-due-process (rational basis), vagueness, and equal-protection
claims, and expressly left the model's validity and volatility "disputed" and unresolved.
Only the procedural-due-process claim survived, and it turns on verifiability and notice,
not on the model being bad science. This matches the commission's framing exactly, but it
means volatility figures must be sourced to the academic literature (ASA, Amrein-Beardsley),
never to the court as a finding. Second, the settlement agreement's own text was not located
as a public document; its terms are corroborated by the plaintiff party (AFT) and by
independent reporting (Education Week), which agree on the substance and the $237,000 figure.

## Contradictions

- **The court did not find EVAAS invalid or volatile; it found it unverifiable.** The
  commission's angle is verifiability and due process, and the record supports that
  precisely -- but the same record cuts against any stronger claim. The court granted HISD
  summary judgment on rational basis, vagueness, and equal protection, and wrote that
  whether "EVAAS algorithms have been validated" is "disputed here," declining to resolve
  it (opinion pp.19-23). It cited three out-of-circuit decisions upholding value-added
  systems on rational-basis review: *Cook v. Bennett* (11th Cir. 2015), *Wagner v. Haslam*
  (M.D. Tenn. 2015, noting TVAAS "is a product of SAS"), and *Trout v. Knox Cty.* (E.D.
  Tenn. 2016, which called it "undisputed that 'TVAAS algorithms have been validated'").
  The writer must keep the court's finding as a finding (unverifiability, procedural due
  process) and attribute volatility/validity claims to the statisticians, not the judge.

- **SAS's defense vs the plaintiffs' case.** SAS's fact sheet asserts "precise, reliable and
  unbiased results" and an "externally validated methodology" (four US DOE Peer Review
  Committees, GAO, RAND, WestEd). The plaintiffs' unrebutted expert testified the scores are
  a "black box" that cannot be replicated without SAS's secret equations. Both are in the
  record; the dispute the court did *not* settle is which is right about the model's science.
  What would settle it, per the court, is access to SAS's equations, source code, decision
  rules, and assumptions -- which SAS withheld as trade secrets.

- **Product name.** SAS's own fact sheet and HISD's own FAQ both name the product
  "Education Value-Added Assessment System (EVAAS)." The court's opinion (p.3) writes
  "Educational Value-Added Assessment System." The owner's spelling ("Education") is
  authoritative for the product name; note the court's minor variant if quoting the opinion.

- **Number of individual plaintiffs.** The opinion states "Nine individual HISD teachers
  have also joined the suit" (p.2), and Education Week reports "nine teacher plaintiffs."
  Some coverage refers to "seven educators" in connection with the settlement. Use the
  court's "nine individual teachers" for who joined the suit; if a count at settlement is
  needed, verify it against the settlement text rather than a news paraphrase.

- **"Fired" vs "exited."** HISD maintained teachers "were not terminated solely on the basis
  of low value-added scores," but the court found "the record indicates otherwise" and
  recounted twelve HFT members whose continuing contracts were terminated for low
  value-added scores 2012-14, of whom four received letters citing value-added scores. Most
  affected teachers resigned, retired, or were reassigned rather than going through formal
  termination -- the court cited this as itself a due-process concern. Write "terminated,
  nonrenewed, or pushed out," not a flat "fired," and ground it in the opinion's pp.8-9.
