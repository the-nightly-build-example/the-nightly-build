# Evidence: when-ai-breaks/saferent-tenant-screening (01)

The record supports the commissioned account firmly on the timeline, the parties,
the legal mechanism, and the settlement terms, all traced to primary court
filings. Two documents carry most of it: the United States' Statement of Interest
(Jan. 9, 2023) and Judge Angel Kelley's Memorandum and Order on the motions to
dismiss (July 26, 2023), both filed on the docket and read here in full. The
weak point is the one the article must not overstate: the disparate-impact claim
against the SafeRent Score was never adjudicated on the merits. It survived a
motion to dismiss on plausibility and then settled. SafeRent admitted no
wrongdoing, maintains its scores comply with the law, and never disclosed the
algorithm, so the statistical claim that the score disproportionately scored
Black and Hispanic voucher applicants low is a pleaded allegation supported by
the plaintiffs' evidence, not a court finding. The settlement dollar figures and
the five-year injunctive terms are confirmed by a neutral litigation aggregator
and by plaintiffs' counsel and wire reporting, but the settlement agreement and
final-approval order PDFs themselves were behind a Cloudflare block and were not
opened directly; the exact fee/service-award split is the least-confirmed number
here.

## Contradictions

- **The score's alleged discriminatory effect was never proven.** SafeRent denies
  it. Yazmin Lopez, for SafeRent, said the company "continues to believe the SRS
  Scores comply with all applicable laws" and settled only because litigation is
  costly; the settlement carries no admission of liability (AP/Fortune;
  Clearinghouse). The disparate-impact and statistical claims cleared a motion to
  dismiss on plausibility (Memorandum and Order, July 26, 2023) but were never
  tested at trial or summary judgment. The article can report the mechanism as
  alleged and plausibly pleaded, and the settlement and its injunctive terms as
  fact, but cannot state as a court finding that the SafeRent Score discriminated.

- **The algorithm was never disclosed.** Per the Statement of Interest (p.14) and
  counsel, SafeRent "does not share any details about its algorithm" with
  landlords, applicants, or the public. What the model actually weighted, and by
  how much, is not in the public record. Any claim about the internal weighting of
  credit versus voucher status rests on the plaintiffs' inference from inputs and
  outcomes, not on the model itself.

- **Voucher-coverage figure: 73% vs. the plaintiffs' own numbers.** Cohen Milstein's
  case page states vouchers cover "over 73%" of monthly rent; the DOJ press
  materials elsewhere echo a high share. But the court's order, reciting the
  Amended Complaint, records Louis's voucher at about 69% and Douglas's at about
  57% of rent (Memorandum and Order, p.5-6). Use the plaintiff-specific figures
  when describing these two applicants; the 73% is a class/general characterization
  from counsel, not a figure the court adopted for the named plaintiffs.

- **"$2.3M" is a rounding.** Wire headlines say $2.3M (or $2.2M); counsel says
  $2.28M. The figure the record owns is $2,275,000 total, of which up to $1,175,000
  is cash to the class. Prefer the exact total or "about $2.28 million."

- **Metropolitan settled/exited separately from the framing.** The named corporate
  co-defendant, Metropolitan Management Group, LLC, is a real party whose motion to
  dismiss was denied; coverage centers on SafeRent, but the article should not imply
  SafeRent was the only defendant.
