# Evidence: when-ai-breaks/biden-deepfake-robocall (researcher 01)

The incident-in-order part of the commission is fully supported by primary documents:
the FCC's own declaratory ruling, its Notice of Apparent Liability and final Forfeiture
Order against Steve Kramer, its Consent Decree with Lingo Telecom, and New
Hampshire's own charging announcement together establish the tool (Eleven Labs),
the maker (a magician Kramer paid $150, publicly identified by NBC News as Paul
Carpenter), the exact call volume (9,581 calls verified by subpoena, not the "thousands"
early press estimated), the call date (Sunday, January 21, 2024) against the primary
date (Tuesday, January 23, 2024), the exact robocall script, and every dollar figure the
commission asks for (a $6,000,000 federal forfeiture against Kramer, finalized
unchanged in September 2024; a $1,000,000 civil penalty against Lingo Telecom,
reduced from a proposed $2,000,000; and $150 paid for the clone, separately described
by its maker as costing "$1" to generate). The mechanism claim the commission wants
taught — that a short sample now makes a convincing clone cheaply — is directly
supported by Eleven Labs' own current documentation and by the maker's own account
of a sub-$1, sub-20-minute job.

The record is thin, and importantly so, on the case's ending. The commission's angle
frames "what authorities did afterward" as the state charges alongside the FCC fine and
ruling, but the account is incomplete without the trial outcome: a Belknap County jury
acquitted Kramer on all 22 counts (11 felony voter-suppression, 11 misdemeanor
impersonation) on June 13, 2025 — after the original May 2024 indictment charged 13
of each. No source explains the gap between 13 and 11 counts; I could not reach the
underlying indictment or the New Hampshire court docket to resolve it (see
Contradictions and Discarded). The federal fine and the carrier settlement stand
regardless of the acquittal — they were never contingent on the criminal case — but a
lesson that ends the New Hampshire story at "indicted" without the acquittal would
misstate the record. This is the single most important limitation of this evidence set,
and the writer needs the full sequence: fined and sued at the federal level, acquitted at
the state level, on Kramer's argument that a non-binding write-in primary doesn't
trigger the voter-suppression statute and that a message never naming Biden isn't
impersonation.

## Contradictions

- **Count of charges, over time.** New Hampshire's May 23, 2024 indictment
  charged Kramer with 13 felony voter-suppression counts and 13 misdemeanor
  impersonation counts (one pair per named voter, doj.nh.gov). Reporting on
  the June 13, 2025 trial verdict (NHPR, WBUR) states he was tried and
  acquitted on 11 of each — 22 counts total. No source available to me
  explains what happened to the other two pairs of counts (dismissed,
  merged, or a reporting simplification). I could not reach the New
  Hampshire court docket or the original indictment document to resolve
  this (see Discarded); it should be stated as "11, down from an original
  13" rather than picking one number, unless the writer or editor can
  confirm the reason from the docket.

- **Motive, contested and unresolved by the verdict.** Kramer has
  consistently described his own motive as a deliberate demonstration of an
  AI risk to lawmakers — "I believe in my actions and I did them
  deliberately," and he did not intend to "apologiz[e] to the people who
  feel disenfranchised" (FCC 24-59, para. 16); at trial, "this is going to
  be my one good deed this year" (WBUR). The FCC, applying a different
  statute (the Truth in Caller ID Act) with a lower intent bar, found
  apparent "intent to defraud and cause harm" from the mechanics alone —
  the false caller ID, the false claim that voting Tuesday forecloses
  voting in November, and the false instruction to call the spoofed number
  to opt out (FCC 24-59, paras. 22-31). The New Hampshire jury, applying the
  state's voter-suppression and impersonation statutes and hearing the
  defense's argument that the write-in primary carried no formal
  consequence, acquitted on all counts. These are not the same legal
  question and can both be true: the FCC's civil finding under a
  caller-ID-fraud statute and the criminal jury's acquittal under different,
  state-specific statutes are not in logical conflict, but a reader who
  isn't told this will reasonably wonder how Kramer can be "guilty" at the
  FCC and "not guilty" in court. What would settle the tension for good is
  the trial record showing exactly what the jury instructions asked jurors
  to find — I did not obtain it.

- **Call volume, as first reported vs. as verified.** Wire coverage from
  Jan. 22, 2024 (cited inside the FCC's own orders) described the calls
  reaching "thousands" of voters, a figure that held up directionally but
  was imprecise; the FCC's subpoenas later fixed the number at 9,581 calls
  placed, of which 3,000 were the formally "verified" set the forfeiture
  math uses (FCC 24-59, paras. 11, 32-35). This is not a real disagreement,
  just successive precision, but a writer reaching for "thousands" should
  know the FCC's own number is exact and larger than that word implies.
