# Evidence: when-ai-breaks/donotpay-ftc (01)

The record fully supports the commission's spine from primaries. The FTC's
finalized complaint and Decision and Order (Docket C-4812, issued January 14,
2025) establish the marketing claims, the $193,000 payment, the subscriber
notice covering January 1, 2021 through December 31, 2023, and the exact
finding the angle turns on: DoNotPay "did not test whether the Service's
law-related features operated like a human lawyer," employed and retained no
attorneys, and built the Service on a natural language processing model, chatbot
software, and an OpenAI ChatGPT API never trained on a corpus of law (Compl.
para 20). DoNotPay's own quoted claims sit in the complaint's exhibits. The
settlement resolved on a "neither admits nor denies" basis, and DoNotPay's
public response is in the record.

Two things are thinner than the commission's phrasing implies, and both need
care in the draft. First, the FTC's legal theory is unsubstantiated and false
advertising, not proven-defective output. The Commission did not find, and did
not need to find, that any specific document was legally wrong. The harm it
charged is that DoNotPay made performance claims it never had evidence to back.
The complaint's one gesture at bad output (para 21) is an allegation of consumer
complaints, not a finding. Second, the in-court AirPods episode is absent from
the FTC record entirely. It is documented only in January 2023 reporting and
Joshua Browder's own statements. The complaint's courtroom-adjacent findings are
about the State Bar of California's unauthorized-practice investigation, a
separate track. Treat the earpiece stunt as contemporaneous reporting, not as
part of the FTC action.

## Contradictions

- **"Fine" vs. redress.** Several secondary outlets (Gizmodo, Slashdot, trade
  blogs) call the $193,000 a "fine." The order does not. Provision III titles it
  "Monetary Relief," and IV.D routes it to a consumer-redress fund. The FTC's own
  Section 5 deception counts here do not carry civil penalties. Use "monetary
  relief" or "redress," not "fine."

- **FTC theory vs. the commission's "confident wrong answer" framing.** The
  commission's angle says "a confident wrong answer looked exactly like a right
  one" and that DoNotPay "never measured its output against a lawyer's." The
  "never measured" half is squarely a finding (Compl. para 20). The "wrong
  answer" half is not something the FTC established: Counts I and II are
  false/unsubstantiated-advertising claims, and the only reference to defective
  output (para 21) is an allegation of consumer complaints. The draft can teach
  why fluent LLM legal prose carries no guarantee of correctness, but it must not
  attribute a finding of wrong outputs to the FTC. The FTC found the absence of
  testing and substantiation, which is the cleaner and defensible version of the
  same point.

- **The courtroom stunt is outside the FTC action.** The commission's incident
  paragraph bundles the January 2023 AirPods-in-court episode with the FTC case.
  They are separate. The complaint never mentions the earpiece plan; its
  courtroom-adjacent material is the California Bar's UPL investigation (para
  25-27). The earpiece episode is sourced only to January 2023 reporting and
  Browder's own words. Keep the two threads distinct in the draft.

- **Which model.** Fortune describes the January 2023 courtroom demo as using a
  GPT-3 model. The FTC complaint (para 20) describes the general DoNotPay Service
  as built on "a natural language processing model," "chatbot software," and "an
  API with OpenAI's ChatGPT." These are not in conflict: they describe two
  different things (the one-off courtroom stunt vs. the shipped Service) at
  different moments. Do not conflate them or state a single model powered
  everything.

- **Dates.** The complaint and order were both issued January 14, 2025 (per the
  documents themselves). The finalization was publicized in February 2025. The
  proposed settlement was announced September 25, 2024. No genuine conflict, but
  the "September 2024" date in the commission is the announcement of the proposed
  order; the binding order dates to January 2025.
