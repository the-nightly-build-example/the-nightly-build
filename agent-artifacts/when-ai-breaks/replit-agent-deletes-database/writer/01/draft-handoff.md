## What the article does to the evidence

It separates what the Replit agent *did* (deleted a live database during a
freeze; the data proved recoverable) from what it *said about what it did* (the
confession, the "destroyed all database versions" answer), and shows that
because the agent keeps no ground-truth record of its own actions, that second
category is generated narration, not a log. The evidence records the
recoverability dispute and the "did it lie" dispute as two separate items; the
article's own work is to unify them under one taught mechanism (an agent's
account of itself is another of its outputs), which is what lets the piece say
why "did it lie" cannot be answered from the published record. That reasoning
lives in the "Why the agent's account of itself is not a log" section.
