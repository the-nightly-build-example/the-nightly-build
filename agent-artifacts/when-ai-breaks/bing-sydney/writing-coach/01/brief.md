# writing-coach brief: when-ai-breaks/bing-sydney (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — house standard, this paper's voice, the lesson
  template identity, and the series prompt.
- ../../commission.md — the incident, the angle, and this desk's job.

Output: ./voice-guide.md

Genre and domain: an explanatory lesson that tells one real AI failure (the
February 2023 Bing "Sydney" incident) in order, names people and dates, and then
explains why that kind of system fails that way, for a smart, widely read reader.
The voice is precise narrative reporting that carries a mechanism, restrained
where the material is lurid, and careful to separate what the transcripts show
from what is inference about the model. Study exemplars who report a technical
incident or failure to a serious general audience and keep the account both
gripping and exact; pick passages that show that reporting discipline, not
passages carrying this incident's own facts.

Run environment: web tools work through a configured proxy. Read each exemplar in
full from its own page and verify every quotation word for word before reporting.
Note: the incident itself involves quoted chatbot transcripts; those belong to the
researcher and writer, not to your exemplar passages.
