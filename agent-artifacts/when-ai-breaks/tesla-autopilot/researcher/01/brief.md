# researcher brief: when-ai-breaks/tesla-autopilot (01)

Inputs:
- `../../commission.md` — the assignment, the desk's what/why/where shape, and the
  boundaries (the two prior AV lessons are a contrast to draw, not to restage).
- `../../editorial-direction.md` — citation standard, series territory, declared reader.

Output: `evidence.md` (this directory)

Focus and known risk surface (decisions the inputs do not settle):

- The two crashes are owned by NTSB reports: the Brown/Williston 2016 case
  (highway accident report HAR-17/02) and the Huang/Mountain View 2018 case
  (HAR-20/01). Read the reports, not summaries. Record for each: the exact date
  and place, what Autopilot was doing, what the driver was doing, the NTSB's
  probable-cause finding and contributing factors (driver-monitoring adequacy,
  operational design domain, infrastructure), and any safety recommendations
  issued. Get names, titles, and dates exact — a wrong date or a misattributed
  finding in display text is the costliest error the desk can print.
- The regulatory thread: NHTSA's 2016-2017 Autopilot investigation (ODI
  PE16-007) closing report and what it did and did not find; NHTSA's later
  engineering analysis and the December 2023 recall of Autopilot/Autosteer
  (Part 573 recall report and/or NHTSA closing documents) — record the specific
  defect described and the remedy. If a later NHTSA closing report characterizes a
  "critical safety gap" or a crash count, record it with its scope and period.
- Tesla's own account: find Tesla's public statement(s) on the Brown crash and on
  Autopilot's supervision requirements, and record Tesla's position verbatim where
  the exact wording is the evidence (this is the disputed-cause axis: Tesla's
  driver-misuse account vs NTSB's system-design account). Treat Tesla as an
  interested party, not an authority.
- The concepts the lesson teaches on the spot need a primary anchor: SAE J3016
  for the levels of driving automation (what Level 2 means); and, for automation
  complacency, a primary from the human-factors literature (NTSB reports
  themselves discuss it; a cited research source strengthens it). Record a crisp,
  sourced definition of each.
- The "where it lives today" close: record the scope of consumer Level-2 ADAS and
  any NHTSA standing-general-order ADAS crash data, and the status of Tesla's
  "FSD (Supervised)," to the owning primary — enough to ground the claim without
  overreaching.
- Contradictions are central here: Tesla vs NTSB on cause, and any dispute over
  whether Autopilot was engaged or the driver warned. Record each side's strongest
  version and what evidence settles it. An accusation needs two independent
  confirmations by parties in a position to know.

Run-environment caveat: record each source's own resolving page (ntsb.gov docket
or report page, nhtsa.gov investigation/recall page, Tesla's own page, SAE), never
a fetch-proxy URL. Some government PDFs sit behind slow endpoints; a gated or slow
source is not dead — resolve it to its real home.
