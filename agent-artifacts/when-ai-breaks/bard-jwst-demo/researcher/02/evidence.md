## Contradictions

- Cause of the share fall. The reporting does not attribute the drop to the demo
  error alone, and the commission's caution is borne out by the sources. Reuters
  (via Al Jazeera) opens by naming two things together: the ad's inaccurate
  answer, and analysts saying the AI search event "lacked details on how it will
  answer Microsoft's ChatGPT challenge" -- not, as the 01 record wrongly had it,
  that the event "failed to dazzle" (that phrase is not on the page; confirmed
  firsthand on this pass). The same report places Microsoft's ChatGPT-in-Bing
  launch one day earlier. NPR notes Microsoft shares rose 3% the same week. No
  source in the record isolates the demo as the sole driver; the honest
  statement is co-occurrence, not proven causation.

- Size of the share move. Sources disagree on the percentage because they measure
  different points of the day. NPR: shares "slid as much as 9%" (intraday low).
  Reuters/Al Jazeera: "fell 8 percent" to a $99.05 close. CNN's reporting (read
  only via search preview, not firsthand -- see Discarded) gives 7.7% at close.
  The market-value loss is reported as roughly $100 billion across sources; at
  least one outlet cited a larger intraday figure. Treat the percentage as a
  range (about 8% at close, up to ~9% intraday) and the dollar figure as "roughly
  $100 billion, as reported."

- Google's own hallucination framing. In the AI Overviews post, Google asserts
  its Search summaries "generally don't 'hallucinate'" like other LLM products.
  That is the operator's claim about its own retrieval-grounded design, and it
  sits against the same post's admission that inaccurate answers appeared. The
  record carries both; the writer should present Google's framing as Google's,
  not as an independent verdict.
