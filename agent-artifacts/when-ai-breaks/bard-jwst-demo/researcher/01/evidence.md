# evidence: when-ai-breaks/bard-jwst-demo (01)

The record supports the commission's spine firsthand. Google's own announcement
post carries the James Webb Space Telescope example (primary), the erroneous
sentence is captured verbatim from a source that shows the promotional GIF
(secondary), and the true imaging history is fixed by ESO's own two releases on
2M1207b (2004 detection, 2005 confirmation) plus NASA's page on Webb's first
direct image (HIP 65426 b, 2022). The market reaction is sourced only to
reporting, as the commission requires, and the reporting itself does not pin the
fall to the ad alone: the underwhelming Paris event and Microsoft's Bing-ChatGPT
launch the day before sit in the same sentences. The record is thin in one place
by design: I could not fetch the original Sundar Pichai tweet (HTTP 402) or read
Reuters, The Verge, or CNN at their own domains (blocked or 451), so the verbatim
error and Google's statement come from an independent outlet (Engadget) and a
verbatim Reuters reprint (Al Jazeera), not from the tweet or Reuters' own page.
The evidence does not undermine the commissioned angle; it sharpens the causal
caution the commission already asked for.

## Contradictions

- Cause of the share fall. The reporting does not attribute the drop to the demo
  error alone, and the commission's caution is borne out by the sources. Reuters
  (via Al Jazeera) frames the fall as following both the ad error and a Paris
  event that "failed to dazzle," and places Microsoft's ChatGPT-in-Bing launch
  one day earlier. NPR notes Microsoft shares rose 3% the same week. No source in
  the record isolates the demo as the sole driver; the honest statement is
  co-occurrence, not proven causation.

- Size of the share move. Sources disagree on the percentage because they measure
  different points of the day. NPR: shares "slid as much as 9%" (intraday low).
  Reuters/Al Jazeera: "fell 8 percent" to a $99.05 close. CNN's reporting (read
  only via search preview, not firsthand -- see Discarded) gives 7.7% at close.
  The market-value loss is reported as roughly $100 billion across sources; at
  least one outlet cited a larger intraday figure. Treat the percentage as a
  range (about 8% at close, up to ~9% intraday) and the dollar figure as "roughly
  $100 billion, as reported."

- Google's own hallucination framing. In the AI Overviews post, Google asserts
  its Search summaries "generally don't 'hallucinate'" like other LLM products.
  That is the operator's claim about its own retrieval-grounded design, and it
  sits against the same post's admission that inaccurate answers appeared. The
  record carries both; the writer should present Google's framing as Google's,
  not as an independent verdict.
