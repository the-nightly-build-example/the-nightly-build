Subject and angle: Chicago Police Department's Strategic Subject List (the "heat
list"). The article narrates the deployment and its failure from the record, then
teaches why this class of system fails. Work from the record itself.

Read first, primary, in full where relevant:
- The RAND Corporation evaluation of the pilot (Saunders, Hunt, Hollywood; get the
  exact authors, year, and journal or report). Pin down the study's finding: what
  effect the list had on a person's chance of being a homicide victim, and what it
  had on their chance of arrest. Get the sample, the period, and the exact
  reported results.
- The program's own documentation: the NIJ grant, the algorithm's designer and
  institution (Miles Wernick, Illinois Institute of Technology), the versions of
  the model, the inputs it used, and the intervention attached to a high score
  (what actually happened to a listed person).
- The Chicago Office of Inspector General review of the program (get its year and
  exact findings), and the decommissioning date and reason.
- Released list data or official counts: how many people were scored, the score
  range, and any breakdown showing how many high-scored people had no violent
  arrest or record.

Then, for the mechanism and the present:
- The best documentation of the feedback loop and proxy-label problem: that arrest
  data reflects policing rather than crime, so a model trained on it targets the
  already-policed. Cite the primary analysis, not a summary.
- Where person-based risk scoring or predictive policing is still deployed now,
  from a current primary or well-supported source.

Requirements:
- Every number (people scored, RAND effect sizes, share with no violent record,
  dates) checked against the primary that owns it. Accusations need two
  independent confirmations by parties in a position to know.
- Give each named person and body its exact title and affiliation.
- Confirm every URL resolves to the document's own page.

Record contradictions in full (for example, CPD's account of the list's purpose
versus the RAND finding, or disputes over how many listed people had no record).
Preserve any verified figure series useful for a chart, only from primary numbers.
Note source assets only if an exact document image, the list's own record, or an
official figure would carry the argument better than prose.
