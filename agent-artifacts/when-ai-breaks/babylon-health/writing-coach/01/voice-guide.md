# Voice guide: when-ai-breaks/babylon-health

## Casey Ross and Ike Swetlitz, "IBM's Watson supercomputer recommended 'unsafe and incorrect' cancer treatments, internal documents show"

Source: https://www.statnews.com/2018/07/25/ibm-watson-recommended-unsafe-incorrect-treatments/

> "Internal IBM documents show that its Watson supercomputer often spit out erroneous cancer treatment advice and that company medical specialists and customers identified "multiple examples of unsafe and incorrect treatment recommendations" as IBM was promoting the product to hospitals and physicians around the world."

Checked: https://www.statnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/IBMs-Watson-recommended-unsafe-and-incorrect-cancer-treatments-STAT.pdf, retrieved 2026-09-25
The lead sentence names the source of the finding (internal documents), the actor (IBM), and the company's own quoted phrase for what went wrong, instead of an adjective supplied by the writers. It also states, in the same sentence, that IBM was promoting the product while this was happening — the timing is the accusation, and it needs no comment.

> "IBM has not publicly acknowledged the shortcomings of the software, which uses artificial intelligence algorithms to recommend treatments for individual patients. To the contrary, top company executives told customers and others that Watson for Oncology's advice for physicians is based on data from real patients, and that it had won nearly universal praise from doctors around the world."

Checked: https://www.statnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/IBMs-Watson-recommended-unsafe-and-incorrect-cancer-treatments-STAT.pdf, retrieved 2026-09-25
This states IBM's public position in full, in the terms IBM itself used to customers, before the documents are turned against it. "To the contrary" is the only connective doing argumentative work; the rest of the sentence just reports what executives said, so a reader can weigh the claim once they later see what it left out.

> "'This product is a piece of s—,' one doctor at Jupiter Hospital in Florida told IBM executives, according to the documents. 'We bought it for marketing and with hopes that you would achieve the vision. We can't use it for most cases.'"

Checked: https://www.statnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/IBMs-Watson-recommended-unsafe-and-incorrect-cancer-treatments-STAT.pdf, retrieved 2026-09-25
The reporters let a named customer's own words carry the verdict and add nothing on top of it. "According to the documents" sits inside the sentence, not after it, so the quote is not softened by a hedge at the end.

## Julia Angwin, Jeff Larson, Surya Mattu, and Lauren Kirchner, "Machine Bias"

Source: https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing

> "Two years later, we know the computer algorithm got it exactly backward. Borden has not been charged with any new crimes. Prater is serving an eight-year prison term for subsequently breaking into a warehouse and stealing thousands of dollars' worth of electronics."

Checked: https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing, retrieved 2026-09-25
Three short sentences, each one checkable fact. "Got it exactly backward" is the only judgment in the passage, and the two plain sentences after it are the evidence for that judgment, not a restatement of it.

> "The algorithm used to create the Florida risk scores is a product of a for-profit company, Northpointe. The company disputes our analysis. In a letter, it criticized ProPublica's methodology and defended the accuracy of its test: "Northpointe does not agree that the results of your analysis, or the claims being made based upon that analysis, are correct or that they accurately reflect the outcomes from the application of the model.""

Checked: https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing, retrieved 2026-09-25
The company's rebuttal is quoted at length, in its own words, in the same section as the finding it disputes, not held back for a later "for its part" paragraph. The writers do not tell the reader whether the rebuttal is convincing; naming the company and quoting the letter is the whole move.

> "ProPublica's analysis reveals that higher Northpointe scores are slightly correlated with longer pretrial incarceration in Broward County. But there are many reasons that could be true other than judges being swayed by the scores — people with higher risk scores may also be poorer and have difficulty paying bond, for example."

Checked: https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing, retrieved 2026-09-25
The qualification names one specific alternative explanation instead of a general disclaimer about correlation and causation. "Slightly correlated," not "linked" or "tied to," keeps the finding exactly as strong as the data support and no stronger.

## Ben Goldacre, "It's a scandal drug trial results are still being withheld"

Source: https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2014/jan/05/scandal-drugs-trials-withheld-doctors-tamiflu

> "The Commons public accounts committee's report into the government's handling of the UK's £424m stockpile of the influenza drug Tamiflu, published last week, was damning. But starting from this narrow remit, the committee also stumbled – with palpable amazement – into a far wider problem."

Checked: https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2014/jan/05/scandal-drugs-trials-withheld-doctors-tamiflu, retrieved 2026-09-25
The figure and the document open the piece before any argument does: the £424m stockpile, the committee, the report. "Damning" is the one word of judgment across two sentences otherwise built from what the report is and what it found.

> "Nobody can give you a fully informed view on the benefits of any treatment, let alone Tamiflu, because the results of clinical trials are being routinely and legally withheld from doctors, researchers and patients. As the committee pointed out, government agencies around the world disagree on whether Tamiflu reduces your chances of pneumonia and death, but we can have no idea who is right, because we can't see the evidence. Astonishingly, in withholding this information for five years, Tamiflu's makers, Roche, have broken no law – and it is only an accident of history that this drug has become the poster child for change."

Checked: https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2014/jan/05/scandal-drugs-trials-withheld-doctors-tamiflu, retrieved 2026-09-25
Goldacre writes as a doctor stating what he personally cannot know, then names the company and the exact mechanism — five years withheld, no law broken — in the same breath. "Astonishingly" earns its place because the clause right after it is the fact that makes the case: legal, and five years.

> "It's tempting to fall back on crude fairytales of evil corporations, and there is no doubt that industry has lobbied hard against change. Secret internal memos, leaked in 2013, showed the US and EU industry representative bodies discussing their "advocacy strategy" against transparency, including a plan to "mobilise" the patient representative groups the industry often generously funds. But the real scandal lies with the very people we expect to protect us."

Checked: https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2014/jan/05/scandal-drugs-trials-withheld-doctors-tamiflu, retrieved 2026-09-25
He names the easiest version of the story — a villainous company — gives it real evidence, and then declines to end there, redirecting the harder accusation toward regulators. Being fair to a target here means refusing the most satisfying available case against it, not softening the real one.

## How this piece should sound

Babylon's "beats doctors" number came from a study it designed and presented; the Watson exemplar states IBM's claim in IBM's own terms, at full sentence length, before the internal documents are turned against it. The 2018 study and Babylon's marketing of it can get the same treatment here, stated plainly before the Lancet correspondence answers them.

Names, dates, and documents carry this kind of piece the way Norden's dated presentations carry the Watson story and the named judges and officials carry Machine Bias. MHRA, the 2018 study, the Lancet correspondence, Watkins, Fraser, Coiera and Wong, and the dates each entered the record are all available in place of a category like "regulators" or "critics."

Where Babylon answered a specific finding — a regulator's concern, a clinician's published critique, a documented test case — quoting that answer, in full sentences, next to the finding it responds to is available here the way STAT sets IBM's and Memorial Sloan Kettering's statements beside the documents they answer, and ProPublica sets Northpointe's letter beside the disputed analysis.

The commission's two failures — an oversold study and documented triage misses — can stay as separate as Goldacre keeps what Roche withheld separate from what regulators failed to do about it: one is a claim about a test, the other is what a missed case cost a patient, and a sentence that blurs them argues something neither piece of evidence supports on its own. "Vignette" and "curated test set" can do the same precise work here that "synthetic cases" does in the Watson piece: naming exactly what was compared to what, rather than asserting that testing in general cannot be trusted.

Whatever documented test cases or transcripts exist from Watkins or from clinical critics are this piece's Borden-and-Prater, or its Jupiter Hospital doctor: a specific, checkable instance that shows what a missed presentation looked like, rather than the piece asserting the category of harm. Where the record gives an exact case, it can carry the "documented triage misses" claim on its own.

Where the record runs out — what a missed case did or didn't lead to beyond what's documented, what MHRA's scrutiny changed and what it didn't — naming the specific unmeasured thing, the way the Broward County passage names bond and poverty as the alternative explanation ProPublica couldn't rule out, is available in place of a general note of caution.
