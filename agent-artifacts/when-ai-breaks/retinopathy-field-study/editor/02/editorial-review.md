# Editorial review: when-ai-breaks/retinopathy-field-study (editor/02)

Revision-confirm read. Editor/01 approved the piece except one publication-blocking
item routed to the writer: the opening epidemiological clause that diabetic
retinopathy "is a leading cause of blindness in working-age adults" (with its
paired "caught early it can be treated before sight is lost") carried no citation
and was absent from the evidence record. This read confirms that single fix and
the opening edge the cut touched. I did not reopen settled findings or add a new
standard.

## Blocker resolved

The uncited clause is gone. A search of the article for "leading cause,"
"working-age," "blindness," "caught early," and "before sight is lost" returns
nothing; the sentence and its treatability pair were removed, not moved. The
writer opened both candidate primaries firsthand and found neither owns the
specific claim: Gulshan 2016 (s2) gives prevalence and the screening case but not
this framing, and CHI 2020 (s1) states a materially different figure ("one of the
leading causes of vision impairment," "5% of cases of blindness worldwide"), not
blindness in working-age adults. With the brief forbidding a new claim or source,
a cut was the correct resolution rather than a reframe to the CHI wording. The
clause was background, not load-bearing: the thesis stands on the quality gate and
workflow, not on the disease's global burden, so no reasoning step was lost.

No uncited claim replaced it. The orientation paragraph now opens on two bare,
uncontroversial definitional sentences — what diabetic retinopathy is, and how it
is detected — then goes straight to the sourced specialist-shortage facts (1,500
ophthalmologists, 200 retinal specialists, 4.5M diabetics, the 1:3,000 ratio, the
60%/half-since-2013 screening target), every one carrying its s1 citation. A
definition of the disease and a plain description of fundus screening are
background, not the specific checkable epidemiological statistic that triggered
the block; they need no citation and introduce no new checkable claim.

## Opening edge (slop / dangling-referent read)

I read the changed edge cold, against the "Where it sits" rule in `spec/slop.md`.

New first body sentence: "Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the small blood
vessels at the back of the eye." It defines its own subject, so it has no referent
hidden in the briefing — a cold reader who arrived from a link is handed the term,
not asked to already know it. It carries a fact (the placeholder test leaves
nothing standing), so it is a real opener, not a weak edge the cut left exposed.
The dramatic hook the old clause supplied is gone, but a factual definition is a
legitimate opener; the paragraph does not read as though a sentence was ripped out.

Second sentence ("Catching it means someone examining a photograph of the retina
for the tiny hemorrhages and swelling that mark the disease") resolves "it" to the
disease named one sentence earlier and carries specifics. "There are rarely enough
eye doctors to examine every one" is mid-paragraph, not an edge; "every one" reads
naturally against the photograph/eye just introduced and hands off to the Thailand
numbers. No dangling referent, no weak edge, no slop introduced by the cut.

The "Why this matters" bookend was not touched by this cut and keeps its editor/01
wording ("how a system will serve the people it screens once real cameras, clinics,
and connections are in the loop"). Its opening reader-address is template-allowed
and carries a real observation; I confirm it reads clean and leave it as settled.
