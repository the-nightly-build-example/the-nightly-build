# Voice guide: when-ai-breaks/optum-health-algorithm (01)

## Directive

Write in the register of the people who found the bias, not the register of
someone reacting to it. The house voice is already plain and unhurried; this
lesson narrows that plainness to forensic calm. The subject invites indignation,
and indignation is exactly what to hold back, because the numbers only carry
moral weight if the prose around them stays flat enough to trust. State the harm.
Do not perform it.

Locate cause in a design choice, not in a person. The system did what it was
built to do; the fault is in what it was built to predict. Name the operators,
the vendor, and the dates as actors who made a choice, never as culprits who need
indicting. There is no villain here, and a sentence that reaches for one is wrong
on the craft as well as the facts.

Teach the mechanism as something the reader can watch happen. Set up the pieces
in order, hold one thing fixed, and let the reader see the gap open before you
name it. The reader who predicts the failure a sentence before you state it is
the reader you are writing for. Trust them to follow a quantitative argument laid
out cleanly, and to feel the weight of a figure without being told it is heavy.

Moves that will change sentences here:

- Anchor each claim to the comparison that reveals it, not to an adjective. Hold
  the risk score fixed and show what differs. The comparison is the evidence.
- Let one exact figure carry a paragraph. Cut the intensifiers crowding it.
- State a consequence in specific terms, then stop. The named condition or the
  number is the force; anything added to heighten it weakens it.
- Build the general rule the way a careful paper does: a bounded, precise claim
  about how this kind of proxy produces this kind of bias, never a sweeping one.

## Licenses

form: flat report of harm (understatement)
move: the exemplars report that sicker patients were passed over by giving the number and the clinical fact and letting it stand; the flatness is the control that keeps the finding credible.
bar:  the sentence names a specific consequence with a number, a named condition, or a concrete outcome; if its force comes from an adjective rather than the fact, cut the adjective or cut the sentence.

form: the earned numeric payoff
move: a longer setup ends in a short declarative that hands over the magnitude the setup promised, so the figure lands as a result rather than a slogan.
bar:  the short sentence delivers a specific number or ratio the preceding sentences built toward; if it announces significance without a figure, it is a punchline and gets cut.

form: second-person walkthrough of the revealing comparison
move: the reader is put at the point where the mechanism becomes visible and led one step to it, arriving at the flaw instead of being handed it.
bar:  the walkthrough holds every variable fixed except the one that exposes the bias and reaches only a conclusion the cited evidence supports; if it invents a fact to make the point, cut it.

## Sendhil Mullainathan, "Biased Algorithms Are Easier to Fix Than Biased People"
Source: https://thelivinglib.org/biased-algorithms-are-easier-to-fix-than-biased-people/
Craft:
- cadence: alternates a clipped line against a longer explanatory one; a short answer-line lands the magnitude after a longer question sets it up.
- argument: two parallel cases set side by side so the comparison does the arguing; the thesis emerges from the pairing rather than from assertion.
- evidence: one or two numbers per case, each introduced through a concrete human scenario before the figure appears, so the data feels consequential.
- stance: a researcher comparing findings, not an advocate; injustice is stated as a measured result and the sentence moves on.
- notice: he flags the counterintuitive turn and lets the reader feel it as a turn, without a slogan to mark it.
- diction: precise but unadorned ("a subtle engineering choice," "proxy"); no term left undefined, no drama added.
- reader: treated as able to hold a causal chain; nothing simplified, nothing inflated.
- the fault is placed in a design decision ("to measure sickness, they used health care expenditures"), so cause reads as mechanical and no person becomes the villain.

## Obermeyer, Powers, Vogeli, Mullainathan, "Dissecting racial bias in an algorithm used to manage the health of populations"
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_events/1548288/privacycon-2020-ziad_obermeyer.pdf
Craft:
- cadence: orderly and dense; long methodological sentences broken by flat declaratives that state a result plainly.
- argument: establish the comparison first (same risk score), reveal the health gap, run the counterfactual of fixing it, then expose the mechanism (the label is cost), then generalize.
- evidence: nearly every claim carries a figure with a comparison the reader already understands (a blood-pressure gap translated into a mortality difference); one ratio does the headline work.
- stance: neutral end to end; the charged finding is reported in the same register as the method, and cause is attributed to the objective function, not to intent.
- notice: they name the trap directly, that the proxy looks sound by some measures of accuracy yet still produces large bias; the danger is that it works on its own terms.
- diction: exact technical terms defined once and reused exactly ("label," "proxy," "ground truth").
- reader: assumed to follow a quantitative argument when it is laid out in the right order.
- the closing generalization lifts one study to a bounded rule about convenient proxies as a source of bias in many settings; the general claim is hedged and precise, never sweeping.

## Hannah Fry, "Hello World: Being Human in the Age of Algorithms"
Source: https://e-edu.nbu.bg/pluginfile.php/993798/mod_resource/content/1/Hannah_Fry%20-%20HELLO%20WORLD%20-%20How%20algorithms%20will%20decide%20our%20future%202018.pdf
Craft:
- cadence: short setup sentences leading into a longer explanatory chain, with frequent one-line turns that pivot the argument.
- argument: narrate the case in order, lay out both kinds of error, then name the general principle and flag that it will recur.
- evidence: concrete percentages dropped into a running scenario rather than a table, so each number advances the story.
- stance: even-handed; the tool's strengths and its failures get the same plain treatment, with no villain.
- notice: she separates the general lesson from the specific case explicitly, marking the moment a one-off becomes a pattern.
- diction: plain but warmer and looser than this lesson should run, reaching at times for colloquialism and a pop-culture analogy; take her pacing and her specific-to-general move, not her chattiness or her mascots.
- reader: addressed directly and walked one step at a time to the point of failure.
- she moves the reader to the flaw through a conditional they can follow, so the mechanism is seen rather than announced.
