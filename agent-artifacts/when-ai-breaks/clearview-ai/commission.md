# Commission: when-ai-breaks/clearview-ai

## Assignment
Teach one real AI failure from the record: Clearview AI built a facial-recognition
search engine from billions of photos scraped from the public web, sold it to
police, and drew findings of illegality from regulators and courts across several
countries. Template: lesson, 1200–2200 words, 0–4 flex sections.

## The incident in order (researcher confirms every name, date, figure from the record)
- What the system was built to do: a face-search product — upload a photo, get
  matches — backed by a database of billions of images scraped from social media
  and the open web, marketed to law enforcement.
- What it actually did / how it surfaced: Kashmir Hill's New York Times
  investigation (January 2020) revealed the scale and the police use. Confirm the
  claimed database size (billions of images; give the figure and its date) and the
  scraping practice.
- Who it affected: everyone whose photos were scraped without consent, plus the
  wrongful-match risk when police rely on it. Establish what the record shows about
  consent and accuracy.
- What operators and regulators did: the findings and penalties — e.g. the UK ICO
  enforcement (and its later appeal history), France CNIL, Italy Garante, Greece,
  Australia (OAIC), and the US Illinois BIPA settlement (ACLU v. Clearview, 2022)
  that restricted sales. Confirm each figure, date, and outcome, and note where an
  order was later overturned or is under appeal.
- Why this kind of system fails this way: scraping public images to build a
  biometric identifier and deploying matching without consent or validated
  accuracy. Teach face-matching briefly (or link the published mechanics/related
  lessons); the distinct lesson here is the scraped-database business model and the
  transnational legal response, not the mechanics of face recognition.
- Where the same weakness lives today: continued scraped-image biometric products
  and the precedents that constrain them.
