This lesson teaches the Clearview AI failure and its multi-country regulatory
record. Work from the record; prefer regulator and court documents' own words.

Source obligations: minimum 8 sources, primary >= 4, secondary >= 1. Every URL
resolves; cite only what you read.

Establish, each with a citation and the exact fact/figure:
  - The system and its scale: the scraped-image database size and date, the
    scraping practice, and the law-enforcement product. Kashmir Hill's NYT
    investigation (Jan 2020) is the load-bearing account of scale — cite precisely;
    corroborate the number where possible.
  - The regulatory/legal record, each with the deciding body, date, finding, and
    penalty/outcome: UK ICO (the fine and its subsequent appeal/tribunal history —
    note the reversal/appeal status), France CNIL, Italy Garante, Greece HDPA,
    Australia OAIC, and the US Illinois BIPA settlement (ACLU v. Clearview, 2022)
    and what it restricted. Where an order was overturned or is under appeal, state
    that plainly.
  - What the record shows about consent and accuracy (did Clearview validate
    accuracy? what did it claim?), and Clearview's own statements/defenses where
    quoted.
  - The mechanism, kept honest: why scraping public images to build a biometric
    identifier and deploying matching without consent or validated accuracy is the
    failure. Do not overstate the internal technology beyond the record; teach
    face-matching only as much as the lesson needs.
  - Where the same failure mode recurs now: continued scraped-image biometric
    products or later enforcement; cite a primary or credible source.

Keep primary (regulator decisions, court/settlement documents) distinct from
secondary reporting. Flag anything disputed (e.g. an order under appeal, a database
figure that varies by source) and say what would settle it.
