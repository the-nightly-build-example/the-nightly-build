# Evidence record: when-ai-breaks/robodebt (02)

This record supersedes researcher/01 by preserving all six sources it verified
and adding two new primaries the article's claims genuinely rest on: the
Federal Court's sealed consent order in Deanna Amato v The Commonwealth
(Justice Davies, VID611/2019, 27 November 2019), and the Senate Community
Affairs References Committee's final report of its Income Compliance Program
inquiry, "Accountability and justice: Why we need a Royal Commission into
Robodebt" (Chair: Senator Janet Rice). With these, eight sources have been read
in full at the cited passages: seven primary (Royal Commission Final Report
Vol 1; Prygodicz (No 2); Ombudsman April 2017; Ombudsman April 2021; Amato
consent order; the Senate References Committee final report; the
Attorney-General's-portfolio Knox settlement release) and one secondary (the
Prime Minister's media release). The source policy (min 8, primary >= 4,
secondary >= 1) is now met.

The two additions do specific work. The Amato order is the originating court
document on income averaging: it reproduces, in the court's own words, the exact
arithmetic that turned an annual PAYG figure into a fortnightly one, and
declares that raising the debt on that basis "was not validly made" and
"irrational, in the requisite legal sense." It gives the writer the worked
averaging example the lesson template requires, sourced to a primary rather than
reconstructed, and it corrects researcher/01's Discarded note: Amato has no
medium-neutral citation (consent orders carry none), but the sealed order and
its reasons-substitute "Notes" are a genuine, citable court primary read here in
full. The Senate References Committee report owns the recommendation that
produced the Royal Commission (Recommendation 1), a first-hand witness account
of automation that could not be overridden, and the program's cost-versus-savings
figures that no other source in this record carries.

Where the record stays careful, it stays where researcher/01 left it: on human
harm, official findings establish serious financial and psychological harm and
record deaths by suicide among people with Scheme debts, but no source assigns a
death toll to the Scheme, and the causal limits are explicit. Two new
qualifications are recorded in Contradictions: Amato establishes invalidity and
irrationality in one applicant's case by consent (not a broad "unlawful" ruling,
which is the Royal Commission's), and it records that a real but "very
substantially smaller" debt was in fact owed by Ms Amato, so averaging invented
the amount rather than always inventing the entire debt. The References
Committee report is a References Committee majority report and carries a
Government Senators' Dissenting Report, so its Royal Commission recommendation
was contested when made; attribute it as a committee-majority recommendation.

A practical access note that binds the gated sources below: the canonical
Australian government and AustLII pages return an automated-fetch block (HTTP 403
Cloudflare challenge, or 503) to a headless request, though they resolve
normally for a person in a browser. researcher/01's four gov/court documents were
read through the Internet Archive's capture of the identical canonical URL. For
this pass, the Senate report's aph.gov.au report pages were read directly with a
normal browser request (the headless fetch 403s; a browser user-agent returns
HTTP 200), and the Amato sealed order was retrieved and read in full from the
Federal Court's own online file on the Commonwealth Courts Portal
(comcourts.gov.au), which resolved without a block. Every URL recorded is the
document's own page, the address a reader should click, not the route used to
read it.

             penalty automatically raised against them." Third, averaging, once
             used only "in limited circumstances and often with the agreement of
             the recipient," was now applied automatically, "despite the fact that
             entitlement to income support was to be determined under the Social
             Security Act 1991 by reference to the actual fortnightly income of
             the recipient." The prior manual review had covered "about 20,000
             such files a year," each checked by a DHS compliance officer who
             contacted the recipient and could require the employer to provide
             specific payment information. On lawfulness: "The use of income
             averaging, in the way it was proposed, was in fact inconsistent with
             social security legislation (that is, it was unlawful)," and "As a
             matter of fact, the use of averaging as the sole basis to determine
             social security entitlement was unlawful." Income averaging is "not,
             per se, unlawful"; the unlawfulness lay in using it as the sole basis
             to prove a debt. The Report also records that the refund/zeroing
             program reimbursed "$746 million to some 381,000 affected
             individuals" and wrote off "debts amounting in total to $1.751
             billion."

## What the new sources anchor (for the writer)

- The Amato consent order anchors, at last from a primary, the worked
  income-averaging example the lesson template requires: an annual employer
  figure of $24,811 divided by 366 days to $67.79/day, multiplied by 14 to an
  invented fortnightly income of $949.05, substituted for the recipient's real
  reported income, with the differences aggregated into a debt. It also anchors
  the reversed burden concretely (the department needed no probative material
  for its assumption while the recipient's own reported earnings contradicted
  it) and the tax-refund garnish (the ATO ordered to hand over the lesser of
  $3,215.38 or her whole refund). Attribute the "not validly made" /
  "irrational" holding to Amato as an individual, consent-based ruling; attribute
  the systemic "unlawful" finding to the Royal Commission.
- The Senate References Committee report anchors the "what the operator did
  afterward" spine: the committee-majority recommendation that a Royal
  Commission be established (Recommendation 1). It also anchors the
  cost-versus-savings point no other source here owns ($2 billion projected
  savings against $606 million spent to build it), and provides a first-hand
  witness account of automation that could not be overridden, useful for the
  removed-human-check thread.

## Where-it-lives-today anchor

The series close needs a comparable automated system that still runs, stated
without editorial reach. One anchor is already sourced to a primary read above:
income averaging as the sole basis for a debt stopped, but automated income
data-matching between Centrelink and ATO records did not. The Ombudsman's April
2021 report records that Services Australia replaced the OCI with the Employment
Income Confirmation system (from February 2017) and then the Check and Update
Past Income (CUPI) system (from October 2018), which "is still in operation"
(PDF p. 10). The Senate References Committee report adds, from Services
Australia's own evidence, that the agency was "seeking to move towards near
real-time compliance," with changes from July 2020 (Ch 2, para 2.28). The
transferable weakness for the close is the pattern, not the brand: a system that
treats a data-match or a model output as proof of an individual fact and places
the onus on the person to disprove it. That pattern is documented across the
desk's prior lessons the commission names
(when-ai-breaks/dutch-childcare-benefits) and is the one the writer should point
to. A reader-facing comparator in a system the audience itself touches, for
example an automated tax-assessment or benefits-fraud scoring system in another
jurisdiction, would strengthen the close but must have its own primary opened
before it is cited; none was opened in this pass, so that comparator is a gap for
the writer or a later research pass to fill, not something to assert from memory.

## Contradictions

- Amato is a consent declaration of invalidity in one applicant's case, not a
  systemic ruling of unlawfulness. Its language is "not validly made" and
  "irrational, in the requisite legal sense" for Ms Amato's specific debts; it
  does not declare the Scheme unlawful in general. The broad unlawfulness finding
  belongs to the Royal Commission (2023). A writer must not upgrade Amato's
  individual, consent-based holding into a general legality ruling; attribute
  each to its owner. Amato is the originating case that established, in court,
  that averaged data could not by itself found a debt; the systemic finding came
  later.

- Amato also cuts against a flat "no one owed anything" reading. The
  Commonwealth's post-commencement review concluded that Ms Amato did owe a debt,
  but one "different and very substantially smaller" than the ~$2,924 demanded
  (Notes, para 2). The lesson is precise: averaging invented the amount of the
  debt (and, for many, a debt where none existed), not that every averaged debt
  was fictional. State it as the record does.

- The Senate References Committee report is a References Committee majority
  report and carries a Government Senators' Dissenting Report. Its Recommendation
  1 (a Royal Commission) was therefore contested when made, along party lines,
  and was not adopted by the government of the day; the Royal Commission was
  established later, after the 2022 change of government. Attribute the
  recommendation as a committee-majority recommendation, not a settled
  parliamentary finding.

- The 2017 Ombudsman report did not find the OCI unlawful, and this cuts against
  a reading in which every official body condemned the Scheme from the start. The
  April 2017 report was "satisfied the debts raised by the OCI are accurate,
  based on the information which is available to DHS at the time the decision is
  made," and said the fairness-of-discretion question "can only be answered by
  the courts." The 2021 Ombudsman report again kept "the lawfulness of the income
  averaging method" out of scope. The Royal Commission records the consequence:
  ministers and the department "relied on the Ombudsman's report as proof of the
  legality and appropriateness of Robodebt," and in 2018-2019 "DHS dissuaded the
  Ombudsman from mentioning the legality issue at all in his 2019 report" (Vol 1,
  p. xxviii). A writer should not enlist the 2017 Ombudsman report as an early
  finding of illegality; the unlawfulness findings came later, from the AAT, the
  Amato and Masterton judicial-review proceedings, the Prygodicz declarations,
  and the Royal Commission.

- The two courts and the Royal Commission differ on what officials knew about
  legality, and by how much. Murphy J in Prygodicz (2021) declined to find that
  ministers and senior public servants "actually knew that the operation of the
  Robodebt system was unlawful" ("There is little in the materials to indicate
  that the evidence rises to that level"; para 6), invoking "a stuff up" over "a
  conspiracy." The Royal Commission (2023), with a wider evidentiary base, found
  DSS had obtained legal advice in 2014 that averaging in this way was unlawful,
  that the Scheme "as it was introduced, was contrary to policy, and was unlawful"
  and that senior people were aware of that advice (Vol 1, printed pp. 27, 37 and
  211-216; PDF pp. 67, 77, 251-256).
  The two are not strictly inconsistent (they answer different questions on
  different records), but a lesson that flattens them into a single "they knew"
  claim overstates what the 2021 judgment found and understates what the 2023
  Report found. Attribute each to its owner.

- Causation of deaths is stated carefully by every source and by no source as a
  toll. The Royal Commission "is aware that a number of people who had alleged
  debts raised against them under the Scheme have died by suicide" but assigns no
  count (Vol 1, Ch. 12, p. 181). For one case it records the minister's concession
  that "it was not possible to say that the Scheme was the cause of the suicide,
  it was equally not possible to say that it was not," and adds the Commission's
  own view that "it seems highly probable that it was" (p. 182). For the case of
  Rhys Cauzzo, the Report notes his debt "was not raised by way of the OCI
  iteration of the Scheme" but by the earlier manual process (both used
  averaging), and documents DHS's effort to "work on a narrative" distancing the
  death from the OCI (p. 181). The transferable point: averaging, not the online
  interface specifically, was the common feature, and a writer must not attribute
  a named death to "the algorithm" or the OCI when the record ties it to the
  manual-but-averaged process. State harm as the record states it: serious
  financial hardship, distress and anxiety established at scale; deaths by suicide
  recorded among affected people; causal attribution limited and case-specific.

- Figure denominators differ across sources and are easy to conflate. See
  Numbers. These are different measurements (people vs debts vs class members vs
  amounts recovered vs amounts written off), not disagreements, with two genuine
  small gaps noted there.
