# writing-coach brief: when-ai-breaks/waymo-recall (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (artifact root) — house standard, paper voice, When AI Breaks series prompt.

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Subject and genre: a teaching lesson that tells one real AI failure in order (a
driverless car's system mispredicting a towed truck's path and colliding, twice,
leading to a recall) and then explains why that class of system fails that way.
The domain is autonomous-vehicle safety and machine perception, explained for a
smart non-programmer. Register: a serious daily course, plain and concrete,
sober; no one was injured, so no reach for drama.

Study writers the field rates who reconstruct a technical failure or accident for
non-specialists: a clear timeline, precise attribution of cause, and restraint
that lets the facts carry the weight. Show how they narrate a sequence of events
cleanly, how they explain a technical cause without jargon, and how they keep the
stakes proportionate to what happened. Accident and safety-engineering writing,
and the best incident reporting, are the models; avoid sensational tech coverage.
Do not quote passages that carry this lesson's own findings about Waymo; quote for
the writing.

This piece has to make the reader understand why a careful system still hit
something rare, without either excusing it or inflating it. Passages that hold a
precise timeline and a measured judgment together are the most useful.
