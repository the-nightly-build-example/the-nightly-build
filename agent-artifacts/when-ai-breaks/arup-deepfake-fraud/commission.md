## The incident and the angle

The desk teaches one real, recorded AI-related failure. This one is the early-2024
deepfake video-call fraud against the engineering firm Arup: a finance employee in
its Hong Kong office was tricked into transferring about HK$200 million (roughly
US$25 million) after joining a video conference in which the chief financial
officer and other colleagues were AI-generated deepfakes.

Tell it in order: the employee received a message about a confidential
transaction and was suspicious; a video call with what appeared to be the CFO and
familiar colleagues reassured them; on that basis they made a series of transfers
(reported as roughly 15 transactions) totaling about HK$200M; the fraud surfaced
only when the employee later checked with head office. Name the firm (Arup, which
confirmed it was the target), the place and date (Hong Kong, January 2024), the
amounts, and the police who described it (Hong Kong Police Force officers at the
February 2024 briefing). Verify each figure against the record.

Then explain why that kind of system fails, teaching the missing piece on the
spot: real-time face- and voice-deepfakes are now cheap and convincing enough to
defeat the human check the process relied on — "I saw and heard my colleagues on
the call." The failure was trusting a live video image as proof of identity.
(Link the published `when-ai-breaks/biden-deepfake-robocall` for voice cloning;
this incident is different — interactive, multi-party video used in enterprise
fraud, not a one-way political robocall.) Close where the same weakness lives
today: any organization that treats a face or a voice on a call as authentication,
and the defenses that actually work (out-of-band verification, call-backs, code
words) because detection alone does not.

Work from the record: the Hong Kong police account, Arup's own confirmations, and
reporting that held up. Where a detail is disputed or unconfirmed (exact transfer
count, how the deepfakes were generated), give the strongest supported version and
say what is not established.
