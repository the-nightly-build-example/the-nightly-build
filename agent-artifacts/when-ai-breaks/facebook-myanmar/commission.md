## Subject
Facebook's role in Myanmar, 2012-2018: how the platform's engagement-optimizing
recommendation and ranking systems amplified anti-Rohingya hate speech and
incitement in the run-up to and during the 2017 military campaign against the
Rohingya, which UN investigators described as genocidal. This is the paper's
first lesson on a **recommender system** failure. Tell it on the series' terms:
what happened in order, why that kind of system fails that way, and where the
same weakness lives today. A mass atrocity is in the record; write with exact,
unsentimental restraint, never lurid and never minimizing.

What happened, in order (name people, bodies, dates; work from the record):
- What the system was built to do: Facebook expanded rapidly in Myanmar as the
  country came online (Facebook effectively WAS the internet for many via
  zero-rating), and its feed ranking/recommendation optimized for engagement.
- What it actually did: engagement-optimized distribution amplified inflammatory
  anti-Rohingya content; Facebook had almost no Burmese-language moderation
  capacity (few Burmese-speaking reviewers; no adequate hate-speech classifiers
  for Burmese at the time), so incitement spread with little friction. State what
  is established: the UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Myanmar
  (2018, A/HRC/39/64) found Facebook a "useful instrument" for spreading hate;
  Facebook's own commissioned BSR Human Rights Impact Assessment (Nov 2018)
  concluded the company was not doing enough to prevent the platform being used to
  "foment division and incite offline violence."
- Who it affected: the Rohingya. Report scale from authoritative sources (e.g.
  the ~700,000+ who fled to Bangladesh from Aug 2017) with the owning source.
- What the operator did afterward: Facebook's admissions and BSR report (2018);
  removal of Tatmadaw-linked accounts/pages (Aug 2018); later litigation
  (the 2021 ~$150bn class actions; Amnesty International's 2022 report "The Social
  Atrocity" calling for reparations). Name dates and who said what. Keep causal
  claims proportionate to the record: the platform amplified and failed to
  moderate; it did not act alone, and the military bears primary responsibility.

Then teach why that kind of system fails that way: an engagement-optimizing
recommender promotes content that maximizes predicted engagement, and outrage and
fear reliably drive engagement, so such content is systematically amplified unless
counter-measures exist; pair that with near-absent local-language moderation and
the amplification runs unchecked. Teach the missing pieces (engagement
optimization; content moderation at scale) on the spot or link where taught.

Close where the same weakness lives today: every algorithmic feed that ranks for
engagement, especially in languages and regions with thin moderation coverage.
Keep it concrete and current.

## Required contribution
Move the reader from "Facebook was blamed for Myanmar" to the specific mechanism
the record documents: an engagement-optimizing recommender amplifying incitement,
compounded by near-absent Burmese-language moderation, so they can recognize the
same amplification-plus-thin-moderation pattern in feeds they use now. Hold the
line between what is established (amplification, failure to moderate, the company's
own admissions) and contested causal magnitude, and never overstate the
platform's share of responsibility relative to the perpetrators.
