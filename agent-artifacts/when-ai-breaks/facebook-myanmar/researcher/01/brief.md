# researcher brief: when-ai-breaks/facebook-myanmar (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — the exact subject, angle, and required contribution

Output: ./evidence.md

Work from the record (series rule). Read firsthand:
- UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Myanmar: the 2018 report
  (A/HRC/39/64) and its detailed findings document if reachable. Read the exact
  passages on Facebook's role ("a useful instrument for those seeking to spread
  hate"), and the genocide/crimes-against-humanity findings as the UN stated them.
  Quote the platform-role wording precisely; represent the atrocity findings in
  the UN's own terms.
- Facebook's commissioned BSR "Human Rights Impact Assessment: Facebook in
  Myanmar" (published Nov 2018) and Facebook's own accompanying statement. Read
  what the company conceded (not doing enough; platform used to foment division
  and incite offline violence) and what it announced (moderation hiring, account
  removals).
- Amnesty International, "The Social Atrocity: Meta and the Right to Remedy for
  the Rohingya" (2022). Read its central claim that Meta's engagement-based
  algorithms "proactively amplified" anti-Rohingya content, and its reparations
  demand. Note it is an advocacy organization's report (primary to its own
  analysis, but stake-holding) and corroborate the mechanism claim where possible.
- Reporting that held up: Reuters' 2018 special reports on Facebook and Myanmar
  hate speech (and the moderation-capacity findings), and the account/page
  removals (Aug 2018 Facebook newsroom post, primary). The 2021 class-action
  filings (~$150bn) — get the filing facts and date.

Verify against owners: the displacement figure (~700,000+ fled to Bangladesh from
Aug 2017 — attribute to UNHCR or the UN FFM), the moderation-capacity facts (how
few Burmese-speaking reviewers, and when Facebook lacked Burmese hate-speech
classifiers), and the dates. CRITICAL for this piece: record in Contradictions the
disputed question of causal magnitude — Facebook's amplification vs the military's
primary responsibility, and any pushback on Amnesty's "proactively amplified"
characterization. The article must not overstate the platform's share.

Source assets: identify any primary figure/quote block (e.g. a UN report passage,
a BSR statement) usable as evidence; note where it lives. Do not prescribe crops.

Source policy: at least 8 sources, at least 4 primary, at least 1 secondary.
Primary = the authoring body (UN FFM, BSR/Facebook, Amnesty for its own analysis,
court filings, Facebook newsroom). Reporting is secondary. Name no company as an
authority on itself.

Environment: fetches go through a proxy; UN PDFs and reports may be large or
gated — on a 403/paywall retry with a browser-style request first, record the
document's own canonical URL, and read the specific cited passages in full.

Sanity check: the full published when-ai-breaks slug list is in the commission's
Boundaries. Do not build the record around a duplicate angle.
