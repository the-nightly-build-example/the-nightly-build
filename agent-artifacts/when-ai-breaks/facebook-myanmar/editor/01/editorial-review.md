# Editorial review: when-ai-breaks/facebook-myanmar (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: an engagement-ranked feed amplified anti-Rohingya incitement that
near-absent Burmese-language moderation left unchecked, widening the reach of a
campaign the Tatmadaw planned and drove — a substantial contribution of
contested magnitude, not a measured share and not the primary cause. The piece
states this cleanly and, more importantly, holds it as the grammar of every
sentence where the two actors meet, not as a one-line disclaimer.

The load-bearing claims and how they held:

1. **Facebook was the internet in Myanmar; it ran the same engagement-ranked
   feed there.** Supported by BSR (s1, primary) for the ~20M "Facebook is the
   internet" dominance; the "same feed everywhere" is a reasonable, uncontested
   inference and Amnesty (s4) describes the news-feed/ranking/recommendation
   stack as the amplifying system. Held.

2. **Near-absent Burmese moderation.** One Burmese reviewer in 2014, ~60 by
   mid/Aug 2018 for ~20M users, no in-country staff, outsourced to Accenture in
   Kuala Lumpur. Triangulated across Reuters (s5, secondary), BSR (s1), and
   Warofka (s6). This is the record's strongest ground and the article rests
   most of its weight here. Held firmly. The `data-nb-kind` on the moderation
   spine is right: an independent secondary (Reuters/UC Berkeley) carries it,
   so the primary-heavy set does not hide a missing independent source.

3. **"Proactively amplified" is Amnesty's conclusion, not Myanmar telemetry.**
   This was the claim I pushed hardest to break, because it is the one the whole
   commission wants and the record is weakest on. The article does not overreach:
   it presents "proactively amplified" explicitly as Amnesty's reading, states
   in the same passage that it "rests on the general Facebook Papers evidence…
   plus inference," that it "does not rest on Myanmar-specific data… because no
   such data is in the record," and closes "Amnesty's reading, not a measured
   share." It then names the exact missing evidence twice (a distribution record
   against a neutral baseline plus a counterfactual). This is exactly the
   restraint the brief demanded, executed. Held, and correctly fenced.

4. **Proportionality (top priority).** I read every sentence where platform and
   military co-occur. The military is "planned and carried out" / "planned and
   drove" throughout; the platform "amplified," "removed friction," "widened the
   reach." The orientation calls the platform question "smaller." The dek leads
   on the feed (the article's subject) but subordinates it to "a campaign the
   Tatmadaw planned and carried out," so the platform is wind and not origin — no
   tip into primary cause, no minimizing of the military. The record-and-limits
   close and the takeaway both restate "not a measured share and not the primary
   cause." No sentence tips the platform into the primary cause; none reads as
   minimizing the Tatmadaw. This is the piece's central success.

Display text, descriptor by descriptor:
- Headline states an established mechanism finding (ranked for engagement; could
  not moderate Burmese), actors named, no colon tell, no performed shock.
- Dek makes a claim about the world and is sized in its own sentence; it does
  not grade the article's method. No comma-triad / semicolon-reversal mold.
- Subheads each carry a step of the argument in the piece's own nouns; no
  comma-and pairs, no scaffolding slots. "What the record settles and what it
  cannot" is faintly method-flavored but the record's limit *is* that section's
  substantive teaching, so it earns the frame.
- Named people/titles verified: Alex Warofka (product policy manager, Nov 2018);
  Senior General Min Aung Hlaing (commander-in-chief); Evelyn Douek (Lawfare,
  2018); Daphne Keller (the structural-flaws vs bad-actor-exploitation
  distinction). All match the evidence.

Numbers and quotes checked against the primaries: UN FFM para 74 block quote is
exact and correctly reproduced through "must be independently and thoroughly
examined" (not truncated at "useful instrument," which would have dropped the
UN's causation caveat); genocidal-intent / prosecution findings cited to paras
85–88; nearly 725,000 fled (para 33); the 13%→52%→63% proactive-detection series

It resolves and is the N.D. Cal. removal packet (No. 3:22-cv-00051) containing

substantial cause,' not the cause" is faithful to the plaintiffs' own framing.

No broken central claim, no miscitation, no source-policy failure found.

## Cut

I ran the earns-its-place test sentence by sentence. The piece is already tight
(2199/2200, with the writer's first-pass length and density warnings resolved by
cutting, not waiving), and the prose is disciplined against slop: grave facts are
stated flat and left unmodified, no horror adjectives, no narrated violence, no
described injury. The single "not X, but Y" contrast in the BSR/Warofka paragraph
("an admission of not removing enough, not of an algorithm that promoted the
incitement") is earned — it corrects a real and named misconception — and stays.

Signpost candidates I weighed and deliberately left standing:
- Orientation's closing "This lesson is about the smaller, contested question…"
  carries the "smaller" proportion sizing and the pivot into the body; cutting
  the whole sentence (the only surgical option, since I cannot rewrite) would
  lose that sizing. Kept.
- "There is a real counterargument about the mechanism." reads as an
  announcement, but it marks the pivot from a *magnitude* caveat (Amnesty is
  unmeasured) to a *mechanism* caveat (the spread was deliberately engineered),
  and keeping those two axes distinct is the piece's core burden. Kept rather
  than regress the distinction.

No prompt leakage: the "substantial contribution of contested size, not a
measured share and not the primary cause" line matches the brief's wording
because the brief mandated that exact synthesis as a claim about the record; it
is sourced synthesis, not a planning label or a claim that the assignment was
fulfilled. "This lesson" is the established unit of this course (press voice and
lesson template), not `this-dossier`-style self-narration.

Furniture: the "Why this matters" / "The takeaway" bookends and the moderation
table each do real work. The table's caption honestly states the buildup
postdates the August 2017 campaign, so the rising trend cannot read as
pre-atrocity diligence — the exact honesty the evidence's asset note required.

No direct cuts made. I found no sentence whose removal clearly improves the piece
without collateral loss, and I declined to trim for polish alone.

Worst tell: none rising above the two signposts above, both of which do enough
work to survive.

## Reader

Read straight through as the paper's declared reader (smart, widely read, no time
in a codebase), what I have that the nine sources alone would not give me: a
single *sized* account that separates blame from mechanism — teaches the settled,
ordinary, working-as-designed mechanism, holds it apart from the contested
magnitude of Facebook's share, and names precisely the data that would settle
that magnitude and does not exist. No single source in the record does this; they
run from Amnesty's "proactively amplified" to BSR's "not fully understood," and
the piece is the thing that proportions them. The draft-handoff's original-work
sentence claims exactly this, and it survives the read. The prose sits closer to
the voice-guide exemplars (Gourevitch's flat grave fact; the Times' "germs are
ours, but Facebook is the wind" proportion) than to a median AI summary: causal
verbs are sized to the record across the whole piece, the gravest facts are
stated flattest, and the failure is framed as a system doing what it was built to
do. Reread as the largest claim, the headline commits only to what is
established and the body defends it.
