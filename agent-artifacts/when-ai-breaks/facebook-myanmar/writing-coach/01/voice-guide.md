# Voice guide: when-ai-breaks/facebook-myanmar (01)

Write plainer than the house default, not louder. The subject supplies all the
weight; the prose adds none. The reader has heard that Facebook was blamed for
Myanmar and cannot tell blame from mechanism. Treat them as capable of holding a
proportioned account: a platform can be gravely at fault without being the killer,
and your job is to keep those two facts in the same steady sentence.

Three moves change sentences here, beyond the house register.

Size every causal verb to the record, and hold the sizing across the whole piece.
Facebook amplified, ranked, failed to moderate, removed friction. The Tatmadaw
planned and carried out. Keep the two subjects grammatically distinct every time
they meet; never let the platform's share swell to fill a sentence where the
military belongs, and never shrink it to a footnote either. The proportion is not
a disclaimer you add once. It is the grammar of the whole lesson.

State grave facts flat and stop. A sourced number or a documented act of
incitement carries its own charge; an adjective of horror on top of it reads as
the writer reaching for an effect the fact already had. Give the displacement its
figure and its source and move on. If a word only raises the temperature, it is
not reporting and it goes.

Frame the failure as an ordinary system doing exactly what it was built to do.
This is the spine of the lesson and the hardest register to hold: the recommender
did not malfunction, and outrage was not a bug in it. Teach that the machinery
worked as designed and that the design is the problem, without letting "as
designed" curdle into either a shrug or an indictment the evidence has not earned.
The mechanism is cold; the stakes are not; the prose reports both without mixing
their temperatures.

Do not editorialize where the fact already testifies. Do not narrate a wound for
its own sake. Do not soften a finding with hedging, and do not harden it past what
its source says. Every number gets a comparison the reader already holds.

## Licenses

form: a single sourced human particular inside the teaching
move: the Times opens on thirteen named family members "glued to Facebook. And
  they were furious," then reads a real viral meme back before making any systemic
  claim, so the mechanism is first seen acting on actual people. It uses the
  particular once to make an abstraction observable, then pulls back to the system.
bar: the detail is drawn from the record and exists to make the mechanism legible
  (amplification reaching a real person, moderation absent at a real moment). If
  the sentence would teach the same with the name, place, or post deleted, it is
  decoration and goes. No injury is described for effect.

form: the bare grave fact, stated and left unmodified
move: Gourevitch reports the dead and the administrative machinery of the killing
  in exact, unadjectived terms and lets the fact indict itself; the restraint is
  the emphasis, not a lack of feeling.
bar: the fact is cited and load-bearing for the lesson, and it names a specific
  number or act, not a magnitude ("more than 700,000 fled," sourced, never
  "unimaginable suffering"). Adding an intensifier is the failure. Delete any word
  that raises heat without adding information.

form: one explicit apportioning of responsibility between platform and perpetrator
move: an official in the Times piece says, "The germs are ours, but Facebook is
  the wind" — the platform's causal role named exactly, wind and not origin, with
  primary agency handed back to the actors who held it.
bar: it assigns each party a verb its cited source supports and keeps the two
  clauses distinct. Facebook's share does not grow to crowd the military out, and
  the military's primacy is stated, not implied. Use it where the misconception it
  corrects (that Facebook caused, or committed, the genocide) is actually in the
  reader's head. At most once or twice; it is the load-bearing beam, not a refrain.

## Amanda Taub and Max Fisher, "Where Countries Are Tinderboxes and Facebook Is a Match"
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2018/04/21/world/asia/facebook-sri-lanka-riots.html
Craft:
- cadence: a long, detail-loaded establishing sentence ("Past the end of a remote
  mountain road, down a rutted dirt track, in a concrete house that lacked running
  water but bristled with smartphones") snapped shut by a four-word one ("And they
  were furious"). Length is a tool; the short sentence lands the point the long one
  set up.
- argument: reconstructs a specific chain, traffic dispute to rumor to viral meme
  to organized attack to a death, so causation reads as a sequence of real steps
  rather than an assertion. The system claim is earned by the narrated chain, not
  announced ahead of it.
- evidence: named people, an exact count (thirteen), a quoted victim's cousin, the
  actual content of the memes. It shows the platform's effect through what real
  users said and did, not through characterization of the platform.
- stance: the platform is gravely implicated and is still not the origin. The piece
  holds both without straining, and lets a source ("the germs are ours, but
  Facebook is the wind") carry the proportion so the authors never have to
  editorialize it.
- notice: the detail that a house lacking running water still bristled with
  smartphones. It picks the concrete fact that makes the whole condition legible.
- diction: plain nouns, ordinary verbs; the one figure ("embers beneath the ashes")
  is quoted from a subject, not minted by the writers.
- reader: assumed to follow a causal argument if it is built step by step, and
  trusted not to need the blame spelled out once the sequence is shown.
- the important move the axes missed: it grounds every systemic claim in a scene it
  already showed, so the abstraction never floats free of a case the reader watched
  happen.

## Philip Gourevitch, "We Wish to Inform You That Tomorrow We Will Be Killed With Our Families"
Source: https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780312243357/wewishtoinformyouthattomorrowwewillbekilledwithourfamilies
Craft:
- cadence: short declaratives that stop where a lesser writer would keep
  qualifying. "The idea is the crime." The full stop does the work an adjective
  would have blunted.
- argument: reframes atrocity as organized system, not chaos: genocide as "an
  exercise in community building," the product of "one of the most meticulously
  administered states in history." The reframe is the analysis, and it is exactly
  the move a mechanism lesson needs.
- evidence: exact, deflating specifics, the millimeter measurements of colonial
  "nasal indices" that built a racial hierarchy. Precision exposes the machinery;
  the number is the indictment.
- stance: moral clarity held through structural clarity rather than emotive
  language. "Denouncing evil is a far cry from doing good" judges without heat.
- notice: admits the writer's own discomfort plainly ("The dead at Nyarubuye were,
  I'm afraid, beautiful") instead of performing grief, which reads as more honest
  than any lament.
- diction: intellectual, cool words for hot facts ("affront," "intent,"
  "administered"); no reaching for horror vocabulary, because the facts hold it.
- reader: addressed as an adult who can look at organized killing analytically
  without being told how to feel about it.
- the important move the axes missed: understatement as the load-bearing technique.
  The gravest facts are stated most flatly, and the flatness is what makes them
  land.

## Steve Stecklow, "Why Facebook is losing the war on hate speech in Myanmar" (Reuters, "Hatebook")
Source: https://www.reuters.com/investigates/special-report/myanmar-facebook-hate/
Note: studied through the report's documented method and findings; Reuters' paywall
blocked reading its sentences directly, so these notes describe what it does, not
its cadence at the word level.
Craft:
- argument: the case is carried by a mismatch of scale. More than 1,000 documented
  hate-speech posts on one side; on the other, one Burmese-speaking reviewer in
  2014, a second in early 2015, roughly sixty by mid-2018, most stationed in Kuala
  Lumpur. The two numbers side by side make the argument without a thesis sentence.
- evidence: internal figures and a systematically counted corpus, not
  characterization. The reliance on users to report hate speech, because the
  automated systems could not read Burmese, is reported as an operational fact with
  its consequence attached.
- stance: plain about failure, disciplined about cause. It establishes that
  moderation was near-absent and that incitement spread with little friction, and
  it does not leap from that to authorship of the violence.
- notice: the specific operational bottleneck (reviewers handling more than a
  thousand items a day, leaving no time to look for hate speech proactively) that
  turns a staffing number into a mechanism.
- diction (inferred from method): attributed, quantified, concrete; the report
  argues with counts and dates rather than adjectives.
- reader: assumed to want the documented number and the source of it, and to draw
  the conclusion once the figures are laid out.
- the important move the axes missed: let the counts do the arguing. When the scale
  of the problem and the scale of the response are both stated exactly, the gap
  between them is the finding, and no editorializing is needed to close it.

## Self-test

A writer holding only the house default would produce clear, plain, well-sourced
teaching. It would not reliably hold causal verbs sized to the evidence across a
whole piece about a genocide, would not know to state the gravest facts flattest,
and would not frame the failure as a system working as designed rather than a
platform going wrong. Those three are what this guide adds. The register it names
is a mechanism lesson that carries a mass atrocity through exact, unmodified fact
and proportioned agency, and never through the temperature of its own prose.

## Missing decision for the orchestrator

The lesson template opens on a "Why this matters" bookend, not a scene, while the
desk prompt asks the writer to "tell what happened in order" and "name the people."
License 1 permits a restrained human particular in the body, but the brief does not
say how far that particular may go given the "never lurid" constraint, nor whether
the opening bookend may carry one at all. I have licensed it conservatively (one
use, sourced, mechanism-serving, no described injury). If the orchestrator wants a
firmer ceiling, or wants the human particular kept out of the bookends entirely,
that bound should reach the writer explicitly.
