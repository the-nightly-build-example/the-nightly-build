# Voice guide: narrating a disputed system failure fairly

## Directive

Write this like a fair investigator who has read the whole record and knows the
honest answer is quieter than the headline that sent readers here. The house
default already gives you plain sentences and no hype. What this piece needs on
top of that is the discipline to deliver an anticlimax as the real story: to
carry a public accusation and a careful "no violation found" at the same weight,
in the same calm register, and refuse the reader the single villain the viral
version promised.

The reader arrives already convinced there was a verdict. You keep their trust
not by confirming it and not by debunking it, but by conceding the strongest
form of each side before you weigh anything, and by narrating the most
inflammatory facts without flinching and without inflating them. A writer who
does this well sounds unhurried and exact where a lesser one would either
prosecute or apologize.

Three moves will change your actual sentences here. First, strip the evaluative
adjective and adverb off the facts that are doing the emotional work; let the
number or the event carry it, and add nothing. A gap between two figures stated
flatly hits harder than the same gap called "staggering," and the flat version
is also the honest one. Second, when two findings both hold, put them in two
plain adjacent sentences and leave them unreconciled; do not reach for the "but"
that quietly dissolves one into the other. Third, when you reach the question of
what would settle the dispute, show the seam in the open: name the specific
evidence that would decide it, then show concretely why no one can get that
evidence. Do not assert that proof is "hard" and move on.

Keep the narration in strict order and let sequence, not commentary, build the
tension. Report what the operator did the way you would report weather.

## Licenses

form: understatement as emphasis — the flat, unqualified declarative on a fact
      the reader expects to see played up
move: the exemplars render an alarming specific plainly and let it land, adding
      no intensifier and no editorial verb; the restraint is the emphasis
bar:  the sentence states a fact that tempts inflation and carries no evaluative
      adjective or adverb and no "seemed"/"appeared" softener; any force in it
      comes from the fact itself, not the framing

form: the unreconciled pair — two adjacent plain sentences, one stating an
      accusation-grade fact, one stating the countervailing finding, left open
move: the exemplar states both poles of a dispute and refuses to rank them or
      collapse them, so the reader holds the tension the evidence actually left
bar:  both statements are documented and load-bearing and neither is a strawman;
      the pair does not use the "not X but Y" reconciling mold and does not tell
      the reader which one wins. It withholds a resolution the evidence withholds,
      rather than staging a false one

form: the diagnostic question — a direct question naming the evidence that would
      settle the dispute, answered at once by why that evidence is out of reach
move: the exemplars show their reasoning in the open, posing what you would need
      to know and then showing you cannot get it, without rhetorical flourish
bar:  the question names one concrete piece of evidence, not a vague "who can
      really say"; the answer gives a concrete reason it is unavailable to the
      person who would need it. At most one or two in the piece

## Patrick McKenzie, "How credit cards make money"
Source: https://www.bitsaboutmoney.com/archive/how-credit-cards-make-money/
Craft:
- cadence: alternates staccato openers ("Swipe it. Tap it. Dip it.") with longer
  systematic sentences, so the plain rhythm earns the room to unfold a mechanism.
- argument: builds mechanism before consequence in each section; establishes the
  one counterintuitive foundation first, then lets the rest hang off it.
- evidence: numbers arrive as punctuation, not wallpaper; a single figure lands a
  section instead of a table of them.
- stance: translator, never evangelist; the system is baroque and worth
  understanding, and he is showing you the wiring, not selling a verdict on it.
- notice: names the paradox others skip (the product "makes money by losing money
  on the card itself"), the load-bearing fact a casual account would miss.
- diction: precise where precision matters ("regional equilibria") and plain
  everywhere else; technical terms are defined in the clause that introduces them.
- reader: assumes intelligence without knowledge, builds complicity ("you have
  probably used one, mostly oblivious to..."), never condescends.
- the move the axes missed: he makes an opaque mechanism feel inevitable rather
  than arbitrary, so the reader ends up understanding why it works the way it does,
  not just that it does.

## Dan Luu, "Reading Postmortems"
Source: https://danluu.com/postmortem-lessons/
Craft:
- cadence: short flat anchors ("Proper error handling code is hard.") set before
  a longer consequence; the flatness is deliberate and does the intensifying.
- argument: moves from specific incidents to a general pattern without ever
  announcing the pattern as a grand claim; lets the cases accumulate it.
- evidence: cites actual findings with their real numbers ("92% of those failures
  are from errors handled incorrectly") and lets the figure speak for itself.
- stance: understated, faintly exasperated; refuses drama on genuinely bad
  outages, which is exactly what makes them read as serious.
- notice: catches that "obvious" and "done" are different things, and that the
  public record is skewed by what operators choose to write up.
- diction: unadorned to the point of bluntness ("bad failures," "really bad
  outages"); no hyperbole anywhere, so the plain words keep their weight.
- reader: treats the reader as a peer who can handle uncertainty; hedges openly
  ("I haven't done any formal analysis... yet") without losing the thread.
- the move the axes missed: he narrates failure without ever assigning melodrama
  to it, so a reader trusts the account precisely because it is not trying to
  alarm them.

## Hannah Ritchie, "Do more Europeans die of summer heat than Americans die of guns?"
Source: https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/heat-guns-america-europe
Craft:
- cadence: dense corrective passages broken by short declaratives ("Not really.")
  that mark each turn of her own reasoning rather than perform confidence.
- argument: corrects a viral claim by naming specific methodological gaps and
  fixing them in view, not by declaring the original simply wrong.
- evidence: distinguishes measurement methods with calm specificity ("death
  certificate figures" versus deaths "modelled as excess"), and footnotes the
  reasoning so the reader can see the scaffolding.
- stance: fair to the impulse behind the claim while disciplining the number
  ("I sympathise with the overall sentiment") and scoring no points on either side.
- notice: refuses the false choice the audience wants ("the two are not mutually
  exclusive"), holding two true things at once.
- diction: measured and unloaded; keeps sympathy and rigor in the same sentence
  ("still not perfect, but hopefully a closer comparison").
- reader: shows her method transparently so the reader can check her rather than
  trust her, which is how she earns the trust anyway.
- the move the axes missed: she makes correcting an overstatement feel like an
  act of fairness to the underlying concern, not a rebuttal of it, so the reader
  ends more sympathetic to the real problem and less sure of the viral version.

## Self-test

Following this guide, the piece will carry a documented accusation and a "no
violation found" at equal weight in adjacent unreconciled sentences, render its
most inflammatory facts with the adjectives stripped off, and stop at the proof
question to name the exact missing evidence and why it cannot be had. The house
default gives plainness and bans hype; it does not, on its own, tell a writer to
deliver the nuanced finding as the payoff rather than the anticlimax, to hold two
true verdicts open instead of choosing, or to make deadpan the tool that keeps a
viral outrage honest. That is what this article should sound like: a fair
investigator whose restraint is the argument.
