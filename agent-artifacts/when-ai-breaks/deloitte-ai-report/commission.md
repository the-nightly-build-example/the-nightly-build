# Commission: when-ai-breaks/deloitte-ai-report

## The incident

In 2025 Deloitte delivered a paid assurance review to the Australian Department of
Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR) that contained fabricated citations
produced with generative AI: references to papers that do not exist, misattributed
work by real academics, and a made-up quotation ascribed to a Federal Court
judgment. After the errors were identified, Deloitte issued a corrected report,
disclosed that a generative AI tool had been used, and agreed to refund part of
the fee. One lesson, one incident, told from the record.

## Why this closes a gap tonight

When AI Breaks has covered hallucinated citations by an individual (mata-v-avianca)
and by a model built for science (galactica), and AI-written journalism
(cnet-ai-articles). It has not covered the case the reader is most likely to meet
at work: a Big Four consultancy shipping AI-fabricated references inside a paid,
official deliverable, and what accountability followed. It teaches how AI failure
enters professional services and government contracting.

## What the lesson must do (follow the series prompt exactly)

1. Tell it in order, with names and dates: what the report was for (an independent
   assurance review of DEWR's Targeted Compliance Framework), what it actually
   contained (the specific fabricated references and the misattributed
   quotation/judgment), who it affected (the department and taxpayers who paid,
   the academics whose names were attached to work they never wrote, public trust
   in the review), and what the operator did afterward (who found the errors, the
   corrected report, the disclosure of AI use, the refund and its exact amount and
   scope).
2. Explain why this kind of system fails this way: a language model generates
   citations that are statistically plausible strings, not retrieved records, so
   fabricated references look exactly like real ones to a reader who does not
   check each one. Teach the missing piece on the spot (why plausibility is not
   retrieval), linking rather than re-teaching where the course already covers it.
3. Close with where the same weakness lives today, in systems the reader uses or
   commissions: AI drafting inside consulting, legal, and government work without a
   verification step. Name the recurring failure, not a generic moral.

## Sources (floor: min 8; primary >=4; secondary >=1)

Primary must include: the corrected Deloitte report itself and its disclosure of
the generative-AI tool used (read the methodology/appendix that names the tool);
the DEWR and/or Australian Senate statements on the review and the refund; the
real Federal Court judgment that the fabricated quotation misattributed (to verify
what was actually said); and the account from the academic(s) who identified the
fabrications. Secondary: the reporting that broke and held up the story (e.g.
Australian Financial Review, The Guardian, ABC). Verify the fee, the refund
amount and scope, the dates, and every name and title against the owning primary.
Accusations of fabrication are established by the corrected report and the
documents themselves, not by a single outlet's paraphrase.
