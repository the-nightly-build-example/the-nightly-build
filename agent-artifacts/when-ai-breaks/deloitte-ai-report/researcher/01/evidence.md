# Evidence: when-ai-breaks/deloitte-ai-report (01)

The record supports the incident as commissioned, from primary documents. The
contract fee (A$439,142.00), the review period (December 2024 to June 2025), the
two report dates (4 July 2025 and the 26 September 2025 correction), the removed
fabrications, and the disclosed AI tool (Azure OpenAI GPT-4o) are all read off
documents owned by the parties: the AusTender contract notice, the department's
own resource page, and both the original and corrected report PDFs. The
fabrications are proven by reading the two report versions against each other and
against the real Amato record, not by any single outlet's summary. The refund is
where the record is thinnest: the amount (A$97,587.11, the contract's final
instalment) was stated by officials at a Senate estimates hearing in early
October 2025, and I confirmed it through two independent stakeholder/press
accounts but did not read the Hansard transcript itself. The real Federal Court
Amato record was read through Victoria Legal Aid, the firm that ran the case, and
cross-checked against the corrected report's own quotation, because AustLII
refused the automated request; the exact paragraph numbering of the consent-order
notes could not be independently opened.

Note on addresses: the live DEWR report page now serves a *later* version (updated
3 February 2026). The 26 September 2025 corrected report and the 4 July 2025
original are no longer the live files, so the citable stable copies are the
Internet Archive snapshots of those specific PDF versions, recorded below beside
the canonical DEWR page. The DEWR and AusTender live pages returned 503/empty and
403 to this session's egress; a normal browser reaches them, and I read them via
the Archive.

## Contradictions

- **Scale of the errors.** The department's resource page calls it "a small number
  of corrections to references and footnotes"; the DEWR spokesperson said "some
  footnotes and references were incorrect." Rudge, who found them, put it at up to
  20 errors, including roughly ten references to one non-existent book. Both can
  be literally true (a "small number" of *distinct* fabricated works, cited many
  times), but the framings pull in opposite directions and the writer should not
  adopt either count as neutral fact.
- **Whether the correction was substantive.** Deloitte's Report Update note says
  the changes "in no way impact or affect the substantive content, findings and
  recommendations," and DEWR says the substance was retained with no changes to
  recommendations. Critics, including Rudge, treat fabricated authorities inside a
  legal-assurance report as going to its reliability regardless of whether the
  recommendations moved. This is a dispute over significance, not over what the
  final recommendations were.
- **Adequacy of the refund.** The government and Deloitte treat the final-instalment
  repayment (A$97,587.11) as resolution; the Greens (Pocock) call it inadequate and
  demand the full A$439,142. No party disputes the figure itself.
- **Cause.** Some coverage reports Deloitte attributing the errors to human/process
  gaps rather than to the AI tool; the disclosure and the fabrication pattern point
  to unverified model output entering the citations. Deloitte has not published a
  detailed post-mortem I could read, so the mechanism is asserted by others, not
  admitted in the primary.
- **Currency in coverage.** US outlets variously render the contract as "$290,000"
  and the refund as "$63,000" (USD conversions), and some round the fee to
  A$440,000. The owning figures are A$439,142.00 (fee) and A$97,587.11 (refund).
