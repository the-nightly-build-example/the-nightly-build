## This round's focus
Tell the incident in order with named parties and exact dates (Apprente acquired
Sept 2019 -> McD Tech Labs -> sold to IBM per the 27 Oct 2021 joint statement ->
AOT tested at 100+ US restaurants -> shut off no later than 26 July 2024 per the
Mason Smoot memo). Then teach why voice order-taking is hard in a drive-thru, and
close on where the same weakness lives today in specific named systems.

Watch these evidence flags (the record details them):
- The SEC ~70%-human-intervention finding is PRESTO AUTOMATION, a competitor, not
  McDonald's/IBM. Do not merge the two human-in-the-loop shapes. Use Presto only
  as the sourced example of the industry's remote-human reality, clearly labeled.
- No measured McDonald's/IBM order accuracy was ever officially published. The
  ubiquitous "~85%" is reporting, not a company figure — attribute it that way
  and say the number was never released.
- The failure clips are real and mainstream-carried but user-posted with no
  stable original source: they show the behavior was filmed, not an audited
  failure rate. Say exactly that.
- The live tension for the disputed-cause treatment: McDonald's "successes to
  date / voice ordering is still our future" framing versus the error record and
  the absent accuracy number. Present both; say what evidence would settle it.

Link the-evidence/whisper for how speech recognition works rather than
re-teaching it from scratch.

## Recent habits to break (do not inherit these from recent pieces)
- Do not end the "Why this matters" opener by telling the reader "By the end you
  will know / be able to …". Let the takeaway resolve what the opener sets up.
- Do not open on a generic second-person everyday scene as a reflex.
- Check the dek against spec/headlines.md's banned molds: no two-clause "and"
  contrast, no comma-triad, no colon subtitle where the right half is atmosphere.
- Avoid a generic closing heading like "The screen most applicants now pass
  through" (a recent when-ai-breaks tic) — name where THIS weakness lives today
  in specific systems. Write headings in the story's own nouns.
- Vary heading construction; do not stack same-built declarative sentences.
- The subject invites a cheap laugh: anchor any wit in the story's own facts and
  let the explanation carry the weight (see the voice guide).
