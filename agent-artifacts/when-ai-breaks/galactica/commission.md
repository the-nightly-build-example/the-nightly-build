# Commission: when-ai-breaks/galactica

## Assignment

Teach the Galactica incident: the science-writing language model Meta released to
the public and withdrew within days after it produced confident fabrications.
One lesson on the lesson template for When AI Breaks. Tell it in order, name the
people and dates, explain why that kind of system fails that way, and close on
where the same weakness lives in systems the reader uses now.

## The incident and the angle

Tell the record in sequence and pin each fact to a primary source:

- What it was built to do. Meta AI trained Galactica on scientific text and
  presented it as a tool to write papers, reason over formulas, and answer
  science questions. Name the team, the release date, and the public demo.
- What it actually did. Within days, users showed it generating fluent, authoritative
  output that was wrong or invented: fabricated citations and papers, confident
  answers on questions with no sound answer. Establish the specific documented
  examples from primary or firsthand accounts, not retellings of retellings.
- Who was involved and what the operator did. Name who promoted it and who
  criticized it, and record exactly what Meta did and when it took the public
  demo down. Give the interval in days.
- Why this kind of system fails this way. The mechanism is that a language model
  produces the most likely continuation, not a checked fact, and a science-styled
  interface makes a fabrication read as a result. Teach only the missing piece and
  link the mechanics lesson that already owns the general phenomenon rather than
  re-teaching it.
- Where the weakness lives now. Close on today's research-assistant and answer
  models that present fluent citations, and link the published cases where the
  same failure reached a real setting.

## Boundary against the published course

Link and contrast; do not repeat:

- `microsoft-tay` — a bot corrupted by adversarial users in hours. Different
  cause: Galactica was not turned by users, it fabricated from its training.
- `mata-v-avianca` — a user filed ChatGPT's invented citations in court. The same
  weakness surfacing downstream; link it as where this failure lands on a person.
- `cnet-ai-articles`, `ai-overviews`, `nyc-mycity-chatbot` — other deployed
  fabrication and retrieval failures. Neighbors, not this story.
- `hallucination` (The Mechanics) — the mechanism. Link, do not re-teach.

No published lesson covers Galactica, so this is new ground.

## Template, sources, production

- Template: lesson. Word band 1200–2200. Bookends are citation-exempt; every
  body section carries its own citations.
- Source policy: at least 8 sources, at least 4 primary and at least 1
  secondary. The Galactica paper, the demo and model material, Meta's release
  statements, and the named promoters' and critics' own posts are primary;
  contemporaneous reporting is secondary. Every date and every documented failure
  example needs the primary or firsthand account that owns it; two retellings of
  one origin count as one.
- Production policy (balanced, model tier "capable", nothing `required`):
  researcher high, writing-coach low, writer medium, editor high. Roles run as
  isolated subagents on the runtime's default capable-tier model. No deviation
  recorded.

## Recent shapes to break

Recent When AI Breaks lessons open on "what X switched off in <month>" or "X
built Y to..." and close on a "the same task still routes to a person" note.
Deks pack a date and a figure. Do not inherit the opener mold or the closing-note
mold; vary section construction and let the closer land this incident's own
present-day weakness. Headings are concrete, in this lesson's own nouns. The
bookend bands and the Sources heading are the only mandatory fixtures.
