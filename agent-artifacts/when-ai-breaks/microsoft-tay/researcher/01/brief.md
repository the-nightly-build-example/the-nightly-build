## Source policy
when-ai-breaks lesson: **min 8 sources; primary ≥ 4, secondary ≥ 1.**

tweets are primaries; Microsoft's account is interest-laden — classify primary

## Required primary documents (read first-hand)
1. **Peter Lee, "Learning from Tay's introduction," Official Microsoft Blog, 25
   March 2016.** Record: Lee's exact title/role at the time (verify: Corporate
   Vice President, Microsoft (Healthcare/Research) — confirm the precise wording
   the post/Microsoft uses); Microsoft's stated goal for Tay; the "coordinated
   attack" / "exploited a vulnerability" framing verbatim; the XiaoIce contrast
   Microsoft itself draws; any timeline Microsoft states.
2. **Archived Tay content** via the Wayback Machine (the @TayandYou account /
   launch site) and/or reporting that reproduced tweets with timestamps.
   Establish: the launch date/time (23 March 2016), representative offensive
   outputs (record precise text, minimally, as evidence), and evidence
   distinguishing "repeat after me" parroting from generated content.
3. **Microsoft's Tay launch/announcement material** (site or press), if
   archived, for the stated design ("the more you chat, the smarter it gets" or
   equivalent) — verify the exact promise.
4. A fourth primary of record if available (e.g. Microsoft's statement on the
   30 March accidental relaunch, or Tay's own relaunch tweets archived).

## Also gather (secondary, for corroboration and mechanism)
- Contemporaneous reporting: The Guardian, The Verge, BBC, TechRepublic — for
  the ~16-hour timeline, the relaunch, and reproduced tweets.
- IEEE Spectrum / an academic case study for the mechanism and retrospective
  analysis of why it failed.

## Research questions to answer with exact readings
- Exact timeline: launch date/time; how long until offline (~16 hours — verify
  and give the source's basis); apology date; relaunch date and what happened.
- Named actors with exact titles: Peter Lee (verify role/affiliation); any other
  named Microsoft figures.
- Mechanism evidence: what set Tay up to absorb user input (the "repeat after
  me" function and any actual learning-from-input); which offensive outputs were
  parroting vs generated. Find the strongest evidence on each side.
- The disputed cause: Microsoft's "coordinated attack exploiting a specific
  vulnerability" vs the "predictable failure of an unfiltered learning system on
  an adversarial platform." Capture the strongest version of each and note what
  evidence would settle it.
- The 2016-vs-today distinction: a first-hand or authoritative basis for how
  Tay's online learning differed from modern fixed-weights chat models (so the
  writer can state it accurately). This may lean on a taught lesson; flag it.

## Contradictions to hunt
- "A few bad actors exploited a vulnerability" (Microsoft) vs "the design made
  this inevitable" (critics). Document both with sources.
- Any disagreement over how much Tay genuinely "learned" vs merely repeated.
