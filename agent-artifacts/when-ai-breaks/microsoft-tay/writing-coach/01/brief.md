## Domain and genre
Post-mortem narrative of a public technology failure that then teaches the
mechanism, for a smart lay reader. Genre: the incident piece that tells what
happened in clean chronological order with named actors and dates, then turns to
why the system failed and where the weakness persists. Study writers the field
rates for technology and institutional post-mortems that are precise, fair to
the operator, and analytically sharp (the tradition of rigorous incident and
systems-failure writing). Pick your own ≥3 exemplars; prefer primary pieces;
skip influencer/SEO content.

## What the voice guide must solve for this article
- Narrating a fast, ugly incident without sensationalizing it; quoting offensive
  material only as evidence, precisely and sparingly.
- Being fair to Microsoft's account while testing it against the record; keeping
  reported fact, the operator's framing, and the writer's synthesis distinct.
- Moving cleanly from story to mechanism to present-day relevance without a
  signposted "here's why it matters" seam.
- Handling the 2016-vs-today distinction (online learning vs fixed weights) so
  the reader generalizes correctly.

## Recently used, do not reuse
- Opener "The number X published about itself"; comma-triad headings/deks; the
  google-flu-trends heading mold "Two accounts, one gap in the timeline."
- Any reusable closer formula or house catchphrase.

Transferable craft only; never a named writer's persona or lines.
