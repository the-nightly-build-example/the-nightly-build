# Editorial review: when-ai-breaks/character-ai-lawsuit (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: a federal judge let Garcia's wrongful-death suit against Character.AI go
past a motion to dismiss and held the app can be treated as a product, and the
lesson uses that posture to teach the alleged-versus-confirmed line, why an
engagement-optimized companion fails a user in crisis, and where the same design
still ships. The piece stands on four claims: the pattern of use and death as
the complaint alleges them; what the May 2025 order did and did not do; the
January 2026 settlement with no finding of causation; and the design failure
plus its industry-wide spread.

I pushed hardest on the ruling, which is where this round's exactness lives, and
it held. The body sizes the order correctly: it teaches what a motion to dismiss
is, states that the court took the allegations as true and settled nothing about
their truth, lists the surviving claims and the one dismissed (IIED), keeps
Google in only as an adequately alleged component-part maker and aider-abettor
with "None of that was proven," quotes the product holding verbatim against the
evidence record, and states plainly that the court "did not rule that a chatbot's
output is unprotected speech" but "declined to decide the question." The
nb-note quotation ("the Court is not prepared to hold that Character A.I.'s
output is speech," p. 31) matches the record and its locator. The closer holds
the ceiling: "a judge let the claims proceed, and the parties then settled. No
court found that the product caused Sewell Setzer's death."

The one break was in the takeaway, the highest-visibility line in the piece. It
read that the judge "decided only that the case could go forward and that the
chatbot could be treated as a product rather than protected speech." "Rather
than protected speech" reads as a decision that the output is not protected
speech, which is exactly the overstatement the body is careful to avoid and the
round is built to prevent. The court declined that question. Fixed directly by
cutting "rather than protected speech"; the body already carries the accurate,
fuller account, so nothing was lost.

Display text checks out after that fix. The headline states only confirmed court
actions and does not reach for liability or causation. The dek attributes the
design and guardrail claims to "his mother's suit says" and states only the
death and its date as fact; it is identical to the nb-meta dek. Every section
subhead is either explicitly alleged ("What the complaint says about..."),
confirmed ("The changes Character.AI announced..."), exact about the ruling, or a
general class claim ("Why an immersive companion fails a user in crisis") rather
than a finding about this death.

Fixed corrections confirmed landed: "Daniel De Freitas" throughout (no caption
"Frietas"); death date February 28, 2024 (not the order's "2025" typo). The FTC
attribution is exact: orders issued September 11, 2025 under Section 6(b) to
seven named companies, the model order's definitions and thresholds quoted from
the instrument (source 10), minor = under 18.

Figures, names, and dates reconcile to the evidence record: the November 2024
scale figures and $9.99 tier, the ~$2.7 billion August 2024 Google deal, the
"12 and older" rating before summer 2024, and each dated company announcement in
the timeline. All ten source `data-nb-kind` labels are correct: eight primaries
(complaint, order, four company posts, FTC release, FTC order) and two
secondaries (Reuters wire for Google's statement and for the settlement),
meeting the lesson floor. No wrong label hides a missing independent source; the
company posts are framed as the operator's own announcements, not verification.
The nb check ran with link-checking on and reported no unresolved source; every
printed href matches the URL the researcher recorded reading firsthand.

Care rules hold. No method or means of self-harm appears; the self-harm pop-up is
named only as pointing to a suicide-prevention hotline; no final messages are
quoted; the register stays sober with no grand words. The complaint's own
confounders (the therapist's attribution to social media; the prior diagnoses)
are reported, and causation is never presented as adjudicated.

## Cut

Ten sentences failed the tests and were removed or repaired directly. The
repeated pattern was signposting: paragraph-opening sentences that announced the
next fact without carrying one themselves. Cut "The complaint's central claim is
about how that product was designed," "The scale is part of the claim," and "The
complaint also puts a value on Google's later involvement," each of which reduced
to an interchangeable frame and left its paragraph leading on the concrete fact
instead, closer to the dated plain-sentence model the voice guide sets. Cut the
self-grading opener "What the record supports about the product at that time is
narrow and specific"; the age rating and the absent guardrail carry the point,
and the paragraph's own closer states the discipline.

One self-reference: "What follows is drawn from the company's own posts" used the
exact phrasing the slop standard rules out. Rewrote to attribute the source
without narrating the article, keeping the load-bearing caveat that these are the
company's announcements and not an independent account of what shipped.

In the closer to the mechanism section, "which is another way of saying" was a
restatement connective dressed as reasoning; split into two sentences so the
regulator's inquiry and the still-shipping design each stand on their own. In the
takeaway, cut "What is settled is the design," a pun on the settled case that
carried nothing the following sentence did not already deliver and risked a note
of cleverness against a sober subject.

Fixed one overstatement in the opening bookend: "seven of the largest technology
companies" labeled all seven FTC recipients as the largest, which is not true of
the defendant startup and others. Changed to "seven companies, several of them
among the largest in technology," which keeps the specific figure and the scale
point without the false label.

Checked the edges, the dangling-referent rule, prompt leakage, and borrowed
phrasing. The body finds its own entry (a definition of the product, not the
chatbot's output). The stated learning objective in the opener is required by the
lesson template, not lifted instruction. The did/did-not-do heading follows the
voice guide's Howe technique rather than any Howe phrasing. No banned-term or
punctuation counts run over (zero em-dashes). Against the recent-pattern notes:
no terse second-sentence dek rebuttal, no reversal tail, no comma-triad-and dek,
no "What X saw in Y" heading, no penultimate "what would settle it" section.

Furniture earns its place. The nb-note isolates the single most-overstated line
of the case in the court's own words; the nb-timeline carries the operator's
dated response sequence. Both are documented components used for deliberate
emphasis, not stacked blocks, and the press's no-Verdict-block rule is respected
(the takeaway lands the judgment). No component was added; a third would have
over-furnished a piece the prose already teaches cleanly.

On the flagged W-SENTENCE-DENSITY warning: the sentence lists exactly which
claims survived the motion to dismiss. Splitting it would force an arbitrary
grouping and blur that the enumeration is one complete set of survivors, which
the round's exactness wants stated together. Kept whole, as the writer intended.
The warning is an accepted intentional one.

## Reader

Read straight through as the paper's declared reader, the piece hands over what
the raw documents would not: the complaint's allegations held apart from the
court's narrow holding line by line, the product-versus-speech distinction sized
exactly, the reason an engagement-optimized companion fails at the seam between
staying in character and stepping out of it where a scripted helpline fails
differently, and the placement of that same design in seven products a federal
regulator is now studying. A reader finishes able to read the next "AI chatbot
ruled/caused" headline correctly. That answer and the draft-handoff's
original-work sentence agree, and both survive: the piece is a teaching line, not
a restatement of its sources. After the cuts the prose sits closer to the
Fink/Howe/Langewiesche exemplars than to a median summary, stating dated facts in
order and grading each claim for how sure it is. The headline, read last as the
largest claim, is the accurate ceiling the record supports.
