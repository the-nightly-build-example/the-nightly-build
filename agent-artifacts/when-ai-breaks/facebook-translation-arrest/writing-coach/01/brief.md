The article: a lesson that reconstructs one real incident — a wrongful arrest
caused by a machine-translation error and a human who trusted it — in order,
with named people and dates, then explains why the system failed that way.
The subject is politically charged; the writing must stay precise, neutral,
and free of editorializing about the conflict. Genre: incident reconstruction
plus mechanism, for a serious general reader. Length band 1200-2200 words.

This round's focus: study writers who narrate a single incident with restraint
and exactness, keeping the human stakes visible without melodrama, and who can
turn to explaining a technical cause without changing register. Exemplars need
not be about AI or translation; if one is, quote only where the passage shows
the writing, not the incident.
