# writing-coach brief: when-ai-breaks/chatgpt-defamation (01)

Assignment: a lesson for the When AI Breaks desk on chatbots stating false,
defamatory facts about named real people, centered on Mark Walters v. OpenAI with
the Turley and Hood episodes as context, and the unsettled liability question.
The reader is smart and widely read but not a lawyer. The piece tells what
happened from the record, explains why a chatbot confabulates a confident false
biography, and lays out what a court decided. Register: plain, sober, precise with
fact versus allegation versus ruling, in the spirit of press/editorial's "no hype
and no doom." This is a serious subject involving real named people; the writing
is careful, never lurid.

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (house standard, slop and headline rules, press voice,
  lesson identity, the When AI Breaks prompt)

Output: writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md

Study writers who explain a legal case and a technical failure to a general
serious reader and are trusted for restraint and accuracy: people who keep fact,
allegation, and holding distinct and never sensationalize a named person's harm.
Pull passages that show how to narrate a legal dispute cleanly and how to state a
finding without overclaiming. Quote for voice, not substance; avoid passages that
hand the writer this case's facts or conclusions.
