Source floor (from nb source-policy --series when-ai-breaks): at least 8 sources,

Research questions to answer from primary documents:

1. Walters v. OpenAI. From the complaint and the court's ruling/order (Superior
   Court of Gwinnett County, Georgia): what ChatGPT output about Mark Walters (the
   fabricated embezzlement suit involving the Second Amendment Foundation), how it
   arose (journalist Fred Riehl's query about a real case), the legal theory, and
   the court's May 2025 decision and its exact reasoning (factual-statement
   question, fault, damages). Record what was decided and what was left open.
2. Jonathan Turley (2023). What ChatGPT asserted (a harassment accusation and a
   fabricated Washington Post article), sourced to Turley's own account and
   contemporaneous reporting. Keep it as the pattern, not the center.
3. Brian Hood (2023). The Australian mayor whom ChatGPT described as a perpetrator
   in a bribery matter he had actually exposed; the threatened defamation action.
   Sourced to his lawyers' statements and reporting. Whether a suit was filed.
4. The liability doctrine and the present. Primary or authoritative secondary on
   the defamation elements at issue for AI output (false statement of fact,
   publication, fault/actual malice, whether outputs are "published" statements),
   and the honest state of the law after Walters. Where confident false
   biographies still appear today.

Verify every name, title, role, date, and dollar/however-many-case figure against
the owning primary. Accusations about a named person need the primary record, not
a retelling; two retellings of one origin count as one. Classify OpenAI statements
as primary-to-OpenAI (interested) and label them so.

Hunt for contradictions: OpenAI's defenses, any disclaimer argument, commentators
who think these outputs are not legally "defamatory," and differences between the
three cases. Record in full.
