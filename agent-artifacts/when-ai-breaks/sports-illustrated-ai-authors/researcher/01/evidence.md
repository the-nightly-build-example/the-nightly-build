# Evidence: when-ai-breaks/sports-illustrated-ai-authors (round 01)

The record firmly establishes the identity-fabrication half of the commission's
angle and only partly establishes the causal claims built on top of it. Futurism's
November 27, 2023 report, corroborated by the archived Sports Illustrated and
TheStreet pages themselves, nails down exactly what was invented: at least five
named "authors" (Drew Ortiz, Sora Tanaka, Domino Abrams, Nicole Merrifield, Denise
McNamara) who do not exist outside these pages, each carrying a headshot traced to
the AI-face marketplace generated.photos and a fabricated one-paragraph biography.
Arena Group and AdVon Commerce, in their own November 28, 2023 statement, admit to
using "pen or pseudo name[s]" — an admission from the accused parties themselves
that corroborates Futurism's forensic finding, though the two accounts disagree
sharply on what that admission means (a privacy pseudonym for a real writer, in
Arena Group's telling, versus a wholly invented person with an AI face, as the
archived pages show). The second contested claim — that the article text itself,
not just the byline, was AI-generated — is far thinner. It rests on unnamed
sources Futurism describes as insiders, a single origin that NPR's follow-up only
retells rather than independently confirms, and it is denied by both Arena Group
and AdVon. The 2024 AdVon investigation supplies real evidence that AdVon used AI
text generation extensively and was not candid about it, but that evidence is
about AdVon's operation broadly (other publishers, other years); Futurism does not
produce an AdVon-side document — an edit log, a CMS screenshot — specific to a
Sports Illustrated article. The causal link from the scandal to Ross Levinsohn's
December 11, 2023 removal is reporting's inference, not a fact any primary source
states: Arena Group's own press release cites only "operational efficiency and
revenue," and Levinsohn's own farewell post does not mention AI. That gap is worth
keeping sharp in the piece rather than smoothing over.

## Contradictions

- **What the pen names were, and why.** Arena Group and AdVon both describe the
  fake bylines as writers using "a pen or pseudo name... to protect author
  privacy." The archived pages themselves show something categorically different
  from a real writer choosing an alias: invented names attached to invented
  biographies and photographs traced to a stock AI-face marketplace, with no real
  person behind them at all. Both parties made this characterization voluntarily,
  in their own statement: it is the accused parties' own softened framing of a
  practice the physical evidence shows went well beyond pseudonymity. Record
  both: the
  self-description (pen names, for privacy) and what the archived pages actually
  contain (fabricated people).

- **Who wrote the text.** Futurism, citing unnamed sources, says some article
  text was AI-generated. Arena Group's Nov 28, 2023 statement says AdVon assured
  it the articles were "written and edited by humans... using both
  counter-plagiarism and counter-AI software." AdVon's 2024 statement to
  Futurism narrows this further, specifically for Sports Illustrated: "authored
  and edited by human writers... MEL AI tool was not used in content processes
  until 2023." No document specific to an SI article (an edit log, a CMS
  "source: AI" tag, comparable to what Futurism produced for AdVon's other
  clients) has surfaced to settle this. What would settle it: an AdVon or Arena
  Group-side content-management record for a specific SI-published article
  showing AI-tool usage, or a named former AdVon employee with direct,
  attributable knowledge of how SI's specific articles were produced (Futurism's
  sourcing here is anonymous).

- **Why Levinsohn was removed.** Arena Group's own press release names generic
  "operational efficiency and revenue" reasons. Nearly every outlet that reported
  the firing (CBS News, NBC News, Fortune, TheWrap, and others found in this
  research pass) frames it as a consequence of the AI scandal, but this is
  editorial inference layered onto a press release that does not say so, made a
  month after the last of the fabricated content was pulled. Levinsohn's own
  LinkedIn farewell post, quoted secondhand, does not mention AI or the
  controversy either. Treat the causal claim as reporting's interpretation, not
  an established fact, unless a primary source is found stating it directly.

## Limitation for the orchestrator

The identity-fabrication claim (fake author, fake headshot, fake bio) is solid:
directly read from the archived artifacts themselves, not just secondary
description, and admitted in substance (if not in framing) by both accused
parties. The text-authorship claim (was the article prose itself AI-written) is
the piece's genuine open question, not a settled fact dressed up as one — hold
that line exactly where the commission asked, and do not let "AI wrote the
bylines" bleed into "AI wrote the articles" without the qualifier. The causal
claim that the AI scandal cost Levinsohn his job is media inference, not a fact
any primary source states.
