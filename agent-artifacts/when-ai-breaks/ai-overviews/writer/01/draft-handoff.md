## Original work

The piece sets Google's own "misinterpretation / hallucination is inherent"
account (Reid; Pichai) against the word-for-word fidelity of the glue answer to
u/fucksmith's comment, and shows that because the model's comprehension was
perfect, the failure has to be an absent model of source trust rather than a
reading error — a weighing the postmortem and the reporting each supply pieces
of but none of them performs.

That work is visible in the "How a real joke became an answer" section: the
Google position card states the counter-account at full strength, and the two
paragraphs after it grant the rocks/data-void case to Google, then use the glue
case to defeat the misinterpretation reading.

## Notes for the editor

- The two confirmed examples carry two distinct jobs, per the round's focus:
  glue is the clean "no trust model" case (a real Reddit joke restated
  accurately); rocks is the data-void case (satire surfaced via a republished
  copy because it was nearly the only match, not a direct Onion retrieval). The
  discarded faked screenshots appear only inside Reid's "never appeared" line.
  No Reddit URL is cited; the glue joke's existence is attributed to the
  secondary reporting and Google's acknowledgment. The Verge/Pichai page is
  cited at its own address.

- Deliberate reading, not an open hole: the dek's "had read its source
  correctly" is the contested thesis. It is decisive for glue (word-for-word
  reproduction) and holds for rocks at the level of restatement; the body
  engages Google's counter-account rather than asserting it unopposed.

- No source asset was captured. The evidence flags the Onion masthead-plus-
  headline pairing as the satire signal and warns against cropping to the
  headline alone, but the masthead sits in a separate page header from the
  article title, so no single-element web capture holds both together. The
  satire signal is carried in prose instead (The Onion named, fictional
  geologist, "hide loose rocks in peanut butter or ice cream").

- Open scale question: the commission's "billions of queries a day" is not in
  the evidence record's Numbers, so the scale contradiction is weighed
  qualitatively — Google's "less than 1 in 7 million" rate against the sourced
  "hundreds of millions" of launch-week users — rather than with an unsourced
  daily-query figure. If the desk wants that figure in, it needs a source.
