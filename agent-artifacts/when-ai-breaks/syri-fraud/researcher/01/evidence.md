# evidence: when-ai-breaks/syri-fraud (01)

The record supports the commissioned angle. The primary document is the full
English translation of the judgment (ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2020:1878), read in full with
paragraph locators. The court struck down the SyRI legislation on 5 February 2020
under Article 8(2) ECHR because it was "insufficiently transparent and verifiable"
[6.86, 6.95], and it did so without any finding of a proven wrongful flag: the
State never disclosed the risk model, its indicators, or any error rate, so the
court could not test SyRI's accuracy at all [6.49, 6.65, 6.89]. That evidentiary
hole is the story. The court found the risk-scoring reached only "problem
districts" and carried a risk of bias by "lower socio-economic status or an
immigration background" [6.93]. Two things are thin, and both are recorded in
Limits and Contradictions. First, it is not established that SyRI used machine
learning: the State described a comparison against a rule-based risk model with a
predetermined cut-off [6.47, 6.89], and the scholarly analysis says the exact
technology "is unknown." Second, the judgment's own list of SyRI projects [3.10]
does not include Eindhoven, which appears only in secondary reporting. SyRI is
kept factually separate from the childcare-benefits scandal throughout.

## Contradictions

- Was SyRI "AI"? The commission and desk frame this as an AI failure, but the
  record does not establish machine learning. The State told the court SyRI
  compares files "with the aid of a simple decision tree" [6.47] and described the
  model as risk indicators plus links plus a fixed cut-off [6.89]. The court
  accepted "as a factual assumption that ... no use is made at this point in time
  of 'deep learning' and data mining" [6.51, 6.63], while noting the legislation
  left room for them. Van Bekkum and Zuiderveen Borgesius state "it is unknown
  what exact technology SyRI used (e.g. machine learning, or artificial
  intelligence)." So SyRI is safest described as a secret rule-based
  risk-scoring system, not a proven machine-learning model.

- Where SyRI ran. The judgment's list of SyRI projects since 2015 [3.10] names
  five: "G.A.L.O.P. II," "Address fraud Afrikaander neighbourhood in Rotterdam,"
  "WGA Vulnerable neighbourhoods Capelle aan den IJssel," "WGA Rotterdam Bloemhof
  & Hillesluis," and "WGA Haarlem Schalkwijk." It does not name Eindhoven.
  Secondary reporting (Human Rights Watch, AlgorithmWatch) says SyRI reached
  Eindhoven, but no primary in this record owns an Eindhoven SyRI project. The
  commission's place list includes Eindhoven; cite the judgment's four named
  municipalities (Rotterdam, Capelle aan den IJssel, Haarlem) for confirmed
  deployments and treat Eindhoven as reported, not adjudicated.

- Did the court say SyRI was discriminatory in fact? No. It found a "risk" of
  bias-based links [6.93] and that SyRI had only been used in "problem districts,"
  but it did not find realised discrimination, and it declined to order disclosure
  of the models [6.115]. The angle (opacity, not a measured error, was decisive)
  holds; do not upgrade "risk of discrimination" to a finding of discrimination.

- Effectiveness. The State cited large fraud sums to justify SyRI [6.76], and the
  court accepted a pressing social need [6.76-6.77]. But van Bekkum and Zuiderveen
  Borgesius report SyRI "was never very successful in uncovering fraud" (Meuwese,
  2020). The court did not rule on effectiveness; both points can stand side by
  side.

- No credible defence that the court erred. The scholarly source calls the court's
  reasoning "solid" apart from a few phrasings, and the government did not appeal.
  The strongest counter is narrowing, not reversal: the same authors stress the
  ruling is confined to SyRI's facts and would not by itself doom a more
  transparent system. That narrows the lesson's reach; it does not undermine the
  angle.

## Limits

- The exact wording of Alston's amicus brief was not read: the OHCHR PDF is a
  scanned image with no text layer. Its author, date (September 2019), case number
  and thrust are confirmed via A/74/493 (fn 51) and the judgment [6.92], and its
  general claims via those documents, but a direct quotation from the brief itself
  is not in this record. If the writer needs a verbatim amicus quote, the file
  needs OCR.
- Whether SyRI used machine learning or AI in any real sense is not established
  (see Contradictions). Treat "AI" in the desk framing as the class of automated
  risk-scoring, and state plainly that SyRI's inner method was never disclosed.
- No SyRI false-positive or error rate exists in the record, by design: the model
  and its validation were never disclosed [6.49, 6.89, 6.95]. This is the point of
  the piece, but it means no accuracy figure can be cited for SyRI itself.
- The OHCHR press-release quotes were captured via search indexing, not a direct
  fetch (the page 403s behind Cloudflare). Verify against the live page before
  printing a verbatim quote.
- The SUWI Act text (Section 64/65) was read as reproduced verbatim in the
  judgment's Chapter 4, and the Decree (Chapter 5a) was read directly from
  Staatsblad 2014, 320. The consolidated standalone Act on wetten.overheid.nl was
  not separately opened; the amending act is Staatsblad 2013, 405 [judgment fn].
- An Eindhoven SyRI deployment is reported by secondary sources but not owned by
  any primary in this record (see Contradictions).
- Everything else the brief asked for is established: the mechanism, the parties,
  the timeline, the ground, the order, the aftermath, and the WGS successor.
