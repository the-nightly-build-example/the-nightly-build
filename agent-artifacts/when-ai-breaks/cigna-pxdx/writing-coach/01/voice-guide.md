# Voice guide: when-ai-breaks/cigna-pxdx

The press voice, applied to this one incident. Read the editorial direction and
the commission for the rules. This file only says how *this* lesson should
sound.

## The subject in one line

Cigna built a process called PxDx that flags a claim when the billed procedure
is not on a pre-set list of diagnoses that justify it, and its own doctors
signed the flagged claims into denial in batches without opening the files. The
lesson teaches why a required human review, kept in name and hollowed out in
fact, produces wrongful denials at scale.

## How to sound

- **Tell it in order.** What PxDx was built to do, what it did, who it hurt,
  what Cigna did after. Name the people (the plaintiffs, the doctors ProPublica
  counted, the committee chairs), the company, and the dates. The series lives
  on the record, so let the record carry the piece.
- **Measured, not heated.** This is a sensitive subject: real patients got real
  bills for care they had already received. State the figures and let them land
  on their own. No adjectives doing the work a number should do. No doom about
  insurance, no lecture about corporate greed. Report the 1.2 seconds and the
  reader draws the conclusion you were going to hand them.
- **Be exactly precise about the mechanism.** PxDx matches procedure codes
  against a list of accepted diagnosis codes. That is code-matching software, and
  Cigna is emphatic that it is not AI or an algorithm in the machine-learning
  sense. Do not write "AI" or "machine learning" as if the tool learned
  anything. The failure is not a mis-trained model. The failure is a human check
  that was reduced to a signature.
- **Steelman Cigna.** Cigna says PxDx reviews claims after treatment, denies
  payment and not care, covers about 50 low-cost services, and that the reporting
  was mischaracterized. Put that case at its strongest, in Cigna's own words,
  then say what the evidence settles and what it does not. A court has let the
  core claim proceed. Individual denials are still contested. Say so plainly.
- **Close on where it lives now.** Automated prior authorization and claims
  adjudication run this way across insurers. Point there, briefly, without
  inflating it.

## Watch for

- The michigan-midas lesson already taught the "no human examiner" failure.
  Link it, do not re-teach it. PxDx is the harder case: the human is present and
  signs, which is what makes throughput the tell.
- Slop collects at the section edges. The last sentence of the body is the one
  most likely to grade the argument instead of ending it. Cut it if it does.
- Headline is a plain actor-and-failure line with a real figure. No "Two X did
  the same Y", no colon subtitle, no comma-tail reversal in the dek.
