# Evidence record: when-ai-breaks/itutorgroup-age-discrimination (02)

This record supersedes 01 with one correction the editor caught, and preserves
everything in 01 that still holds. The correction: 01 said the EEOC's 2022 suit
press release "Uses no AI/ML/algorithm language." That is wrong. I reopened the
2022 release in full and it does carry AI/ML language — but only about the
agency's own separate initiative, never as a description of iTutorGroup's system.
The precise, corrected finding the writer must rely on: the EEOC never describes
iTutorGroup's *own software* as AI or machine learning. The complaint, the
consent decree, and the 2022 and 2023 releases all describe that software only as
software "programmed ... to automatically reject" applicants by a date-of-birth
age cutoff. The single occurrence of "artificial intelligence," "AI," and
"machine learning" in the whole record is boilerplate in the 2022 release naming
the EEOC's cross-agency "Artificial Intelligence and Algorithmic Fairness
Initiative" — the agency framing this enforcement action against the backdrop of
that initiative, not calling iTutorGroup's date filter an AI system. The lesson's
honest-mechanism point survives intact and is in fact sharpened: an explicit,
human-written programmed rule, framed by commentators and by the agency's own
initiative branding as "AI hiring." Everything else in 01 — the two age cutoffs,
the 200+, the $365,000, the dates, Judge Chen's signature, the two Mobley orders
read in full, and the birthdate-filter-vs-participating-tool line — held against
the primaries on my re-check and against the editor's independent read, and is
carried forward unchanged. One href is corrected below: source s8 now records the
document-level page for the July 12, 2024 Mobley order rather than the docket
index. The record remains thinnest on the reported May 16, 2025 Workday
collective certification; note that I did, this round, incidentally read that
order's own text on govinfo while hunting the July 2024 order URL — see
Contradictions.

## Contradictions

- CORRECTED, and now more precise than 01. The largest gap remains between the
  coverage and the record on what the technology was — but the correct statement
  of that gap is narrower than 01 claimed. Law firms and outlets widely call this
  the EEOC's "first AI / artificial-intelligence hiring settlement" (Greenberg
  Traurig, Sullivan & Cromwell, Duane Morris, National Law Review). Against that:
  the EEOC never describes iTutorGroup's *own software* as AI or machine learning.
  The complaint (zero AI/ML/algorithm hits), the consent decree (zero), the 2023
  settlement release (zero), and the 2022 suit release all describe iTutorGroup's
  system only as software "programmed ... to automatically reject" by a
  date-of-birth age cutoff, pleaded as intentional, willful disparate treatment.
  The ONE place AI/ML wording appears in the whole record is the 2022 suit
  release's boilerplate about the agency's separate "Artificial Intelligence and
  Algorithmic Fairness Initiative" (and Chair Burrows invoking that initiative as
  the reason the EEOC brought the case). That boilerplate is the agency framing
  its own enforcement posture; it is not a characterization of iTutorGroup's date
  filter. So: do NOT write that the words "AI"/"machine learning" never appear in
  the record — they do, once, in the agency's initiative boilerplate. DO write
  that the record never calls iTutorGroup's system AI or machine learning; it was
  an explicit human-written date-of-birth rule, and the "AI" framing is supplied by
  commentators and by the agency's initiative branding, not by any description of
  the system itself. Epstein Becker Green (s7) makes the same deliberate-programming
  point. This is where the lesson's honesty now lives, sharpened rather than
  weakened by the correction.
- An internal wording inconsistency worth handling with care: the complaint's "Nature
  of the Action" and the decree's recital say "over the age of 55" and "over the age
  of 60," while the operative allegation (complaint para. 19b) and both EEOC press
  releases say "age 55 or older" and "age 60 or older." Use "55 or older" / "60 or
  older": the charging party, rejected at "over the age of 55," was the boundary case,
  and the operative pleading and the agency's public statements both use "or older."
- The class and the "more than 200" figure are framed as "age 55 and over" for both
  sexes (complaint para. 19e), even though the pleaded male cutoff was 60. The 200+
  are the 55-and-older applicants rejected in March-April 2020; do not describe them
  as "over 60" or split them by the two thresholds.
- No contradiction on the numbers themselves: the two cutoffs, the 200+, the $365,000,
  and the dates are internally consistent across complaint, decree, and both releases.
- On the present landscape, one point from 01 is now partly resolved. 01 flagged the
  reported May 16, 2025 conditional certification of a nationwide ADEA collective of
  Workday applicants 40+ as reported-by-coverage only. While hunting the July 2024
  order URL this round, I incidentally read the primary on govinfo:
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-3_23-cv-00770/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-3_23-cv-00770-1.pdf
  is the "Order Granting Preliminary Collective Certification" (Re: Dkt. No. 106),
  Case 3:23-cv-00770-RFL, Document 128, Filed 05/16/25, by Judge Rita F. Lin. It
  GRANTS preliminary certification of a nationwide ADEA collective of applicants aged
  40 and over who applied via Workday from September 24, 2020, holding the proposed
  collective similarly situated because Mobley "substantially alleged the
  recommendation system to score, sort, rank, or screen applicants" with a common
  disparate-impact question. This corroborates the coverage the 01 record rested on
  secondhand. It is available to the writer as a primary if the landscape section
  wants it, though the commission's landscape can still rest on the two Mobley
  motion-to-dismiss orders already read in full.
