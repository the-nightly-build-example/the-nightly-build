## Skeptic

The routed break in 01 was the mechanism section's sweeping, false claim that
the words "artificial intelligence," "AI," "machine learning," and "algorithm"
appear nowhere in the record. This round had to confirm the recast heading and
sentence are now true and precise.

I opened the 2022 EEOC suit release myself
(eeoc.gov/newsroom/eeoc-sues-itutorgroup-age-discrimination) rather than trust
the record. It confirmed the corrected finding exactly: the release describes
iTutorGroup's own system only as software "programmed ... to automatically
reject" older applicants, and never as artificial intelligence or machine
learning. The sole AI/ML wording is the agency's own "Artificial Intelligence
and Algorithmic Fairness Initiative" branding — the standalone boilerplate
paragraph ("the use of software, including artificial intelligence (AI), machine
learning, and other emerging technologies ...") and Chair Burrows' quote naming
that same initiative. So the recast heading, "The record never calls the
software AI," is true display text, and the corrected premise holds: the "AI"
label is supplied by commentators (s6) and by the agency's initiative branding,
not by any description of the system. The honest-mechanism lesson stands on the
corrected premise, and no sweeping "the words never appear" claim survives
anywhere in the piece.

One precision fault in the recast sentence. As drafted, it said the AI/ML words
appear in exactly one spot — "a paragraph of boilerplate." But the initiative
name (carrying "Artificial Intelligence") appears in two places that both invoke
that same initiative: the boilerplate paragraph and Chair Burrows' quote, as
both the corrected record and my own read of the release confirm. "The one
place ... a paragraph of boilerplate" therefore undercounts and is imprecise
against the record. I fixed it directly, since I was removing an over-specific
false detail rather than adding a fact: the sentence now attributes the wording
to the 2022 suit announcement's invocation of the initiative, which is true
whether it sits in the quote, the boilerplate, or both, and keeps the load-
bearing characterization ("naming the backdrop for the case rather than
describing this system"). The claim the article makes is unchanged.

Source s8. The href now resolves to the Mobley Document-80 page
(courtlistener.com/docket/66831340/80/mobley-v-workday-inc/), the July 12, 2024
order granting in part and denying in part the motion to dismiss. It is cited
for the birthdate-filter-versus-participating-tool distinction the mechanism
section turns on, which that order owns at p.11 per the evidence record — the
right document for the claim. The display text ("Order on Motion to Dismiss
(July 12, 2024)") matches. The deliberately-docket "Go deeper" bookend link was
left as-is, correctly outside this brief.
